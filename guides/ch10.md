## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning (course wording, condensed) |
|---|---|
| **Underwriting** | Evaluating applications to decide **insurability** and the right **premium** for the risk. Also called **risk selection**. |
| **Adverse selection** | The tendency of **higher-risk (poorer) risks** to seek coverage, or the chance an insurer accepts bad risks (poor health, moral hazards). "Those most likely to have a loss are most likely to seek insurance." Underwriting exists to **protect the insurer against it**. |
| **Field underwriting** | The **first step**, done by the **producer/agent**: screening, gathering information, submitting applications. |
| **Application** | The underwriter's **principal tool** and **primary source**; reviewed **first**. Also the applicant's formal request for insurance. |
| **Attending Physician Statement (APS)** | Detailed report from the doctor who treated the applicant, requested when the medical section raises questions about a **specific condition**. Needs a copy of the **signed authorization**. |
| **Medical Information Bureau (MIB)** | Industry database, formed by **700+** member insurers, of **coded** medical information. Helps prevent **fraud and misrepresentation**. Does **not** make underwriting decisions. |
| **Morbidity** | Risk of **illness or disability** in a defined population. Morbidity tables show the **frequency, extent and duration** of disability expected in a group. |
| **Premium** | Periodic payment to keep a policy in force, **always paid in advance**. |
| **Risk classification** | Sorting applicants into **preferred, standard, substandard** (or declined) by likelihood of claims. |
| **Representations** | Answers true **to the best of the applicant's knowledge and belief**. Not warranted exact in every detail; must be accurate as far as **material** to the risk. |
| **Moral hazard** | Habits or lifestyle that signal extra risk: excessive drinking, drug use, poor credit, dishonest business practices. |
| **Avocations** | Another word for **hobbies**. |
| **Insurable interest** | Applicant would **suffer a loss** if the insured has medical expenses or can't work. A **prerequisite** for issuing a health policy. |
| **Earned premium** | Prorated part of paid-in-advance premium the insurer has "earned" by already providing coverage. |
| **Unearned premium** | Prorated part not yet earned. A **liability** on the insurer's balance sheet, **refunded if the policy is canceled**. |
| **Policy fee** | Small transaction fee charged in addition to premium, paid annually or once at issue. |
| **Loading (expense factor)** | The "expenses" part of the premium formula: commissions, wages, admin, overhead, required reserves, and **profit** (called **surplus** by mutual companies). |
| **Community rating** | Same rate structure for everyone in a community regardless of past or potential losses; used for **small groups and individuals**. |
| **Experience rating** | Group health premiums based on the **group's own claims experience**. |
| **Conditional receipt** | Coverage as of the application date (or medical exam date, whichever is later) **if** the applicant proves insurable. Also called a **temporary receipt**. Most common. |
| **Binding receipt** | Coverage **guaranteed until the insurer formally rejects** the application, even if the applicant is uninsurable. Also called **temporary insurance agreement** or **unconditional receipt**. |
| **Trial application** | Application submitted **without** the initial premium. |
| **Constructive delivery** | Legal delivery without physical delivery: the insurer gives up all control of the policy (e.g., mails it to the agent for **unconditional** delivery). |
| **Inspection receipt** | Signed when the applicant reviews the policy **before** paying. **Not** a premium receipt. |
| **Statement of continued good health** | Signed when premium is collected at delivery: health hasn't changed since the application or exam. |
| **Delivery (policy) receipt** | Signed by the insured on receiving the policy; proof of delivery that can **start the free-look period**. |
| **Outline of coverage** | Summarizes the policy's **benefits and limitations**; most states require it for each applicant. |
| **Buyer's Guide / Shopper's Guide** | Generic NAIC publication explaining insurance in general terms; doesn't address the specific policy. Required in some sales, e.g., **long-term care**. |
| **Policy summary** | Describes the **specific** product: agent, insurer, policy, riders, premiums, benefits, etc. |

## 3. The parties in underwriting

| Party | Who they are | Key duties / facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Producer (agent)** | The **field underwriter**. Performs the first step. | Decides which risks are desirable and submits them. Solicits, completes applications, collects premiums, submits to the home office. **Doesn't issue the policy**, but gives required disclosures (replacement notice, Buyer's Guide, outline of coverage, policy summary). |
| **Applicant** | Requests the insurance, completes and signs the application. | Often the proposed insured, but **not always**. Signing means false statements can cause **loss of coverage**. |
| **Proposed insured** | The person being insured (if approved). | Provides health information; signs if different from the applicant. |
| **Policyholder** | Holds all rights and options. | Usually also the **payor**. |
| **Payor** | Responsible for premiums being paid when due. | — |
| **Underwriter** | Reviews the application and other information and classifies the risk. | **Selects, classifies and rates** risks. Each insurer sets its **own** standards. Individually reviews all applicants for individual policies. Protects against **adverse selection**. |

### Agent responsibility (field underwriter): 5 tasks
1. Proper solicitation
2. Completing the application properly
3. Obtaining appropriate signatures
4. Collecting the initial premium
5. Issuing the correct premium receipt

- **Fiduciary duty** to the **insurer** when collecting premiums, and to the **public**.
- **Proper solicitation:** solicit **profitable** business while keeping the **highest professional standards**.
- An honest mistake is a tort (error or omission); insurers are advised to buy **E&O insurance** for producers.

## 4. The underwriting process

| Stage | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-selection** | Agent | Answer every question **in complete detail** (including physician info); give insight on possible rating; stress **honesty**. **An agent cannot legally guarantee or bind coverage.** |
| **Post-selection** | Underwriting department | Evaluate the risk using all sources; decide acceptability and class (**preferred, standard, substandard**; substandard may be uninsurable or written at higher premium). |
| **Outcomes** | Everyone | **Insurer** (business written), **insured** (gets coverage?), **agent** (issue → **commission**). Premium is set by the **likelihood of paying a claim**. |

## 5. The application

The number of sources checked depends mainly on **policy size** and the **risk profile** from the first review. **Larger policy → more thorough research.**

A **non-medical** application (no information beyond the application) is used most for **low coverage amounts** and **young** applicants. Most insurers set **non-medical limits**; above them a medical report may be required.

| Part | Content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Part I: General Applicant Information** | Name, age, address, birth date, sex, income, marital status, occupation. Type and amount of coverage, beneficiary, other insurance owned, pending applications, additional insureds. | Also hazardous hobbies, foreign travel, aviation, military service, and **tobacco use**. |
| **Part II: Medical and Health History** | Proposed insured's health and history, plus **family** history. | **Must be completed in full on every application.** May trigger an exam, blood test, urine specimen. **Insurer-requested exams are at the insurer's expense.** |
| **Part III: Agent's Report** | Agent's observations: **financial condition and character**, background and purpose of the sale, **how long the agent has known the applicant**, and **replacement**. | Really a **confidential communication between agent and insurer**. |

**Exam Tip:** the application is the applicant's **formal request for insurance**; the completed application **plus the initial premium** is the applicant's **consideration**.

### Replacement procedures (when Part III says "yes")
- Discuss underwriting that may bring a **new, higher premium** (new age, new conditions)
- Identify **pre-existing conditions** that may limit coverage
- Disclose possible new benefits, limitations and exclusions

### Completing the application
- **Incomplete application →** delays, applicant goes elsewhere, insurer **returns it to the agent**.
- **Misrepresentation is fraudulent** only when **material** and made with **fraudulent intent**. Even an **unintentional** material misrepresentation can void coverage in the **first 2 or 3 years** (contestable period).
- In-chapter quiz: *"Representations must be exactly correct in every detail or the policy can be voided"* → **False**.

### Signatures

| Scenario | Who must sign |
|---|---|
| Applicant is the insured and policyowner | **Applicant + Agent** |
| Third-party ownership (e.g., parent/child) | **Applicant + Proposed Insured + Agent** |
| Policy replacement | Applicant + Agent + Insured (if different) **+ replacement forms required by law** |

- No producer signature → **not underwritten**. **The beneficiary never signs.**
- The **authorization form** for investigative reports/medical information is signed by the **proposed insured**, with the **agent as witness**.
- The **insurer's name** and the **agent's name and license ID number** must appear on the application (printed, typed, stamped or handwritten, if legible).

### Changes to the application
- Agent may correct an error, but the **applicant initials** it.
- Insurer finds an uncorrected mistake → returns it to the agent → agent corrects → **applicant initials**.
- After issue: insurer may **rescind or cancel** only during the **contestable period**.
- Once attached to the policy, the application is part of the **legal contract**; no one but the applicant may change it **without the applicant's written permission**.

## 6. Risk classification

| Class | Description | Premium | Example factors |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Preferred** | Lower-than-average risk; better-than-average **morbidity** | **Lower** | Non-smoker, non-drinker, ideal BMI/healthy weight |
| **Standard** | Average risk; fits guidelines **without special restrictions or additional rating**; same as the **tabular risks** rates are based on. The **baseline**. | Standard | **Controlled BP**, no major risks |
| **Substandard (rated)** | Higher-than-average risk | **Higher ("rated-up")** or rejected; increase depends on degree of risk | Chronic illness, risky job, poor health, hazardous habits |
| **Uninsurable** | Risk too high | **No coverage offered** | **Terminal illness, fraud** |

"Rated policy" / "rated premium" = substandard contract with an above-average premium.

**Exam Tip:** know the exact definitions of standard, preferred and substandard. Many questions give health and lifestyle details and ask for the class.

### Risk classification case studies (same policy, three applicants)

| | **Sarah: Preferred** | **Michael: Standard** | **Robert: Substandard** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age / job | 32, software developer | 45, retail manager | 51, construction worker |
| BMI | 22 (ideal) | 27 (slightly overweight) | 33 (obese) |
| Tobacco | Non-smoker, exercises | Former smoker, quit **5 years** ago | Current smoker, 1 pack/day |
| Health | No chronic conditions; normal BP and cholesterol | **Controlled high BP** with medication | Type 2 diabetes (3 yrs); **irregular treatment compliance**; recent hospitalization |
| Family | No early-onset disease | Father: heart disease | — |
| Hobbies | None hazardous | None hazardous | Recreational **motorcycle racing** |
| **Decision** | About **15% below** standard rate | **Standard rate** | Rated up about **50%**; may have **exclusions** for racing |

Lesson: underwriters weigh the **combination** of factors, not any single one.

### Risk factors (3.4)
- Health insurance risk is broader than life: insureds file **multiple claims**; even healthy people see a doctor yearly. It isn't just acceptable vs. unacceptable; underwriters analyze the **degree** of risk.
- **Insurable interest** is a prerequisite.
- **Physical condition** (health and body build) is of **primary importance**; unusual build (extreme height/weight) may mean higher risk. **Health history** matters as much as current health; **family history** helps predict future needs.
- **Moral hazards:** excessive drinking, drug use, poor credit, dishonest business practices.
- **Occupation:** direct link to disabling injury. Low risk: professionals, office workers. High risk: heavy machinery, strong chemicals, high-voltage equipment. **Two jobs → benefits and premiums based on the more hazardous one.**
- **Hobbies (avocations):** skydiving, bungee jumping, etc. may lead the insurer to decline; insurer uses a **special questionnaire**.
- **Age and sex:** *(page body not in screenshots; see 4.8.2)*

## 7. Worked example: the Johnson case study

Underwriter **Elena Rodriguez** reviews **Robert Johnson, 52**, for comprehensive health coverage.

| Section | Key information | Underwriting impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Part I** | Age 52; construction supervisor; **former smoker (quit 3 years ago)**; weekend mountain biking | Age **+15%**; occupation moderate risk; former tobacco **no surcharge after 3 years**; biking **no impact** (recreational) |
| **Part II** | **Controlled hypertension (140/85)**; father's heart attack at 59; cholesterol slightly high; 5'11", 210 lb (**BMI 29.3**) | Hypertension **+10%**; family history noted, **no direct impact**; BMI **+5%** (overweight, not obese) |
| **Part III** | Known client **5 years**; steady job; no observed concerns; no replacement | Positive character; stable finances; no replacement concerns |

**Final decision:** *Standard risk with a 30% total premium adjustment* (15% + 10% + 5%).

Course questions, answered from the table:
1. *Biggest Part I factor?* **Age** (+15%).
2. *How did Part II affect it?* Hypertension (+10%) and BMI (+5%) added premium; family history noted only.
3. *Why does the agent's report matter?* Confirms character, finances and **no replacement**.
4. *If Robert lost 20 lb and improved his BP?* The course doesn't say; the BMI and hypertension loadings are the ones that could drop.

⚠️ See section 16: "standard with +30%" conflicts with the course's own definitions.

## 8. Premium calculations

### Basics
| Concept | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Premium mode** | **Frequency** of payment. Annual is the base; quarterly, semiannual or monthly can carry an **extra charge** (more admin cost, delayed investment funds). **More frequent = higher total cost.** Industrial policies: **weekly**. |
| **Earned premium** | Part already "earned" by providing coverage. |
| **Unearned premium** | Part not yet earned; a **liability**; **refunded on cancellation**. Example: **$120/year**, canceled after **6 months** → insurer earned **$60**, refunds **$60**. |
| **Policy term** | Period the policy stays in existence while premiums are paid. |
| **Policy fee** | Small transaction fee on top of premium; annual or one-time at issue. |

### The formula: **Morbidity – Interest + Expenses**
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Morbidity** | From **morbidity tables** (frequency, extent, duration of disability in a group). Actuaries use them to set rates. |
| **Interest** | Insurer invests premiums. **Higher assumed interest rate → lower premiums.** Actual earnings may be higher or lower than assumed. |
| **Expenses (load / loading charge / loading factor)** | Commissions, wages, admin, overhead (rent), **required reserves**, plus **profit** (mutual companies call it **surplus**). |

### Worked example: Maria (45-year-old office manager, disability income)

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Morbidity | $42 per $100 of monthly benefit × 30 (for **$3,000**/month) | **$1,260.00** |
| 2. Interest | 3% projected return: $1,260 × 0.97 | **$1,222.20** |
| 3. Expense loading | 35% (admin, commissions, profit): × 1.35 | **$1,649.97** |
| 4a. 90-day elimination period | 15% discount: × 0.85 | **$1,402.47** |
| 4b. 5-year benefit period (vs. to age 65) | × 0.80 | **$1,121.98** |
| 4c. Non-smoker | × 0.90 | **$1,009.78** |
| **Final annual premium** | | **$1,009.78** |
| Quarterly instead | × 0.275 per quarter | **$277.69/quarter = $1,110.76/year** |

Note the order: interest is **subtracted** (×0.97), expenses **added** (×1.35), matching *morbidity – interest + expenses*. Paying quarterly costs **$100.98 more** per year.

### Secondary factors
| Factor | Effect on premium |
|---|---|
| **Benefits** | More/higher benefits = **higher** premium |
| **Benefit period** | Longer = higher. Common: **12, 24, 30 months; 5, 10, 20 years; to 65; life** |
| **Elimination (waiting) period** | Works like a **deductible**; must be met **each** time the insured is totally disabled. **Longer = lower** premium |
| **Probationary period** | **One-time**; accidents covered immediately, sickness after a **one-time 30-day** period; lets insurer deny small pre-existing claims |

### Elements that influence morbidity
| Element | Course point |
|---|---|
| **Claims experience** | Claims tables based on past experience; **group** health uses the group's **experience rating** |
| **Age and sex** | Costs rise with age. Age aside, **women pay more for HEALTH**, **men pay more for LIFE** |
| **Occupation and hobbies** | Hazardous jobs = higher premium; crucial for **disability** insurance |
| **Community rating** | Same rates for everyone in a community regardless of loss experience; **small groups/individuals**; based on insurer's overall claims and local healthcare costs |

## 9. The initial premium and premium receipts

- A receipt shows the applicant's good-faith payment of the initial premium for the insurer's promise to pay. It can establish coverage **before** the policy is issued.
- It's in the proposed insured's interest to **pay with the application** (immediate protection, with restrictions).
- No premium with the application? The agent **still submits it** (a **trial application**). Even if approved and issued, the policy **isn't effective until the initial premium is collected**.
- **Consideration** = **application + first premium**. No insurer lets an applicant possess a policy without the initial premium.
- Agent must leave a **premium receipt** when collecting the premium. The **receipt type decides when coverage starts**. The receipt date is always **earlier** than the issue date.
- Two types: **conditional** (predominant today) and **binding** (limited use).

### Receipt comparison

| | **Conditional: insurability type** | **Conditional: approval type** | **Binding receipt** | **Temporary insurance agreement** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage starts | **Later of** application date or required medical exam date | Only after the insurer **approves** | **Immediately** on premium payment | Immediately |
| Condition | Applicant proves **insurable as applied for** (at that date) | Approval | None; coverage until **formal rejection** | Covers the whole underwriting period |
| If uninsurable | **No coverage**, premium refunded | No coverage | Still covered until rejection; claim must be paid | Insurer may cancel when denied; claims during underwriting still paid |
| Notes | **Most common**; the default meaning of "conditional receipt" | Covers only between approval/issue and delivery. Legal system opposes it; **rarely used** | Also "unconditional receipt." Has a **maximum payable**. Far more common in **auto/homeowners** | Similar to binding; terms sometimes interchangeable; **limited** amount |

**Insurability-type example:** application **Aug 2**, required exam **Aug 4** → coverage from **Aug 4**. If the applicant dies before underwriting finishes, the insurer still underwrites normally: approved → pays the claim; declined → no death benefit, premium refunded to the owner or beneficiary. **Policy delivery isn't needed** for coverage.

**Exam Tips (receipts):**
- A "conditional receipt" with no qualifier = **insurability type**. (Said twice.)
- **Conditional:** coverage only if underwriting confirms insurability as of the application or exam date. **Binding:** immediate coverage until the insurer decides.

### Case study: "A Tale of Two Receipts" (critical illness, XYZ Insurance)

| | **Michael Wilson: conditional (insurability)** | **Thomas Garcia: binding** |
|---|---|---|
| Applied / paid | March 10, paid premium | March 10, paid premium |
| Exam | March 15: no diagnosable conditions | March 15: **undiagnosed diabetes** found |
| Event | **Stroke March 30** | **Appendicitis March 25** (unrelated) |
| Decision | Approved and issued **April 5** | **Declined April 8** (diabetes) |
| Event claim | **Covered**: insurable as of **March 15** | **Covered** despite denial |
| Ongoing diabetes | N/A | **Not covered after April 8** |
| Premium refund | None (policy issued) | **Full refund minus claim costs** |
| Future | Policy continues | Must find coverage elsewhere |

**Key lessons:** conditional = coverage only if insurable; binding = temporary coverage regardless of insurability; receipt type changes claim outcomes during underwriting; agents must explain receipts clearly.

Discussion question answers (worked out from the course rules):
1. *Michael with a binding receipt?* Same result here: covered until a decision, and he was approved.
2. *Thomas with a conditional receipt?* He wasn't insurable as of March 15, so **no coverage** for the appendicitis; premium refunded.
3. *Why do insurers prefer conditional?* They only cover people who turn out insurable.

### Offer and counteroffer
- Application + first full premium = applicant's **offer**. Issued as requested = **acceptance**, contract in force. Declined = offer rejected.
- Issued only as **rated/substandard** → insurer **rejects** the offer and makes a **counteroffer**. The applicant may reject it, or **pay the extra premium, sign a statement of continued good health, and accept**.

## 10. Policy issue and delivery

- After underwriting, other departments issue the policy; it goes to the **agent** for delivery (not straight to the owner) so the agent can **explain** it.
- Most states require an **outline of coverage** for each applicant; some sales (e.g., **LTC**) require an NAIC **Shopper's/Buyer's Guide**, generally **before** the agent accepts the initial premium.

### Effective date
Why it matters: shows when coverage starts; sets the date future annual premiums are due; if a receipt was issued, the **receipt's date** generally becomes the effective date.

No premium with the application → **not effective until the premium is paid**, usually at delivery. Then: **policy delivered + first premium paid + Statement of Good Health obtained**.

**Exam Tip:** "effective date of coverage" / "when coverage begins" is **NEVER the issue date**. Premium with the application → coverage begins sooner. No premium → not effective until the policy is **delivered** and the **first premium collected**.

### Delivery concepts
| Concept | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Constructive delivery** | Insurer gives up **all control** to someone acting for the owner, **including its own agent**. Mailing to the agent for **unconditional** delivery counts even if the agent never delivers it. If told **not to deliver unless the applicant is in good health → no constructive delivery**. Mere possession isn't delivery if conditions aren't met. |
| **Inspection receipt** | Applicant reviews the policy before buying: no premium, no receipt, no health statement; signs an inspection receipt, then pays. **Not in force** during inspection. Mostly **obsolete** because all states require a **free look**. |
| **Explaining ratings** | Deliver in person to prevent misunderstandings, returns and lapses. On a **rated** policy, explain why the need is **greater** given the condition, and the extra premium. |
| **Statement of continued good health** | Premium collected at delivery → agent gets a signed statement that health hasn't changed since the application/exam, and sends it with the premium. No contract until premium is paid. |
| **Delivery receipt** | Insured signs on receipt; proves delivery; can **start the free-look period**. |

**Exam Tip:** inspection receipts are **not premium receipts**; they **delay** payment. If a question says "premium receipt," rule out the inspection receipt.

### Delivery scenarios (course table)
| Scenario | Premium | Health statement | Policy status | Agent must |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Complete delivery | Paid at delivery | Signed | Immediately in force | Get delivery receipt; explain |
| Premium previously paid | With application | Not needed if within a reasonable time | In force from **receipt date** | Verify receipt; explain |
| Delayed delivery | With application | Required if **significant delay** | In force after verification | Document delay; get new statement |
| Inspection only | None | Inspection receipt | **Not in force** | Explain no coverage; follow up |
| COD policy | Collected at delivery | Signed | In force after processing | Collect and submit promptly |
| Health change at delivery | Paid | Reveals change | **Pending re-underwriting** | Report it; **don't leave the policy** |
| Constructive delivery | Paid | N/A | **Legally in force** | Document; notify company |
| Free-look period | Paid | N/A | In force but cancellable | Explain free-look rights |

Matching quiz answers: **delivery receipt** = starts free look; **statement of good health** = health unchanged; **inspection receipt** = review before buying; **outline of coverage** = summarizes benefits and limitations.

## 11. Sources of underwriting information

The **application** is the primary source. Others are added when the application reveals conditions or risks.

| Source | What it provides / when used |
|---|---|
| **Application** | Primary: personal, medical, financial |
| **Agent's report** | Agent's observations, replacement info |
| **Attending Physician Statement** | Details on specific conditions; requested for questionable medical history; needs **signed authorization** |
| **MIB** | Past applications, coded medical info |
| **Medical report / exam / lab tests** | Objective health data for **higher face amounts** (EKG, treadmill, blood, HIV) |
| **Prescription drug databases** | Medication history (and compliance) |
| **Inspection / credit report** | Character, reputation, finances, lifestyle; **larger policies**; moral hazard |
| **Special questionnaires** | Hazardous hobbies, aviation, foreign residence, finances, military, occupation |
| **Motor vehicle (DMV) report** | Listed among sources |

### MIB details
- Formed by **700+** insurers; the industry's **"shared memory."**
- Shows prior health history, **life insurance in force with other carriers**, lifestyle habits (drugs, drinking, overeating, smoking).
- Example: diabetes disclosed before but omitted now → shows up on the MIB check.
- Purpose: reliable medical source; catch forgotten, concealed or fraudulent information. Helps keep costs down by preventing misrepresentation and fraud; helps insurers avoid high-risk applicants.
- Members **must report** impairments using a **code number**. Info is for **member companies only**, used **only for underwriting and claims**. May be released to the proposed insured's **physician**.
- **MIB doesn't make underwriting decisions.**

### Other sources
- **Medical report:** insurers set **non-medical limits**; above them a report from the insured's physician or an exam may be required.
- **APS example:** **Jason** disclosed type 2 diabetes; underwriter asked for 2 years of A1C levels; consistently **below 7.0** → **standard** rates, not rated or declined.
- **Rx database example:** 5 years of BP medication = managed but ongoing condition. Tell clients to disclose **all** regular medications.
- **Special questionnaires:** non-medical aspects; most common is the **aviation questionnaire**. Example: mountain climbing.
- **Inspection reports:** for **large amounts**; from national investigative firms; may include a phone interview and a credit report; interviews with employers, neighbors, associates. Applicant has the **right to a copy** of an investigative consumer report. Insurer's duty on nonpublic information: **give notice, explain, allow opt-out**. Insurer must tell the prospect it may get the report under the **FCRA**. Usually not for small policies.
- **Credit reports:** poor credit → likely to **lapse early**, maybe before the second premium, so the insurer can't recover acquisition costs.

### Case study: Jennifer Chen (43, marketing executive, comprehensive health)
Application alone (non-smoker, no reported conditions, mother's breast cancer at 51, social drinker, exercises 2–3×/week) looked **preferred**.

| Source | Revealed | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| MIB | Code for past **anxiety** treatment | Triggered more requests |
| Rx database | Current anxiety Rx; past short-term sleep Rx | Confirmed **undisclosed** condition |
| APS | Anxiety managed **5 years**, well controlled | Context; lowered concern |
| Medical exam | Normal vitals, great cholesterol, BMI **26.4** | Good health; minor weight concern |
| Credit report | Excellent credit, stable job | Positive for premium payment |

**Result:** **standard** (not preferred), **no rating or exclusions**. Without extra sources the underwriter would have missed the anxiety and wrongly classed her preferred.

## 12. Relevant laws

| Law | What to know |
|---|---|
| **GINA (2008)** | No discrimination based on **genetic information** in accident and health insurance. Insurers **can't use genetic testing** for underwriting. |
| **Unfair discrimination (state)** | **Sexual orientation, religious preference, geographic location** are **prohibited** underwriting factors. Where state law requires, agent signs a form saying a **disclosure statement** was given. |
| **HIPAA Privacy Rule** | Protects individually identifiable health information. Agent must give **privacy notices** when submitting an application with personal info. Insurer must notify affected parties of improper release (see section 16). Producers must get a signed **HIV consent form** even when blood tests are routine. |
| **FCRA (1970)** | Protects consumers whose **inspection or credit reports** are requested; applies to insurers. Covers **confidentiality, accuracy, disclosure**. Declined for poor credit → insurer must tell the applicant a copy is available from the credit bureau. MIB members must say whether the report played any part in **denial or a higher rate**. |
| **Information and Privacy Protection Act** | Comply with state/federal rules on sharing private info. Insurers **can't base a decision solely on previous adverse underwriting decisions** from MIB or medical reports. |
| **USA PATRIOT Act (2001)** | Deter terrorism; insurers need written **anti-money laundering (AML)** programs (compliance officer, training, ongoing AML training). Concern: **cash-surrender-value life insurance**. Expanded law enforcement search of records and Treasury authority over financial transactions. |
| **State regulations (review notes)** | Prohibit unfair discrimination, require disclosure statements, mandate **free-look** periods. |

**Exam Tip:** HIPAA requires applicants to get a **privacy notice** on how their health information is handled. Know **when and how** privacy notices are required.

## 13. Every EXAM TIP in the chapter

1. **Replacement:** the most important thing when replacing medical insurance is the **exclusions or limitations on the new policy**.
2. **Application = consideration:** application + initial premium = the applicant's **consideration**; the application is the **formal request** for insurance.
3. **Misrepresentation:** incomplete or inaccurate applications → **delayed underwriting or denial**. Expect questions on misrepresentation consequences.
4. **Signatures:** applicant **and** agent must sign; if the insured isn't the owner, **everyone except the beneficiary** signs. "A common exam trap!"
5. **Risk classes:** know standard, preferred and substandard precisely; scenarios ask you to classify.
6. **Conditional receipt default:** with no qualifier, assume **insurability type**.
7. **Conditional receipt default (again):** unless approval type is stated, "conditional receipt" means insurability type.
8. **Conditional vs binding:** conditional = coverage only if underwriting confirms insurability as of the app/exam date; binding = immediate coverage until a final decision.
9. **Effective date:** NEVER the issue date. Premium with application → sooner; no premium → at delivery + first premium.
10. **Inspection receipts:** not premium receipts; they delay payment. "Premium receipt" rules them out.
11. **HIPAA:** applicants must get a **privacy notice**; know when and how.

### Review-notes exam tips
- **Distinguish:** conditional vs binding; insurability vs approval type; preferred/standard/substandard; **physical, moral and morale hazards**.
- **Trick questions:** receipt types and when coverage begins; signature requirements; misrepresentation; **MIB usage and limits**.
- **Remember:** applicant and agent both sign; conditional receipts are most common in health; misrepresentation can void coverage during the contestable period; **MIB prevents fraud but doesn't make decisions**; underwriting protects against adverse selection; premium = morbidity, interest, expenses; field underwriting is the agent's job; HIPAA requires privacy notices.

## 16. Spots where the course contradicts itself (or is unclear)

1. **Johnson is "standard" with +30%.** Standard is defined as **no additional rating**, and a rated-up premium means **substandard**. In the 3.3.5 cases, standard Michael (controlled BP, BMI 27, ex-smoker) pays the **standard rate**, while substandard Robert is rated up 50%. **For the exam: extra premium = rated/substandard; standard = standard rate.**
2. **What "insurable" means for a conditional receipt.** 5.2.1 says insurable **as a standard risk**; 5.2.1.1 says insurable **as applied for**. The review notes say coverage starts at the **later of application or exam date**, then say insurability type means insurable **as of the application date**. Go with **later of the application or exam date**, insurable as applied for.
3. **Tale of Two Receipts:** Michael had a **stroke**, but the outcome table says **"heart attack."** Same event; the answer is the same (covered).
4. **Temporary insurance agreement:** the text says claims during underwriting are paid **regardless of approval**; the quick-reference table says coverage applies **"only if the applicant would qualify."** Text and review notes agree on "regardless," so go with that. The section is also numbered **5.2.3 twice**.
5. **Issue date as effective date:** 6.1 says the issue date "may be listed as the effective date," but the Exam Tip says coverage **never** begins on the issue date. **For the exam: never the issue date.**
6. **Binding receipts: rare in what?** Quick reference: "rare in **life**." Review notes: "rare in **health**." Text: much more common in **auto/homeowners** than life or health. Takeaway: rare in both life and health.
7. **"Agent cannot bind coverage" vs binding receipts.** The agent can't promise coverage on their own; a binding receipt is the **insurer's** contract that the agent hands over.
8. **Probationary period length.** This chapter says a **one-time 30-day** period. Chapter 9 called **30 days the maximum** and 10 days common. Use 30 days for this chapter's questions.
9. **HIPAA wording.** The course says the insurer must notify affected parties of an "**authorized**" release of private information. It almost certainly means **unauthorized**.
10. **Life-insurance wording in a health chapter.** Policy summary lists cash values, dividends and the **life insurance cost index**; MIB "minimizes the cost of **life** insurance" and flags applications "for **life** insurance"; the receipt example talks about a **death benefit**. The rules apply the same way in health questions.
11. **"Morale hazard"** is listed in the review notes' exam tips, but no page in these screenshots defines it (only moral hazard). It may be in the missing 3.4.4+ pages.
12. **Numbering typo:** "The Applicant" is labeled **[2.12]** instead of 2.1.2.
