## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning (course wording, condensed) |
|---|---|
| **Administrative Services Only (ASO)** | A self-funded employee welfare benefit plan contracts with an insurer (or independent TPA) for administration only. The plan stays responsible for claim costs; the insurer acts as a third-party administrator. Services: claims, Rx drug cards, COBRA administration, employee communications. |
| **Blue Cross / Blue Shield** | Service providers that pay participating providers directly. **Blue Cross = hospital** expenses; **Blue Shield = physician / surgical & medical** services. Members are called **subscribers**. |
| **Capitation** | Payment **per patient, not per service**. HMO pays a flat amount per person. If the patient never uses care, the fee is "wasted" for the HMO; heavy use means the provider takes a loss. |
| **Case management** (utilization review) | A specialist inside the insurer (e.g., an RN) reviews potentially large claims as they develop and discusses treatment alternatives with the insured. |
| **Certificate of insurance** | Given to each group participant ("evidence of insurance"). Participants are covered under one master contract and do **not** get separate policies. |
| **Change-of-life event** | Marriage, divorce, birth: lets a person buy/change coverage outside normal enrollment. |
| **Closed panel** | Providers deliver services inside HMO facilities (staff, group, network models). |
| **Open panel** | Providers see members in their own offices, part-time (IPA model). |
| **COBRA group** | Employer with **20 or more** employees. |
| **Coinsurance** | A **percentage** of costs, up to an annual maximum. |
| **Copayment** | A **fixed dollar** amount per visit. Primary purpose: cover **administrative costs** (may also be cost sharing). |
| **Community rating** | Small groups. Premium based on the surrounding community's claims experience and costs. |
| **Experience rating** | Large groups. Premium based on the group's own average number and cost of past claims. |
| **Contributory plan** | Employees and employer share the premium. |
| **Noncontributory plan** | Employer pays the entire premium. |
| **Conversion privilege** | Right to convert group coverage to an individual policy at termination, without evidence of insurability. |
| **Creditable coverage** (HIPAA) | Prior coverage with **no break of 63 days or longer**. Reduces/eliminates a new plan's preexisting-condition waiting period. Includes individual major medical, group major medical, Medicaid, comparable plans, and COBRA coverage. |
| **Dental HMO (DMO)** | HMO approach for dental. A primary care dentist (PCD) manages care and refers. Copays, sometimes coinsurance. Sometimes called a prepaid plan. |
| **Discount Medical Plan Organization (DMPO)** | **Not insurance.** Access to providers at discounted rates for a fee/dues. Typically regulated by the state department of insurance; some states may not permit them. |
| **Employee welfare benefit plan** | ERISA (1974)-compliant, employer-funded plan giving benefits other than pensions. |
| **Enrollment period** | Limited window (typically once a year, a set number of days) to sign up. Most providers allow **31 days** for new eligibles. |
| **Evidence of insurability** | Required when enrolling outside the enrollment period (late enrollees). Controls adverse selection. |
| **Extension of benefits** | Keeps paying an **existing** covered claim after the person is no longer eligible (fixed period or as long as the claim lasts). Does **not** cover new claims. |
| **Franchise (wholesale) plan** | Coverage for association / professional society members who don't qualify for true group. **Individual policies** are issued; the association is only the sponsor; premiums typically discounted. |
| **Group health insurance** | One master contract covering a group (employers, trade/professional associations, labor unions, credit unions). No individual underwriting / evidence of insurability for eligible members who enroll on time. |
| **HMO** | Comprehensive, **prepaid** plan that combines **financing and delivery** of care; emphasizes preventive care; fixed periodic fee instead of fee per service. |
| **Indemnity plan** (major medical) | Traditional plan, no networks; reimburses the insured. |
| **Master contract** | Issued to the sponsor (master policyholder), who is responsible for premiums. Contract is between insurer and sponsor. |
| **MET (Multiple Employer Trust)** | Combines **10 or more** employers (per keywords) into one pool; the **MET holds the master contract**, not the employers. A fully insured MEWA set up by a third party (insurer or TPA). |
| **MEWA** | Arrangement giving health/welfare benefits to employees of **2 or more unrelated employers** (ERISA §3(40)). Subject to ERISA and state law (except where state law conflicts with ERISA). Can be fully insured or self-funded. |
| **Natural group** | A group that exists for a reason **other than obtaining insurance**. |
| **Notice of Privacy Practices** | HIPAA notice given at enrollment and at least **every 3 years** after. |
| **Participating providers** | Providers who contractually agree to specific fees (BC/BS). |
| **POS** | HMO-like in-network care plus limited out-of-network coverage. |
| **PPO** | Sponsor contracts with a provider network at **prearranged discount prices**; fee-for-service. |
| **EPO** | Hybrid of HMO and PPO: no referrals needed for in-network specialists, but **no out-of-network coverage except true emergencies**. |
| **Pre-admission certification** | Evaluation before a hospital stay for surgery to confirm it's medically necessary. |
| **Pregnancy Discrimination Act (1978)** | Amends the Civil Rights Act of 1964: pregnancy treated like any other condition for employment purposes. |
| **Primary care physician (PCP)** | The **gatekeeper** in HMOs and POS plans; controls referrals. |
| **Probationary period** | (1) Employment: waiting time before joining a group plan (often 1–3 months). (2) Policy: illness excluded (accidents covered) for 10–30 days after the policy is first in force. |
| **Qualifying event** (COBRA) | Termination, disability, death, divorce, etc. (full list in Section 9). |
| **Prospective / Concurrent / Retrospective review** | Before admission / while care is provided / after treatment. |
| **Service basis / service providers** | BC/BS: services from participating providers in exchange for premium. |
| **Small employer** | Generally **2 to 50** full-time employees (30+ hrs/week); varies by state. |
| **Stop-loss insurance** | Bought by a self-funded employer; reimburses the employer when total claims exceed the risk retention limit (deductible). Best for large employers. |

## 3. Traditional plans

### Indemnity (major medical)
- Insurer **reimburses** the insured after services. Sold by commercial insurers (life, casualty, monoline companies).
- **No networks**, national scope, free choice of providers, higher out-of-pocket costs.
- **Right of assignment**: insured can assign benefits directly to the provider.

### Blue Cross / Blue Shield
- Service providers, organized locally / by state; some started as unincorporated cooperatives. Collect premiums, administer, adjust losses. Scheduled benefits. Offer **individual and group** coverage.
- Differ from indemnity plans in **2 ways**:
  1. **Pay providers directly.** Participating providers agree to costs; any remaining balance is billed to the insured.
  2. **Community-basis underwriting** (indemnity insurers use individual and group experience).

### Same patient, two plans (course scenario: Robert, 54, cardiac referral)
| | Indemnity plan | BC/BS |
|---|---|---|
| Specialist access | Calls cardiologist directly, no prior authorization | No prior authorization; shows Blue Shield card, $5 copay |
| Who bills | Robert pays hospital $2,200 up front, then files a claim | Doctor bills Blue Shield; participating hospital bills Blue Cross at pre-negotiated rates |
| Payment math | Plan: $250 deductible, 80% of UCR. UCR = $2,000, so insurer pays $1,600. Robert owes the $200 over UCR | Robert sees no bills except small copays / noncovered items |
| Robert's total out of pocket | **$850** = $250 deductible + $400 coinsurance + $200 over UCR | Small copays only |
| Pharmacy / follow-up | Pays and submits receipts | Small copay, pharmacy bills Blue Shield |

## 4. Managed care and cost control

Managed care = controlling how policyholders use their policies (also called medical cost management, cost containment, claims control, case management). Policy design tools: deductibles, coinsurance, shortened benefit periods.

**Four general approaches:** mandatory second opinions, pre-certification review, ambulatory surgery, case management.

| Method | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Mandatory second surgical opinion** | Before elective surgery; insured pays more out of pocket if they get only one opinion. |
| **Preventive care** | Annual physicals, early detection. |
| **Ambulatory surgery** | Outpatient surgery, often at standalone ambulatory surgical ("ambulatory care") centers, costs less than a hospital. |
| **Case management / utilization review** | Insurer takes an active role on potentially large claims. |

### Utilization review types
| Type | When | Purpose / details |
|---|---|---|
| **Prospective** (pre-certification, pre-admission, pre-hospitalization) | **Before** admission | Insurer approves elective hospital stay; checks overall health and medical necessity; shortens stays. Skipping it in a nonemergency **reduces or eliminates** the insurer's obligation. Emergencies: notify after admission. |
| **Concurrent** | **During** care | Nurse/case manager monitors length of stay, setting and regimen; decides release, home health care, or transfer (hospice, extended care facility). |
| **Retrospective** ("look-back") | **After** care | **Not** meant to deny claims; checks care matched coverage. Research tool using large data; refines future claim approvals and internal guidelines. Downside: biases and incomplete data. |

## 5. Managed care networks

### HMO basics
- Prepaid, comprehensive; **combines financing and delivery**. Can be for-profit or not-for-profit.
- Sponsors: employers, physicians, hospitals, labor unions, government, medical schools, associations, BC/BS, consumer groups, insurers.
- Regulated by state and federal law. Access **24/7**.
- **30-day open enrollment** each year: must advertise it, let current subscribers stay or switch, and let non-subscribers join.
- Covers hospital and outpatient services and ACA essential health benefits; typically **not dental or vision**.
- **Preventive care with no deductible** (the ACA later adopted this approach).
- **Local or regional** networks (major medical is national).
- **Emergencies**: in-network coverage at any hospital if life-threatening; go to the nearest ER and notify the PCP ASAP.
- Non-emergency out-of-network: member may pay up to **100%** of the bill.

**Federal requirements for an employer to offer an HMO:** at least **25 employees**; employer must contribute; can't charge more than a commercial plan; must stress preventive care and cover family planning; keep minimum reserves; set premiums by **community rating**.

### The four HMO models
| Model | Panel | How physicians work / get paid |
|---|---|---|
| **Staff** | Closed | Physicians are **employed (salaried) by the HMO**, work only for it, in HMO-owned facilities. |
| **Group** (medical group / group practice) | Closed | HMO pays **capitation** to **one** physician group; the group pays its physicians. |
| **Network** | Closed | Like group, but **more than one** physician group; capitation; physicians work in HMO facilities. |
| **IPA** (Independent Practice Association) | **Open** | Physicians in **their own offices**, **part-time** with the HMO; may also see non-HMO patients; **broadest choice** of PCPs and specialists. |

### HMO vs PPO vs POS vs EPO
| Feature | HMO | PPO | POS | EPO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Role | Finances **and** delivers | Finances only | HMO + indemnity hybrid | HMO + PPO hybrid |
| PCP gatekeeper / referrals | Yes | No (direct access to in-network specialists) | Yes, for full in-network coverage (some plans no longer require) | No referrals needed |
| Out-of-network coverage | None except emergencies | Yes, at higher cost | Yes, higher cost-sharing (coinsurance often **30–40%** of U&C, higher deductibles and OOP max) | None except actual emergencies |
| Payment style | Prepaid, copays | Fee-for-service at discounted prices; coinsurance (may mix with copays) | In-network like HMO (copays); out-of-network like indemnity | — |
| Cost | Lowest OOP | Often higher OOP | Higher when out of network | Less than PPO |
| Utilization risk | Shared with providers (HMO pays even if no care used) | Sponsor never pays more than the negotiated price | — | — |

Why PPO out-of-network costs more: (1) reduced benefits / higher coinsurance, and (2) out-of-network providers don't follow discount pricing, so the PPO pays only a % of usual and customary.

PPO extras: can be open or closed panel; may include dental and long-term care; contracting groups include commercial insurers, HMOs, BC/BS, employers, and trade unions.

### HMO vs PPO: the course's cardiac scenario
| Stage | HMO | PPO |
|---|---|---|
| Initial access | Must see assigned PCP | Any physician |
| Specialist referral | PCP sends formal referral to HMO for approval | None |
| Waiting | Wait for authorization, often **1–7 days** | None |
| Specialist choice | HMO network only | Any; chose in-network to save |
| Diagnostic tests | Covered with referral | Subject to deductible and coinsurance |
| Cardiac catheterization | Separate prior authorization | Prior authorization |
| Hospital | HMO-affiliated | Any (chose in-network) |
| Follow-up | May need more referrals | Book directly |
| PCP visit cost | **$20–30 copay** | Deductible, then **20%** |
| Specialist cost | **$40–50 copay** | Deductible, then 20% (~$40 on a $200 visit) |
| Procedure cost | **$100–250 facility copay** | Deductible, then 20% (~$1,000 on $5,000) |
| Out of network | None except emergencies | Reduced rate, typically **50–60%** after a higher deductible |
| Paperwork | Higher for providers, lower for patient | Lower for providers, higher for patient |
| Care coordination | Centralized through PCP | Patient's job |

## 6. Group health insurance

- A **policy class, not a provider**. More Americans have group coverage than individual coverage; group members pay much less out of pocket, even in contributory plans.
- **Eligible (natural) groups:** employers, labor unions, trade associations (same industry), creditor-debtor groups, multiple-employer trusts (same industry), fraternal lodges.
- Employers may set different benefits for different **classes** (full-time vs part-time, salaried vs hourly, union) but **cannot discriminate against individuals within a class**.
- **Minimum size:** most states, small employee health plan minimum = **2** employees; fewer than 51 = "small group"; disability or LTC group plans may need **10 or more**.

### Participation requirements (protect against adverse selection and control admin cost; insurer may terminate if participation drops)
| | Contributory | Noncontributory |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | Employees pay part | Employer pays all |
| Typical participation | **75%** of eligibles | **100%** of eligibles |
| Course example | 400 eligible → need **300** | 400 full-time → cover **all 400** |
| Adverse selection | Enrollment periods help control it | Less concern |

These percentages are **benchmarks, not laws**.

### Ownership
Master contract goes to the employer/sponsor (**master policy owner**). Each person gets a **certificate of insurance** plus a booklet (name, benefits, beneficiaries). Group benefits are usually broader: ancillary plans, higher maximums, lower OOP.

### Why group costs less
1. **Consolidated administration** (one contract; sponsor handles info and services)
2. **Predetermined benefits** (employer + insurer schedules simplify underwriting)
3. **Increased persistency** (contract stays in force when one person leaves; new people keep joining)

### Underwriting
| | Experience rating | Community rating |
|---|---|---|
| Used for | **Large groups, 51+** | **Small groups, 2–50** |
| Based on | Group's own claims history | Claims of similar groups in the community |
| Result | Premium reflects group's own risk (age, industry, prior claims) | Similar rates for all small groups in the area |

If small groups join a larger association, premiums can be based on the combined experience.

Underwriters look at: reason the group exists; **stability** (low turnover); **persistency** (groups that switch insurers every year are not acceptable); benefits set by schedule; how eligibility works (probationary period preferred); contributory or not; prior claims (51+); size and composition (**higher average age = higher premium**); industry (hazardous jobs = higher premium). Participants must be a representative sample, or the plan fills up with the highest risks.

### Eligibility and enrollment
- Full-time = **30+ hours/week**. Working employees **65+** must be offered the same benefits as younger employees.
- Probationary period: often **1–3 months** of employment.
- Enrollment period: most allow **31 days**. Miss it = **late enrollee** → must qualify individually with **evidence of insurability**.
- Annual open enrollment; **special enrollment** for change-of-life events (marriage, birth).

### Small employer groups
- **2 to 50** full-time employees (30+ hrs), part-timers optional.
- Under the ACA, the group must include at least **one employee besides the employer** (not an immediate family member); an all-partners group is allowed if there are no other employees.
- Temporary workers generally not covered (employer's option). Community-rated. All employees must have access.

| | MEWA | MET | Franchise (wholesale) | Self-funded | ASO |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who | 2+ **unrelated** employers | Small employers pooled by a third party (insurer or TPA); 10+ employers per keywords | Association / professional society members | Single employer | Self-funded employer |
| Insured? | Fully insured or self-funded | **Fully insured** MEWA | Individual policies | Employer pays claims | Employer pays claims |
| Master contract holder | — | **The trust** | None (each gets own policy) | — | — |

| Key facts | Trust, corp, LLC or other entity; defined by **function**, not tax status | Employer subscribes to become a trust member; employees get certificates; premiums go into the trust | Separate application each; discounted premium since sponsor markets it; medical, hospital, surgical, disability; contributory or not. ## 8. Regulations

- **Marketing:** state ad and unfair-practice rules apply; strict penalties.
- **Jurisdiction (multistate groups):** governed by the state where the insurer **delivers** the group policy (Constitutional Doctrine of **Interstate Comity**). Sometimes: state of incorporation, where most employees live, or principal/home office.

### Termination of a group plan
Coverage ends when: group membership ends; employee stops being eligible (any reason); master policy is surrendered or canceled; employee stops paying a required contribution. Continuation options: extension of benefits, COBRA / state continuation, conversion.

### Extension of benefits
- For someone **sick or disabled when group membership ends**. Covers **existing** claims only, not new ones.
- **Disability:** benefits continue until the disability ends (course example: John's back injury, position eliminated, still paid).
- **Medical expense:** most states mandate **90 days to 12 months** for inpatient care that began while insured; most give coverage until **hospital discharge or 3 months, whichever comes first**.
- **Change of insurers:** old insurer extends benefits on the open claim; new insurer must cover everyone covered under the old plan, commonly credits deductibles paid, and covers other claims **without reference to preexisting conditions**.

### Conversion
- Convert to an individual policy **without evidence of insurability**; typically **31 days** to decide. Downside: **higher cost, fewer benefits**. ACA makes it less relevant.
- If an employer allows current employees to convert at termination, it must offer the same to former employees when COBRA ends.

## 9. COBRA

- Applies to employers with **20 or more** employees ("COBRA groups"). Non-compliant employers can lose the federal tax deduction for health insurance costs.
- Eligible as soon as the employee qualifies for the group plan.
- **Continuation, not conversion.** Temporary. Insured pays the **full premium**. Ends if the employer stops offering group health (e.g., bankruptcy). If the employer changes plans, COBRA members get the same enrollment option.

**Qualifying events:** employee dies; employee divorces covered spouse; dependent ages out (child turns 26); employee qualifies for Social Security disability; employee voluntarily resigns or retires; employer terminates employee for any reason **other than gross misconduct**.

### COBRA timeline
| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| Employer notice | No later than **14 days** after the qualifying event |
| Election (decision) period | Up to **60 days**, starting from the **later** of the event date or notice date. Fail to notify promptly → forfeit |
| Back premiums | Owed back to the date of separation (fired Mar 31, elect May 30 → pay April + May then; June due soon) |

### COBRA benefit periods
| Months | Trigger |
|---|---|
| **18** | Loss of employment (quit or fired, not gross misconduct, e.g. stealing) |
| **29** | Disability: qualifies for Social Security disability. Bridges to Medicare: the **5-month SSDI waiting period + 24 months** of SSDI before Medicare |
| **36** | Dependents: employee's death, divorce (or legal separation), child reaching the age limit |

### COBRA premium
**102%** of the full group premium (employee share + employer share + **2%** admin), paid directly to the insurer's COBRA claim unit.
Example: employee paid $200, total premium $500 → COBRA = **$510**/month.

COBRA coverage counts as **creditable coverage** under HIPAA.

## 10. HIPAA and the Pregnancy Discrimination Act

### HIPAA
- Passed in **1996** (the section text; the keyword list says 1997, so expect 1996 if it comes up).
- Stops new preexisting-condition exclusions when people change plans/jobs, as long as there's **no gap of 63 days or more** (creditable coverage).
- Governs special enrollment after marriage, divorce, birth.
- ACA reduced its role, but it still applies to non-ACA contracts such as **Medicare Supplement** and **Medicare Advantage** plans.
- **Notice of Privacy Practices** (Privacy Rule): plan sponsor gives it **no later than enrollment** and reminds subscribers at least **once every 3 years**. Must disclose: how protected health information is used/disclosed; individual rights; the plan's duty to protect it; privacy rights; contact info for complaints; need for permission before disclosures.
- Revised notice of material changes within **60 days**; website changes displayed by the effective date.

### Pregnancy Discrimination Act (1978)
- Amends the **Civil Rights Act of 1964**. Pregnancy, childbirth, and related conditions treated like any other condition.
- Must cover abortion **if the mother's life is in danger**, plus complications.
- Pregnancy is a disability to the extent the person can't work. If the employer offers disability, sick leave, or medical expense coverage, pregnancy must be covered on the same basis.
- Applies to insured and self-insured plans. Covers private employers with **15 or more** employees and public sector employees.

### Small-group special rules
- Federal: small group = **50 or fewer**; states usually **2–50**.
- **HIPAA and ACA**: small groups meeting participation rules get **guaranteed issue**; can't be refused for health history; must offer to all eligible employees and dependents; no discrimination by type of business.
- Must **renew** unless: nonpayment, fraud / intentional misrepresentation, or noncompliance (e.g., participation).
- Plan types: healthcare center plans, HMO plans, small employer carrier plans. Carriers must offer **at least 2 plans: standard and essential (basic)**.
- **Large groups (over 50):** not guaranteed issue, but **guaranteed renewable** once insured (renewal can be denied for nonpayment, fraud, leaving the market, or failing participation/contribution rules).

## 11. Every EXAM TIP from the chapter

1. **"Indemnity" has more than one meaning.** A major medical indemnity plan reimburses actual costs; a **hospital indemnity** plan pays a **fixed daily amount**.
2. **Blue Cross = hospital; Blue Shield = surgical & medical (physician).** Both offer individual and group; members are subscribers.
3. **Group limits are general standards.** For participation, size, or other group questions, use the general guidelines.
4. **"Probationary period"** can mean (a) the employment waiting time before joining a group plan, or (b) a policy provision excluding illness (accidents covered) for **10–30 days** after the policy starts.

**Review-notes exam tips:** know indemnity vs hospital indemnity; BC = hospital, BS = physician; the four HMO structures; PCP gatekeeper role in HMOs and some POS plans; copay (fixed $) vs coinsurance (%); the three utilization reviews; COBRA events and periods (18 / 29 / 36); experience vs community rating; contributory vs noncontributory participation; MEWA vs MET.

**"Remember" list:** probationary period has two meanings; COBRA = 102%; creditable coverage = no gap over 63 days; small groups are guaranteed issue but must meet participation; extension of benefits covers existing claims; PPOs cover out of network, HMOs and EPOs don't (except emergencies); self-funded plans are under ERISA and may buy stop-loss; group needs a natural group; managed care emphasizes prevention and cost control; Notice of Privacy Practices at enrollment and every 3 years.

## 13. Memory tricks (made up by Claude, not from the course)

- **Blue Cross = Building** (hospital); **Blue Shield = Surgeon** (doctor).
- **HMO models in order of freedom: "Staff Gets No Independence"** → Staff, Group, Network (all closed), then **I**PA = **I**ndependent, open panel, own offices.
- **Staff = Salary.** Group = one group; Network = "net" catches several groups.
- **EPO = "Exclusive"**: exclusive to the network, no outside coverage. No referrals though.
- **COBRA "18-29-36":** Job (18) → Disabled adds 11 (29) → Dependents get double the job (36).
- **COBRA "14 then 60":** employer has 2 weeks to tell you; you have 2 months to decide.
- **102% = "you pay the boss's share too, plus tip"** (the 2% admin fee).
- **63 days ≈ 2 months:** a gap of 2 months or more breaks creditable coverage.
- **Contributory = 3 out of 4 (75%); Noncontributory = everyone (100%)** because it's free to them.
- **Experience = Enormous groups (51+); Community = Corner shop (2–50).**
- **MET holds the "Master"**: M-E-T and M-aster both start with M.
- **Franchise = Fran gets her own policy**: individual policies, association just sponsors.
- **Retrospective review "looks back, doesn't take back"**: it's not for denying claims.
