## Key terms

These are the course's own keyword definitions, plus the core concepts from section 2.

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Insurance | The transfer of risk through the pooling or accumulation of funds |
| Insurer | The insurance company that provides coverage and assumes risk (also "insurance company" or "carrier") |
| Insured | The customer who receives protection under the policy |
| Agent | Authorized to solicit, sell, and transact coverage for specific insurers under an agent contract |
| Broker | Represents the insured (client), not the insurer, and cannot bind coverage |
| Producer | Anyone licensed to sell, solicit, or transact insurance: agents and brokers both |
| Stock company | Owned by stockholders; typically issues nonparticipating policies |
| Mutual company | Owned by policyholders; typically issues participating policies with potential dividends |
| Participating policy | Owner can receive dividends and elect the board of directors |
| Nonparticipating policy | No dividends and no voting rights for the owner |
| Underwriting department | Reviews applications, approves or declines coverage, assigns risk classifications |
| Claims department | Processes, investigates, and pays claims |
| Principle of indemnity | Restore the insured to the same financial position as before the loss; the insured may not profit from a loss |
| Contract of indemnity | Reimburses the actual loss: most accident, health, property, and casualty contracts |
| Valued contract | Pays a predetermined amount regardless of the actual loss: life insurance |

## Types of insurers

Insurers are either private (commercial) or government (social). "Insurance company," "carrier," and "insurer" all mean the same thing. A company selling more than one line is **multiline**; one line only is **monoline**.

### Private insurers at a glance

| Type | Owned by | Policy type | Dividends | Run by | What sets it apart |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stock company | Private investors (stockholders) | Nonparticipating | No | Board elected by stockholders | Goal is profit for stockholders |
| Mutual company | Policy owners | Participating ("par") | Yes, never guaranteed, not taxable | Board elected by policy owners | Insurance at lowest possible cost |
| Fraternal benefit society | Members | Participating | Yes, but not a priority | Directors and officers elected by members | Non-profit, lodge system, social activities |
| Reciprocal insurer | Subscribers | Participating | Yes | Attorney-in-fact | Risk is shared among subscribers, not transferred |
| Risk retention group (RRG) | Members | Participating | Yes | Directors and officers elected by members | Risk shared among members; liability only |
| Risk purchasing group (RPG) | Depends on policy bought | Depends | Depends | Officers appointed by founding entity | Buys insurance; not an insurer |
| Captive | Parent company | Varies | Varies | Varies | Insures the parent's own loss exposure |
| Reinsurer | n/a | Insurance for insurers | n/a | n/a | Ceding insurer stays responsible to client |
| Surplus lines | n/a | Insurance for high risks | n/a | n/a | Nonadmitted; fewer consumer protections |
| Lloyd's of London | n/a | Covers unusual risks | n/a | n/a | Not an insurer: a syndicate |
| Industrial insurer | n/a | Low face values ($1,000 to $2,000) | No | n/a | Agent collects premiums weekly at home |

### Details worth knowing

- **Stock companies:** stockholders supply capital and share profits or losses. Stock dividends are taxed like long-term capital gains. Retained earnings are called **earned surplus**.
- **Mutual companies:** each buyer is both customer and owner. Excess earnings are held as **divisible surplus** and returned as policy dividends, usually yearly. A policy dividend is a partial refund of premium.
- **Mutualization** turns a stock company into a mutual. **Demutualization** turns a mutual into a stock company; policyholders get shares in proportion to their premiums, often to raise money.
- **Mixed plan:** a company that issues both participating and nonparticipating policies.
- **Fraternal benefit societies** must be non-profit, have a lodge system with ritualistic work and a representative government with elected officers, and exist for reasons other than insurance. Membership may be based on religion, nationality, or ethnicity. They began on a pure assessment basis; today they mostly issue group insurance and annuities. Examples: Independent Order of Foresters, Knights of Columbus.
- **Reciprocal insurers** are unincorporated. Members (subscribers) insure each other, so it is **risk-sharing, not risk transfer**. The **attorney-in-fact** runs day-to-day affairs for the subscribers.
- **Assessment mutuals:** a pure assessment mutual charges no premium in advance; members are assessed a share of losses as they occur. An advance premium assessment mutual charges up front, returns surplus as dividends, and assesses more if premiums fall short. Maximum assessments are limited by state law or the bylaws.
- **RRGs** were created under the federal **Liability Risk Retention Act (LRRA) of 1986** for people with a common bond (for example pharmacists, dentists, engineers). They are licensed only in their home (domicile) state but can insure members nationwide. An RRG can be a type of captive; the exam treats it as its own class of carrier.
- **RPGs** fall under the same 1986 act, but they **buy** insurance instead of acting as insurers. The RPG is the master policyholder and members get certificates. Benefit: bargaining power and simpler administration.
- **Reinsurance:** the insurer that transfers risk is the **primary or ceding company**; the one that accepts it is the **reinsurer or assuming company**. What the ceding insurer keeps is the **net retention** (net line). **Treaty** reinsurance is automatic, by preset criteria. **Facultative** reinsurance is case by case for a specific risk.
- **Surplus lines** cover unusual or substandard risks (hole-in-one coverage, non-appearance coverage) through a licensed excess or surplus lines broker. Many states require proof of an unsuccessful try in the authorized market first. You cannot go surplus lines just because it is cheaper.
- **Lloyd's of London** works like the New York Stock Exchange: it provides the marketplace, gathers underwriting information, and helps settle claims. Its member underwriters provide the coverage.
- **Service providers** sell medical and hospital services, not insurance, to **subscribers**. **HMOs** take a fixed premium in advance, provide both financing and care, and stress preventive care. **PPOs** get discounts from providers in exchange for referring a group's members.
- **Industrial (home service or debit) insurers:** small face amounts, agent visits weekly to collect.

### Government (social) insurance

Government insurers are owned by a federal or state entity. They either cover catastrophic perils private insurers won't (war, flood, nuclear reaction) or compete with private insurers, as with workers' compensation.

Examples: Social Security (OASDI), Original Medicare (Parts A and B), Medicaid, SGLI and VGLI, the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), and the Federal Crop Insurance Corporation (FCIC). **SGLI pays up to $500,000 in $50,000 increments** for full-time armed services members. Social insurance ranges from crop insurance to FDIC deposit insurance.

## Classifications, people, sales, and oversight

### Insurer classifications

Insurers are classified two ways: by **authorization** (may it do business in this state?) and by **domicile** (where is it incorporated?).

| Classification | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Authorized / admitted | Holds a certificate of authority to do business in that state |
| Unauthorized / nonadmitted | No certificate of authority; prohibited from doing business in that state |
| Surplus lines insurer | Nonadmitted insurer allowed only in certain circumstances; heavily regulated |
| Domestic | Incorporated under the laws of the state where it is doing business |
| Foreign | Doing business in a state other than the one where it is incorporated |
| Alien | Incorporated outside the United States |

Course examples: XYZ, incorporated in Oklahoma, is domestic in Oklahoma and foreign in Texas. RST of Toronto is alien in Oklahoma. ABC of Puerto Rico is domestic in Puerto Rico but **foreign in Florida**. Rising Sun Life of Tokyo is alien in both Florida and Puerto Rico.

### Departments

- **Marketing or sales:** brings in prospective applicants; sales completes applications and meets buyers.
- **Underwriting:** reviews applications, investigates applicants, assigns risk classifications, approves or declines.
- **Claims:** processes, investigates, and pays claims.
- **Actuarial:** calculates rates, reserves, and dividends; does statistical studies on morbidity and mortality tables.

### Key people

| Role | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Producer | Solicits sales to the public; fiduciary to both insurers and clients; also called the "field underwriter"; part of the sales department |
| Agent | Represents one or more insurers under an appointment contract; acts for the insurer |
| Broker | Acts for the insured; can sell for multiple insurers |
| Solicitor | **Not licensed** to sell; solicits applicants for a producer |
| Service representative | Insurer employee; no license needed unless they get commissions, solicit, countersign policies, or collect premiums |
| Underwriter | Assesses and classifies applicant risk; approves or declines; sets the cost |
| Actuary | Calculates rates, reserves, dividends; cost of insurance as a whole or for a class of risk |
| Adjuster | Investigates to adjust, settle, or deny claims; may look into the insured's identity and credibility |
| Public adjuster | Paid to act for **insureds** in settling claims |

### How insurance is sold

Most people buy through licensed producers. Insurers also use **direct selling** (vending machines, ads, salaried sales reps) and **mass marketing** (internet, newspapers, magazines, radio, TV). In most states a producer must be contracted and appointed before taking an application. An agent has an agent's contract and can **bind** the insurer; a broker has a broker's contract. In a dispute, the agent who took the application represents the insurer, not the insured or beneficiary.

A **captive (career) agent** sells for one company. An **independent agent** sells for many and may represent as many insurers as will appoint them.

| System | Description | Recruited by | Supervised by | Agents are |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Career agency | Branch of a major stock or mutual insurer; focuses on building sales staff | General agent (GA) | GA | Selling for the agency; GA has vested right in their business |
| Managerial | Form of career agency with branch offices | Salaried branch manager | Branch manager | Working out of the branch office |
| Personal producing general agency (PPGA) | Like career agency, but PPGAs mainly sell; may build a small sales force | PPGA | Regional directors | Employees of the PPGA, not the insurer |
| Independent (American) agency | Creation of the property and casualty industry | n/a | n/a | Independent; paid by commission or fee; **own their book of business** |
| Direct selling | Insurer deals directly with consumers | n/a | n/a | Salaried sales reps |
| Mass marketing | Internet, print, radio, TV | n/a | n/a | n/a |

### Industry oversight

The main purpose of regulation is **insurer solvency**, plus consumer protection, fair trade practices, and fair contracts at fair prices.

| Law or case | Year | What it did | Remember |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paul v. Virginia | 1868 | Insurance is not interstate commerce | States regulate |
| U.S. v. SEUA | 1944 | Insurance is interstate commerce | Federal regulation |
| McCarran-Ferguson Act | 1945 | Returned primary regulation to states; federal antitrust laws still apply | Fines up to $10,000 and/or 1 year; health insurers lost antitrust exemption in 2021 |
| Fair Credit Reporting Act | 1970 | Consumer privacy; transparency in investigations | Tell applicants about investigations; name the reporting agency if coverage is denied |
| Fraud and False Statements Act (USC 1033 and 1034) | 1994 | Felons convicted of dishonesty or breach of trust need written state consent to work in insurance | Up to $50,000 and 10 years; up to 15 years if solvency is threatened; license revocation |
| Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act | 1999 | Banks, insurers, and securities firms may affiliate (repealed Glass-Steagall of 1933); privacy rules | Written privacy notices; opt-out for non-affiliated sharing; **opt-in** for health information; security programs |
| USA PATRIOT Act | 2001 | Fights terrorist financing | Anti-money laundering programs; monitor and report certain transactions |
| Do Not Call Implementation Act | 2003 | National Do Not Call Registry | Exempts charities, political organizations, surveys |
| CAN-SPAM Act | 2003 | Commercial email rules | Accurate headers, labeled as ad, physical address, easy opt-out, honor opt-outs within **10 business days** |

**Organizations**

- **NAIC (National Association of Insurance Commissioners):** every state regulator is a member. Formed shortly after Paul v. Virginia. Four goals: uniform state laws, efficient administration, protect policy owners and consumers, preserve state regulation. **The NAIC is not a regulator**: it writes model acts and regulations but does not enact or enforce laws.
  - Model **Unfair Trade Practices Act**: lets the state insurance head investigate, issue cease-and-desist orders, impose penalties, and seek injunctions. Unfair practices include misrepresentation, false advertising, coercion, intimidation, unfair discrimination, and unfair claims handling.
  - Model **Advertising Code**: bans words that are misleading by nature and requires full disclosure of renewal, cancellation, and termination provisions.
- **NCOIL (National Conference of Insurance Legislators):** formed **1969**; made up of state legislators. Preserves state regulation, writes Model Laws, and opposes federal encroachment on state authority under McCarran-Ferguson.
- **NAIFA and NAHU:** professional associations of life and health agents. They created a **Code of Ethics**: selling to needs, suitability, full and accurate disclosure, documentation, and client service (a sale is the beginning of the relationship).
- **Rating services** (A.M. Best, Fitch, Standard & Poor's, Moody's): not oversight. Their main purpose is to judge an insurer's **financial strength**, using reserves and liquidity. **Reserves** are the insurer's future obligations to policyholders, carried as liabilities. **Liquidity** is the ability to make unpredictable payouts. States set minimum reserve and liquidity levels before issuing a certificate of authority.

## Exam tips from the course

Every EXAM TIP box in the chapter, word for word in substance.

1. Insurance is the transfer of risk from one party to another in exchange for the payment of premiums.
2. The company providing the insurance is the **insurer**; the person receiving coverage is the **insured**.
3. **Participating** policies let policyholders participate by electing the board and receiving dividends from the divisible surplus. Mutual companies issue participating policies.
4. **Nonparticipating** policies give no election rights or dividends. Stock companies issue nonparticipating policies.
5. A company that issues both participating and nonparticipating policies operates under a **mixed plan**.
6. Reinsurance is "insurance for insurance companies."
7. **Treaty** reinsurance is broader and automatic. **Facultative** reinsurance is tailored to specific risks, case by case.
8. The **ceding** (original) insurer remains responsible to the client.
9. ABC Insurance Company of Puerto Rico is domestic in Puerto Rico and foreign in Florida. Rising Sun Life of Tokyo is alien in Florida and Puerto Rico.
10. **Agents represent insurers. Brokers represent insureds.**
11. **Career agencies** recruit, train, and supervise agents through managers or general agents; they build staff. **PPGAs** don't recruit, train, or supervise; they mainly sell. **Independent agents** (American agency system) represent any number of insurers by contract.

The chapter's review notes add: focus on differences between company types, legislation dates and purposes, distribution systems, department functions, regulatory organization roles, and participating versus nonparticipating. The regulatory framework is state-based, and consumer protection is a primary focus.

## Memory hooks

These are my own mnemonics built on the course facts, not from Xcel.

- **Mutual = Me.** Policyholders own it, so the profits come back to me as dividends. **Stock = Stockholders**: profits go to someone else.
- **"Par" means you get a share of the pie** (participating = dividends + vote). "Non-par" means no pie, no vote.
- **Agent = Arm of the insurer; Broker = Buyer's side.** Only the agent can bind.
- **Solicitor = Sends leads only.** No license, no sale.
- **Domestic = Home state. Foreign = Other state. Alien = Abroad.** Think of it like people: a Floridian in Georgia is from out of state, not out of the country.
- **Ceding = Ceding away** the risk; the reinsurer **assumes** it. The ceding insurer still owes the client.
- **Treaty = Tons, automatic** (a standing deal). **Facultative = For one** specific risk.
- **Reciprocal = Re-share.** Subscribers insure each other; the attorney-in-fact runs it. Sharing, not transfer.
- **RRG Retains risk; RPG Purchases coverage.** Both come from the LRRA of 1986, both are liability only.
- **Lloyd's is a Lobby, not a Lender of coverage.** It is a marketplace like the NYSE; its members underwrite.
- **Indemnity restores; valued pays a set amount.** Life = valued; health, accident, property, casualty = indemnity.
- **Regulation timeline: "Paul said States, SEUA said Feds, McCarran gave it back."** 1868, 1944, 1945.
- **NAIC writes Models, not laws.** NCOIL = actual Legislators (1969).
- **GLBA: Opt-OUT for general sharing, Opt-IN for health.** Health info is the special one.
- **CAN-SPAM: 10 business days** to honor an opt-out.
- **Fraud penalties go 50 / 10 / 15:** $50,000, 10 years, 15 if solvency is threatened. McCarran-Ferguson is the small one: $10,000 and 1 year.
- **Career builds staff; PPGA just sells; Independent owns the book.**
- **SGLI: $500,000 max in $50,000 steps.**
- **Industrial insurance: small face ($1,000 to $2,000), weekly visits.**

## Practice questions

Cover the answer line, pick, then check.

1. Which type of insurer is owned by its policyholders and typically issues participating policies? a) Stock company  b) Mutual company  c) Reciprocal insurer  d) Captive insurer **Answer: b.** Mutuals are owned by policyholders and issue par policies that may pay dividends.
2. A life insurance policy that pays a predetermined amount regardless of the actual loss is a: a) Contract of indemnity  b) Valued contract  c) Nonparticipating policy  d) Mixed plan **Answer: b.** Life policies are valued contracts; most accident, health, property, and casualty contracts are indemnity contracts.
3. Who represents the insured rather than the insurer and cannot bind coverage? a) Agent  b) Solicitor  c) Broker  d) Service representative **Answer: c.** Agents represent insurers; brokers represent insureds.
4. An insurer incorporated in Georgia is licensed to do business in Florida. In Florida it is a: a) Domestic insurer  b) Foreign insurer  c) Alien insurer  d) Nonadmitted insurer **Answer: b.** Foreign means incorporated in another U.S. state. Alien means incorporated outside the U.S.
5. In a reinsurance agreement, the company that transfers its risk is called the: a) Assuming company  b) Reinsurer  c) Ceding company  d) Attorney-in-fact **Answer: c.** The ceding (primary) insurer transfers risk and stays responsible to the client.
6. Reinsurance arranged case by case to cover one specific risk is: a) Treaty  b) Facultative  c) Net retention  d) Surplus lines **Answer: b.** Treaty reinsurance is automatic and broad; facultative is for a specific risk.
7. Which organization is made up of subscribers who insure each other through an attorney-in-fact? a) Fraternal benefit society  b) Risk purchasing group  c) Reciprocal insurer  d) Assessment mutual **Answer: c.** Reciprocals are unincorporated risk-sharing arrangements run by an attorney-in-fact.
8. Which is NOT a required trait of a fraternal benefit society? a) Non-profit  b) Lodge system with ritualistic work  c) Publicly traded stock  d) Exists for reasons other than insurance **Answer: c.** Fraternals must be non-profit, have a lodge system and representative government, and exist for more than insurance.
9. What is the key difference between a risk retention group and a risk purchasing group? a) Only RRGs offer liability coverage  b) RPGs buy insurance from an insurer; RRGs act as insurers  c) RPGs are licensed in every state  d) RRGs were created by the NAIC **Answer: b.** Both come from the LRRA of 1986, but an RPG is a master policyholder, not an insurer.
10. Lloyd's of London is best described as: a) A stock insurer  b) A government insurer  c) A syndicate of individuals and companies that underwrite insurance  d) A reinsurer only **Answer: c.** Lloyd's is not an insurer; it is a marketplace compared to the New York Stock Exchange.
11. Which Supreme Court decision held that insurance is interstate commerce subject to federal regulation? a) Paul v. Virginia (1868)  b) U.S. v. SEUA (1944)  c) McCarran-Ferguson (1945)  d) Gramm-Leach-Bliley (1999) **Answer: b.** McCarran-Ferguson then returned primary regulation to the states in 1945.
12. Under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, sharing protected health information requires: a) An opt-out notice  b) Affirmative consent (opt-in)  c) Approval from the NAIC  d) Nothing, if the party is affiliated **Answer: b.** General sharing with non-affiliated third parties is opt-out; health information needs opt-in.
13. Which statement about the NAIC is true? a) It enacts insurance laws  b) It enforces compliance in each state  c) It writes model acts and regulations  d) It was formed in 1969 **Answer: c.** The NAIC is not a regulator. NCOIL, made up of state legislators, was formed in 1969.
14. In which agency system do agents own their book of business and represent many insurers? a) Career agency  b) Managerial system  c) PPGA  d) Independent (American) agency **Answer: d.** The independent agency system came from the property and casualty industry.
15. Under the CAN-SPAM Act, opt-out requests must be honored within: a) 3 business days  b) 10 business days  c) 30 days  d) 60 days **Answer: b.** Email must also have accurate headers, be labeled as advertising, and include a physical address.
16. The primary purpose of a rating service such as A.M. Best is to: a) License insurers  b) Write model laws  c) Determine an insurer's financial strength  d) Investigate claims **Answer: c.** Ratings look at reserves and liquidity. Rating services are not technically oversight.
17. Who is NOT licensed to sell insurance but solicits applicants on behalf of a producer? a) Broker  b) Solicitor  c) Public adjuster  d) Agent **Answer: b.** A service representative also needs no license unless they take commissions, solicit, countersign, or collect premiums.
