## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning (course wording, condensed) |
|---|---|
| **Accelerated death benefit rider** | Pays part of a life policy's death benefit when the insured is diagnosed with a terminal illness (or other condition). Usually a **lump sum**. Amount paid is deducted from the death proceeds. **NAIC does not count it as LTC insurance.** |
| **Accepting assignment** | Provider accepts the **Medicare-approved amount as full payment** and won't bill the patient for more. |
| **Activities of daily living (ADLs)** | Six: **bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, continence, eating**. |
| **Adult day care** | Care for seniors who live at home while the family caregiver is away or at work. Level of care is like home health care. Centers usually provide transportation. Can be medically based (PT) or recreation-based. |
| **Assisted living facility (ALF)** | Residential community with **more limited services than a nursing home**. No around-the-clock care. Intermittent nursing (including medication management), housekeeping, meals, social activities. Someone needing help with **1 or 2 ADLs** may qualify. |
| **Automatic inflation rider (AIR)** | Raises the LTC **stated daily benefit**, compounded annually, typically **5% per year**, often for a limited number of years. Cost is **built into the initial premium**. |
| **Benefit period (Part A)** | Starts the day you're **admitted** to a hospital and ends **60 days after discharge**. Per claim, not per year. |
| **Benefit period (LTC)** | How long benefits are paid for one claim: 12 months, 2, 3, 5, 10 years, or life. Minimum **1 year**. **3 years** is common. |
| **Benefit trigger (LTC)** | Loss of **2 or more ADLs** or **cognitive impairment**. Either one qualifies. |
| **Buyer's Guide (Medigap)** | "Choosing a Medigap Policy: A Guide to Health Insurance for People with Medicare," made by CMS and NAIC, updated yearly. Must be given **at the time of application**. |
| **CMS** | Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services. Federal agency that runs Medicare and Medicaid. |
| **Cognitive impairment** | Deficiency in short or long-term **memory**; **orientation** to person, place, and time; deductive or abstract **reasoning**; or **judgment** about safety. |
| **Cold lead advertising** | **Illegal**. Marketing that doesn't disclose that an agent may contact you to sell a policy. |
| **Continuing care community (CCRC)** | Lets residents move from independent living to assisted living to an onsite skilled nursing home without leaving the community. |
| **Contingent nonforfeiture benefit** | Built into **all tax-qualified LTC** policies. Triggered by a **"significant premium increase."** Owner can choose reduced coverage or paid-up options. |
| **Core benefits** | Benefits every Medigap plan must include (Plan A's benefits). |
| **Custodial care** | Help with personal needs (bathing, dressing, walking, eating, taking medication). Given by trained people who are **usually not medical personnel**, but it must be **ordered by a healthcare professional**. |
| **Duplication of coverage** | **Illegal** to sell someone a second Medigap policy (other than a replacement). |
| **Elimination period (LTC)** | Works like a deductible (as in disability income). Insured pays all costs until it ends. In LTC it often happens **only once**, because the condition usually isn't curable. |
| **ESRD** | End-stage renal disease (kidney failure needing dialysis or transplant). |
| **Formal care** | Paid care from professionals or aides. **Covered** by LTC. |
| **Informal care** | Unpaid care by family. **Not covered**, but a policy may offer a **training allowance**. |
| **Formulary** | A Part D plan's list of covered drugs, sorted by category and tier. |
| **Guaranteed insurability option (LTC)** | Lets the insured buy more coverage at set future times, at **attained age**, based on assumed inflation (generally **5%**), **without proof of insurability**. |
| **Hands-on assistance** | Physical help with an ADL. |
| **Stand-by assistance** | Caregiver is **within arm's reach** and can help if needed. |
| **Substantial assistance** | Either hands-on **or** stand-by assistance (NAIC definition). |
| **High-pressure tactics** | **Illegal**. Selling through threats, excessive pressure, or fear. |
| **Home care** | **Personal care** at home: ADLs, meal prep, housework, transportation. Also called home setting or personal care. |
| **Home health care** | Mostly **skilled** care at home, part-time (visiting nurses, medication, wound care, therapy ordered by a doctor). |
| **Hospice** | Comfort care for the terminally ill (life expectancy **6 months or less**) at home or in a facility, instead of curative treatment. |
| **Hybrid (linked, asset-based) LTC** | Whole life or annuity combined with LTC. If no care is needed, you still get life or annuity benefits. Prioritizes LTC; must be underwritten. |
| **Intermediate care** | **Intermittent** (occasional) nursing and rehab care by skilled personnel on a doctor's orders. |
| **Lifetime reserve days** | **60** extra hospital days usable once in a lifetime. **Not restored.** |
| **LTC Partnership Program** | Federally supported, **state-operated**. Buying a qualified partnership policy protects some assets from Medicaid spend-down. Must be **tax-qualified** and include **inflation protection**. |
| **Medicare Administrative Contractors (MACs)** | Private insurers contracted by region to process Original Medicare claims. |
| **Medicare Advantage (Part C)** | Private, Medicare-approved "all-in-one" plan that replaces Parts A and B. |
| **Medicare Select** | A **Medigap** policy that uses a network (works like a closed-end HMO) in exchange for a **lower premium**. |
| **Medicare Summary Notice (MSN)** | Mailed **every 3 months**. Lists services billed, what Medicare paid, and what you may owe. **Not a bill.** |
| **Medigap (Medicare supplement)** | Private policy that fills gaps in **Original Medicare only**. Standardized lettered plans. |
| **Nonforfeiture options (LTC)** | Give value if the policy lapses for nonpayment: reduced paid-up, shortened benefit period, return of premium. |
| **Participating provider** | Accepts assignment, bills Medicare directly. Balance billing is **strictly prohibited**. |
| **Nonparticipating provider** | Doesn't accept assignment. Can charge **up to 15% more** than the approved amount. Patient pays the provider and files with Medicare. |
| **Pool of money** | LTC total benefit is limited by **dollars, not days**. Cheaper care makes benefits last longer. |
| **Post-claims underwriting** | **Illegal**. Approving everyone and underwriting only when a claim comes in. |
| **Respite care** | Short break for the unpaid family caregiver (overnight in-home stay, short facility stay, or adult day care). |
| **Skilled nursing care** | Daily nursing and rehab by or under skilled medical staff, on a doctor's orders, **24 hours**, with periodic physician review. |
| **Tax-qualified LTC** | LTC policy that meets **HIPAA** standards. |
| **Twisting** | **Illegal**. Using misrepresentation to get someone to replace a policy. |

## 3. Medicare overview

- **Founded 1965.** Run by **CMS**.
- Serves people **65 and older**, plus some younger people with disabilities or certain conditions.
- **Part A** = hospital insurance (inpatient, skilled nursing facility, home health, hospice).
- **Part B** = medical insurance (doctors, outpatient, DME, preventive).
- **Part C** = Medicare Advantage, private managed-care alternative (HMOs, PPOs).
- **Part D** = private prescription drug plans; add-on to Original Medicare or to MA plans that lack drug coverage.

### Original Medicare vs Medicare Advantage

| Feature | Original Medicare (A + B) | Medicare Advantage (Part C) |
|---|---|---|
| Run by | Federal government | Private insurers approved by Medicare |
| How it pays | Fee-for-service | Government pays the insurer a fixed amount per person (typically **95%** of average cost) |
| Cost sharing | Deductibles and coinsurance, **no annual out-of-pocket max** | **Annual out-of-pocket limit**, copays common |
| Doctors | Any provider that accepts Medicare | Networks (HMO restricted except emergencies; PPO costs more out of network) |
| Referrals | None | Required with HMOs |
| Coverage area | **Nationwide** | Limited to the plan's service area |
| Drugs | Not included (buy Part D) | Usually included |
| Extras | None | Often vision, dental, hearing |
| Medigap | Needed to fill gaps | **Not allowed** |
| Travel | Full U.S. (some Medigap plans add foreign emergencies) | Emergencies only outside the area |

## 4. Eligibility

### Age 65 and older
- Anyone 65+ entitled to Social Security automatically qualifies for **Parts A and B**.
- Full benefits at 65 if:
  - **U.S. citizen**, or permanent legal resident for **5+ continuous years**, **and**
  - they or their spouse earned **40 credits (10 years** of work, not necessarily in a row) under Social Security or Railroad Retirement, or paid Medicare taxes 10+ years.
- People 65+ who don't qualify can still buy in by paying an **unsubsidized premium**.

### Under 65

| Situation | When Medicare starts |
|---|---|
| SSDI or Railroad Retirement disability | After **24 months** of benefits (which follow the **5-month** SSDI waiting period) |
| **ALS** (Lou Gehrig's disease) | **Immediately**, no 24-month wait |
| **ESRD** | After a kidney transplant, or after **3 months** of dialysis |
| ESRD **and** group coverage (own or spouse's) | Group plan is **primary for the first 30 months**; Medicare is secondary |

## 5. Enrollment

### Basics
- Already getting Social Security at 65: **automatically enrolled in Part A** and offered Part B.
- Still working, not on Social Security: must enroll yourself through the **SSA** (in person or online).
- Enroll in Part A as soon as eligible, even if not retiring at 65.
- When you retire, enroll in Part B to avoid penalties, even if choosing MA.
- Three Part B enrollment periods: **initial, general, special**. Two change periods: **annual open enrollment** and the **MA open enrollment period**.

### Group coverage vs individual coverage at 65

| Situation | Who pays first | Part B |
|---|---|---|
| Still working, covered by employer group plan with **20+ employees** (COBRA group), own or spouse's | **Group primary**, Medicare secondary | May **delay Part B** with no penalty; use the SEP when you retire |
| Individual or retiree health policy | **Medicare primary** | Enroll in Part B **right away** with Part A |
| Group with **fewer than 20** employees (non-COBRA) | **Medicare primary** | Enroll in Part B right away |

- **ADEA** (Age Discrimination in Employment Act): employers can't deny benefits to older workers; they must give the same major medical benefits regardless of age (age-based reductions only in limited cases where the employer's cost stays equal to that for younger workers).
- Employers **cannot offer incentives** to Medicare-eligible workers to drop group coverage.

### Enrollment periods compared

| Period | Dates | Who / what | Coverage starts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)** | **7 months**: 3 months before birth month, the birth month, 3 months after | First chance for Part B (also Part C and Part D) | 1st of birth month, or 1st of the month after enrolling, whichever is later |
| **General Enrollment Period (GEP)** | **January 1 to March 31** each year | Missed the IEP; **penalty may apply** | 1st day of the following month |
| One-time switch to MA after late Part B | **April 1 to June 30** | After enrolling late in Part B during the GEP | (one time only) |
| **Special Enrollment Period (SEP)** | Any time while covered by an active-employment group plan, or **8 months** after the month group coverage or employment ends (whichever first) | Delayed Part B because of a **COBRA-regulated (20+) group**; **no penalty** | Enroll before or in the 1st month of retirement: 1st of the month enrolled. Remaining 7 months: 1st of the following month |
| **Annual Open Enrollment** | **October 15 to December 7** | Switch between Original and MA, switch MA plans, join/switch/drop Part D | **January 1** |
| **MA Open Enrollment** | **January 1 to March 31** | **MA members only**, change MA plans | 1st of the month after the plan gets the request |
| **Medigap open enrollment** | **6 months** starting the month you're both 65 **and** enrolled in Part B | Guaranteed issue, no evidence of insurability | — |

- IEP example: birthday in October → IEP runs **July 1** of the year you turn 65 through the **last day of January**.
- Miss the 8-month SEP → wait for the next GEP and pay the penalty.
- To delay Part B, notify Social Security and request "delayed enrollment." Only for **active** employees covered by their own or spouse's employer group plan.

### Late enrollment penalties

| Part | Penalty |
|---|---|
| **A** (for those who must pay a premium) | Monthly premium penalty based on delay. Review notes: **10% premium increase for twice the number of years delayed** |
| **B** | **10% for each full 12-month period** you could have been covered. **Lifetime** |
| **D** | **1% per month** without creditable drug coverage. **Lifetime** |

## 6. Premiums

| Part | Premium |
|---|---|
| **A** | **Most pay none** ("fully insured": FICA taxes for **10 years / 40 quarters**). Others pay a premium. Funded by FICA payroll and self-employment taxes. |
| **B** | **Everyone pays a monthly premium**, deducted from the Social Security check or billed monthly. Changes every year. (2025: $185; 2026 projected $206.50.) |
| **C** | Still pay the **Part B premium** (it helps fund MA), plus possibly the plan's own premium. Varies by network, extra services, cost sharing. |
| **D** | Each plan's premium is unique (deductibles, copays, formulary, pharmacy network). MA members can pick a plan with Part D for extra premium. |

## 7. Claims and appeals

- **MACs** process Original Medicare (Parts A and B and DME) claims on a fee-for-service basis.
- Original Medicare cost sharing is the patient's (unless they have Medigap). Part C cost sharing is set by the MA plan.

| Provider | How billing works |
|---|---|
| **Participating** (accepts assignment) | Bills Medicare and is paid directly. Accepts the approved fee as full payment. **No balance billing.** |
| **Nonparticipating** (non-assignment) | Collects from the patient. Can charge **up to 15% more**. Patient owes the extra and files the claim with Medicare to be reimbursed. |

- **Medicare Summary Notice (MSN):** mailed **every 3 months**, shows services, what Medicare paid, what you may owe. **Not a bill.**
- **Appeals:** **120 days** from receiving the MSN. Three ways:
  1. Send a **Redetermination Request Form** to the MAC.
  2. Use the MSN itself (follow "Appeals Information": explain, circle your info, list disputed charges).
  3. Send a written request to the Medicare contractor at the address on the MSN (dates, items, services, name and Medicare number, explanation, and any representative).

## 8. Part A: Hospital insurance

- Covers **inpatient hospital, skilled nursing facility, home health, hospice**.
- Free for people 65+ eligible for Social Security, but still has **deductibles, copays, and benefit period limits**.
- New benefit period **per claim**, not per year.

### Inpatient hospital
- Covers semi-private room, meals, nursing, drugs, tests, operating room, supplies. **Not** physician or surgeon charges.
- Up to **90 days per benefit period** (consecutive or not). Restored each new benefit period.
- **60 lifetime reserve days**, **never restored**.
- Benefit period: **admission** to **60 days after discharge**. Back in under 60 days = same period. Over 60 days = new period.
- Course examples:
  - **John**: 20 days in the hospital over 2 months → **70** days left. Healthy 6 months, then readmitted → new 90-day period.
  - **Jane**: 92 days → next time she has 90 basic days but only **58 reserve days**.

### Skilled nursing facility (SNF)
- Short-term recovery benefit after a hospital stay of **at least 3 inpatient days**, for the **same condition**.
- Up to **100 days**: days **1 to 20** paid in full; days **21 to 100** daily copay. Ends sooner if maximum recovery is reached.
- **No custodial care.**

### Other Part A benefits
- **Inpatient psychiatric**: **190 days** lifetime max.
- **Home health**: nursing and therapy at home.
- **Hospice**: life expectancy **6 months or less**. Two **90-day** periods, then unlimited **60-day** periods if recertified. Includes family counseling. Patient pays **$5** for outpatient drugs and **5%** for inpatient respite care.

### Part A cost sharing summary

| Service | Medicare pays | Patient pays |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital days **1 to 60** | 100% after deductible | **Deductible** (once per benefit period) |
| Days **61 to 90** | Rest | Daily coinsurance = **25% of the Part A deductible** |
| **60 lifetime reserve days** | Rest | Daily coinsurance = **50% of the deductible** (twice the day 61-90 amount) |
| After reserve days | Nothing | **100%**, no out-of-pocket max |
| SNF days **1 to 20** | 100% | Nothing (see contradiction note) |
| SNF days **21 to 100** | Rest | Daily coinsurance = **12.5% of the Part A deductible** |
| SNF beyond 100 | Nothing | 100% |
| Home health | 100% of approved | Nothing |
| Hospice | 100% with a few exceptions | Limited cost sharing |
| Blood | 100% after first 3 pints | **First 3 pints** (unless replaced by donation) |
| Psychiatric | Same as inpatient, 190-day lifetime max | 100% after 190 days |

### Part A does NOT cover
- Private duty nurse or attendant, private rooms
- **First 3 pints of blood**
- Personal conveniences (phone, TV)
- **Surgeons and anesthesiologists who aren't hospital employees** (Part B covers them)
- Services paid for by a government entity
- Stays after the benefit period and lifetime maximum are used up

## 9. Part B: Medical insurance

- Called **supplemental medical insurance**. Technically **optional**.
- Covers doctors and outpatient care anywhere in the U.S.: lab work, diagnostic tests, supplies, **DME**, X-rays, blood, therapy (occupational, physical, speech), prosthetics, chiropractic (manual adjustments only), home health for people with Part B but not Part A, and **surgeons and anesthesiologists who aren't hospital employees**.
- **100%** of certain preventive care: annual wellness visit, some screenings (cardiac, diabetes), vaccinations.

### Cost sharing
- **Monthly premium** (everyone) + **annual deductible** (2025: $257; 2026 projected $288).
- After the deductible: Medicare pays **80%**, insured pays **20% coinsurance**. **No annual out-of-pocket max.**
- Blood: insured pays the **first 3 pints** (outpatient), then 20%.

| Service | Medicare pays | Patient pays |
|---|---|---|
| Medical expenses (doctor, supplies, surgery, therapy, diagnostics, equipment) | **80%** after deductible | Deductible + **20%** |
| Clinical lab (blood, urine, other tests) | **100%** | **Nothing** |
| Home health (no prior hospital stay) | 80% | 20% |
| Blood | 80% after first 3 pints | First 3 pints + 20% |

### Part B does NOT cover
- Private duty nurse
- **Intermediate or custodial care**, long-term care, unskilled nursing home care
- **Vision, hearing, dental**
- **Outpatient prescription drugs**
- Cosmetic surgery
- **Routine physicals and foot care**
- X-rays and tests **ordered by chiropractors**
- Acupuncture and massage
- Physician charges above Medicare's approved amount
- Services paid for by a government entity

## 10. Part C: Medicare Advantage

- Formerly **Medicare + Choice**. Created by the **Medicare Prescription Drug, Improvement, and Modernization Act of 2003 (MMA)**.
- Private companies approved by Medicare. Government pays a fixed amount per person, typically **95%** of its average cost.
- **Replaces** Parts A and B, so no Medigap needed.

### Who qualifies
- Must be enrolled in **both Parts A and B**, keep paying the **Part B premium**, and live in the plan's **service area**.
- Course says enrollees must **not have ESRD** (but see SNPs below and section 15).
- **Illegal** to sell Medigap to someone with MA, and vice versa.
- Enrolling in MA **automatically ends a stand-alone Part D** plan.
- Only **one MA plan** at a time.
- Can use **HSAs**. Coordination of benefits may apply with a group plan at or after 65.

### Plan types

| Type | How it works |
|---|---|
| **HMO** | Network, primary care physician manages care and gives referrals. Fixed monthly fees and copays. Out of network = **emergencies only**. |
| **PPO** | Network with discounted fees. **No referrals** generally. **Some out-of-network** coverage. |
| **PFFS** (Private Fee-for-Service) | See **any Medicare-approved provider** that accepts the plan's payment. Insurer sets what it pays and what you owe. |
| **SNP** (Special Needs Plan) | For people with **both Medicare and Medicaid**, nursing home residents, or specific chronic conditions (e.g., **ESRD**). |

- MA plans must cover everything Original Medicare covers, but can structure costs differently and add extras (dental, vision, hearing).

## 11. Part D: Prescription drug plans

- Created by the **MMA of 2003**. Covers **outpatient** prescription drugs.
- Stand-alone PDPs require enrollment in **Part A, Part B, or both**.
- Also eligible: under 65 on SSDI for **2+ years**, kidney dialysis or transplant need, **ALS**.
- Enrollment is **voluntary**, but late enrollment = **1% per month** lifetime penalty for each month without **creditable coverage**.
- IEP is the same **7 months** as Part B. Later changes in the **Oct 15 to Dec 7** window, or an SEP.
- **SEP triggers**: moving out of the service area, losing other creditable coverage, qualifying for **Extra Help**, joining a **5-Star plan**, plan stops serving your area or changes its formulary.

### Formulary
- Must cover **at least 2 drugs in each class**.
- Must cover **all** drugs in the **6 protected classes**.
- Can change the formulary **after the first 60 days** of the plan year. Can drop a drug only for limited reasons: safety concern, FDA guidance change, temporary shortage, or a generic is available.
- **Tiers**: 1 = generic, 2 = preferred, 3 = non-preferred, 4 = specialty.

### Cost sharing
- **Two layers**: basic and catastrophic.
- After the deductible (if any), insured pays **up to 25%**; plan and drug makers pay **75%**.
- After the out-of-pocket limit: **catastrophic** coverage, insured pays **nothing**.
- New: option to pay out-of-pocket drug costs in **monthly installments** (maximum monthly cap).
- Government sets maximum deductibles and out-of-pocket limits, which change yearly.

### Parts A to D at a glance

| | Part A | Part B | Part C | Part D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name | Hospital insurance | Medical (supplemental medical) insurance | Medicare Advantage | Prescription Drug Plan |
| Run by | Federal (CMS via MACs) | Federal (CMS via MACs) | Private insurers | Private insurers |
| Covers | Inpatient hospital, SNF, home health, hospice | Doctors, outpatient, lab, DME, preventive | Everything in A and B, often drugs and extras | Outpatient drugs |
| Premium | Most pay **none** | **Everyone** pays monthly | Part B premium + maybe plan premium | Varies by plan |
| Cost sharing | Deductible per benefit period, coinsurance after day 60 | Annual deductible, then 20% | Plan copays, annual OOP max | Deductible, up to 25%, catastrophic |
| Late penalty | 10% for twice the years delayed | **10% per 12 months, lifetime** | — | **1% per month, lifetime** |

## 12. Medicare Supplements (Medigap)

- Sold by private insurers to fill Original Medicare's gaps (deductibles, coinsurance, more hospital days).
- **Only for Original Medicare** (A and B). Not for MA.
- **NAIC** was authorized by the federal government to create standardized forms. Only those forms may be called a "Medicare supplement."
- **OBRA 1990** (Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act) required minimum standards.
- Can't duplicate Medicare benefits. **No extras like dental.** **No networks**, coverage is **nationwide**.
- **Only one** Medigap policy per person.
- Benefits change every year with Medicare. Insurers give **at least 30 days' notice** of changes.

### Enrollment
- **Medigap open enrollment**: **6 months** starting the month you're both **65 and enrolled in Part B**. **Guaranteed issue**, no evidence of insurability.
- After that, insurers may underwrite, rate up, or decline.
- **6-month pre-existing condition exclusion** with a **6-month look-back**.

### Standardized plans
- Plans **A through N**. **E, H, I, J** are no longer sold (grandfathered). **10 plans** currently available.
- Since **January 1, 2020**, plans sold to newly eligible people **can't cover the Part B deductible**. So **Plans C and F** are only for people eligible before 2020. **D and G** replace **C and F** for new enrollees (same benefits minus the Part B deductible).
- **K and L**: fully cover only the Part A daily coinsurance at first, partially cover the rest, then **100%** once the annual out-of-pocket limit is hit.
- **F and G**: high-deductible option in some states.
- **N**: covers Part B coinsurance with copays for some office visits (review notes).

### Core benefits (Plan A, required in every plan)
1. Part A **coinsurance** for days 61-90 and reserve days, **plus 365 extra lifetime hospital days** after Medicare runs out
2. Part B **20% coinsurance** (not amounts above what Medicare approves)
3. **First 3 pints of blood**
4. Part A **hospice** coinsurance or copays

### Contract requirements (NAIC Model Act)
- **30-day free look**
- Exclusions can't be more restrictive than Medicare
- Named pre-existing conditions **can't be excluded** in underwritten policies
- Guaranteed issue during open enrollment or a change in Medigap coverage
- Up to **6-month pre-existing exclusion**, **6-month look-back**
- Benefits change automatically with Medicare
- **Guaranteed renewable for life** (cancel only for nonpayment)
- **No new probationary period** when replacing a similar Medigap policy
- Minimum loss ratios: **65% individual**, **75% group**
- Premium methods: **issue age** (age at purchase), **attained age** (rises each year with age), **community rating** (same for everyone in the area)

### Marketing rules
- Policies filed with the state before marketing. **Ads filed at least 30 days before use.**
- Give the **Buyer's Guide** at application.
- On replacement, agent and applicant both sign a **Notice of Replacement**.
- **Prohibited**: duplication of coverage, high-pressure tactics, cold lead advertising, twisting.
- **Commissions**: first-year commission **can't exceed 200%** of the second year's. The year-two amount must continue for **5 years**. Replacement sales earn only the renewal rate. Example: **$220** first year → at least **$110** in year 2, and $110 each year in years 3 to 6.

### Medicare Select
- A **Medigap** policy (not MA) that works like a **closed-end HMO**. Not available everywhere.
- Use insurer-designated providers for a **lower premium**. Network only except emergencies; PCP referral needed.
- Benefits match the standard Medigap plans.
- Examples: provider groups, hospital marketing plans, insurer or TPA groups.

## 13. Long-term care insurance

### Basics
- Pays for medical and personal services for people who need help with **ADLs** for an extended time, due to **cognitive impairment** or **physical loss of function** (injury, stroke, Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, aging).
- Covers **at least 12 consecutive months** of care **outside a hospital**.
- Covers **chronic**, not acute, conditions.
- Costs: nursing home median **over $100,000/year**; assisted living approaching **$75,000**.
- Medicare and Medigap give **minimal** LTC protection (they focus on recovery).
- Can be bought as an individual policy, group plan, or life insurance rider.

### Three levels of care

| Level | What it is | Who gives it | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Skilled nursing** | Nursing and rehab on a doctor's orders | Skilled medical personnel (or supervised by them) | **Daily, 24 hours** |
| **Intermediate** | Nursing and rehab on a doctor's orders | Skilled medical personnel | **Intermittent / occasional** |
| **Custodial** | Personal needs (bathing, dressing, walking, eating, medication) | Trained, usually **non-medical** people; ordered by a healthcare professional | As needed |

### Care settings

| Setting | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Nursing home** | 24-hour skilled care; significant loss of bodily function (incontinence); end-of-life or recovery; least freedom |
| **Assisted living facility** | Less than a nursing home; no 24-hour care; intermittent nursing, meds, meals, housekeeping, social. **RCFEs** are nonmedical. Help with **1 or 2 ADLs** |
| **Home health care** | **Skilled** care at home, part-time (visiting nurses, wound care, therapy) |
| **Home care** | **Personal** care at home (ADLs, meals, housework, transportation) |
| **Adult day care** | Daytime care while family is at work; transportation usually provided |
| **Continuing care community** | Independent living → assisted living → onsite nursing home |
| **Respite care** | Temporary relief for the unpaid caregiver |

### Types of LTC contracts
- **Stand-alone** LTC policy (the exam focuses on this)
- **Life insurance riders**: LTC rider uses part of the face amount for a monthly benefit, same triggers as stand-alone, often extra underwriting. (Accelerated death benefit riders are **not** LTC under NAIC.)
- **Hybrid (linked)** plans: whole life or annuity + LTC; get LTC benefits or, if not needed, insurance benefits

### NAIC LTC Model Act minimum standards (all 50 states adopted, fully or partly)
- Stand-alone must pay for **at least 12 months**
- **Free look** with full refund
- **Guaranteed renewable or noncancellable**
- **No prior hospital stay** required
- **No facility stay required** before home care benefits
- Skilled care benefits **can't be substantially greater** than intermediate or custodial
- Pre-existing exclusion max **6 months**, **6-month look-back**
- **No impairment riders**
- Excludes: lump-sum accelerated death benefit policies, policies requiring prior hospitalization, Medicare supplements, and policies requiring a particular diagnosis
- Insurer must give an **outline of coverage**. **Producers** are principally responsible for **suitability**.

### Benefit triggers
- **Loss of physical function**: can't do **2 or more of 6 ADLs** without **substantial assistance** (hands-on or stand-by).
- **Cognitive impairment**: memory, orientation, reasoning, judgment. Covers organic diseases of aging (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, non-Alzheimer's senile dementia), plus stroke or brain injury.

### Policy features
- **Elimination period**: like a deductible; often only **once** per policy.
- **Waiver of premium**: premiums stop while receiving benefits. Some policies make it an optional rider with a **90-day** wait.
- **Exclusions**: war, drug or alcohol treatment, intentional self-injury, attempted suicide, nervous disorders, mental illness (unless organic), care outside the U.S., care covered by workers' comp.

### Benefits

| Method | Example ($100/day policy, $88 of costs) |
|---|---|
| **Fixed daily indemnity** | Pays **$100** |
| **Reimbursement** (actual costs) | Pays **$88** |

- Higher daily limit = higher premium.
- Reimbursement policies may cap **home care at 50% to 80%** of the nursing home max. Example: $200/day max → home care $100 to $160/day.
- **Benefit periods**: 12 months, 2, 3, 5, 10 years, or life. Min **1 year**. **3 years** is common (average nursing home stay is about **2.5 years**).
- **Pool of money**: $200/day × 730 days (2 years) = **$146,000**. Using only home care at $125/day, it lasts **3.2 years** (1,168 days).

### Optional provisions

| Provision | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Third-party notification of lapse** | Named person also gets lapse notices. Declining usually requires a **signed waiver**. |
| **Return of premium** | Refunds all or part of premiums if the insured dies without using benefits. Built into hybrids. |
| **Nonforfeiture** | Must be **offered** before issue (no age limit). Forms: **reduced paid-up** (lower daily benefit, same period), **shortened benefit period** (same daily benefit, shorter period), **return of premium** (most expensive; banned in some states). |
| **Contingent nonforfeiture** | Built into **all tax-qualified** policies; triggered by a **significant premium increase**. |
| **Inflation protection** | At **5%** inflation, costs double in about **15 years**. Virtually all states require insurers to **offer** it (at least to applicants under an age such as **76**). Forms: **guaranteed insurability option** (buy more at attained age, no proof) and **automatic inflation rider** (5% compounded, cost in initial premium). |

### Marketing, premiums, underwriting
- Sold to adults **18+**. Stand-alone policies generally stop being issued **after age 79**; hybrids can go into the 80s.
- Insurers must ensure fair comparisons, avoid excessive insurance, and warn that a policy may not cover all LTC costs.
- **Replacement**: new insurer must **waive pre-existing limits**; agent gives a **notice regarding replacement** before the application is accepted.
- Disclosures by application: **outline of coverage**, **NAIC Shopper's Guide to LTC**, **personal worksheet** (finances and suitability).
- Prohibited: high-pressure tactics, cold lead advertising, twisting, **post-claims underwriting**.
- **Premiums** are lower when: younger, shorter benefit period, lower level of care, **longer elimination period**, fewer optional benefits.
- Underwriting is like disability insurance, but DI looks at **ability to earn a living**; LTC looks at **ability to live independently**.

### Association and employer group LTC
- **Association (affinity)**: main job is to **educate** members. Must disclose compensation, how the program was chosen, and any interlocking directorates; board approves policies and compensation. Insurer files the contract, outline of coverage, and ads.
- **Employer group**: often voluntary, employee-paid, simplified underwriting, discounted premiums, can include parents and siblings. Large employers may offer true group LTC with limited **guaranteed issue** coverage.

### Taxes

| | Tax-qualified (HIPAA) | Non-qualified |
|---|---|---|
| Premiums | **Deductible** (if itemizing; limits vary by age). Self-employed: fully deductible from 1099 income | **Not deductible** |
| Benefits | **Tax-free**, even above actual costs | **Taxable** if they exceed actual costs (risk with indemnity policies) |
| Employer-paid group premiums | Deductible to employer, not taxable to employee | — |

- LTC expenses (including qualified premiums) are medical expenses, deductible above **7.5% of AGI**.
- **HIPAA requirements for tax-qualified**: can't pay Medicare-reimbursable expenses; trigger = can't do **2 of 6 ADLs** or **severe cognitive impairment**; at least **guaranteed renewable**; **no cash surrender** nonforfeiture option; LTC services only; **30-day free look**; claims based on a diagnosis of disability lasting **at least 90 days**; functional assessment by a gerontology expert and a written plan of care.
- Some policies combine bathing and dressing into one ADL.

### Medicare, Medicaid, and LTC

| Program | Role |
|---|---|
| **Medicare** | Federal medical insurance, 65+ and some disabled. **Acute care and recovery.** SNF up to 100 days. **No custodial care.** |
| **Medicaid** | **Means-tested**, federal + state funded, **state-run**. Main public payer for **LTSS**. Must **spend down** assets (home excepted). **5-year look-back** on asset transfers. |
| **LTC insurance** | **Private** alternative for LTSS. Covers extended care Medicare doesn't. |
| **Partnership Program** | Federally supported, **state-operated**. Protects assets equal to LTC benefits paid. Policies must be **tax-qualified with inflation protection**. |

**Order:** Medicare first for acute care → LTC insurance for extended care → Medicaid as the safety net after assets and LTC benefits run out.

## 14. Every EXAM TIP from the chapter

1. **Group insurance = COBRA group.** If the exam says "group insurance" in this topic, assume a COBRA group of **20 or more** employees.
2. **Know whether there's a premium, not the dollar amounts.** It's more important to know whether there's a monthly premium and what the annual deductible is than exact dollar figures. Exams rarely test numbers that change every year.
3. **Blood.** ALWAYS assume the insured pays for the **first 3 pints** unless the question says donations were made. (Appears in both Part A and Part B sections.)
4. **Part D structure.** Exams don't test annual values, but will ask about the basic structure: deductibles, **more than one coverage level**, constant percentages, and the **monthly installment plan**.
5. **PLANS vs PARTS.** "Plans" = Medicare supplement policies from private insurers. "Parts" = sections of the government Medicare program. Read carefully.
6. **Home health care vs home care.** Some states use "home health care" to include "home care." Check the state-specific chapter.

**Review-notes exam tips:** know the differences between Parts A, B, C, D; enrollment periods and late penalties; Medicare cost sharing and benefit periods; Medigap core benefits; the three levels of LTC; LTC benefit triggers; qualified vs non-qualified LTC taxes; how the Partnership Program works.

## 16. Memory tricks (made up by Claude, not from the course)

- **"A is for Admitted, B is for Bills from doctors, C is for Combo, D is for Drugs."**
- **Parts vs Plans:** *Parts* are from the *Public* program; *Plans* come from *Private* companies.
- **Part B penalty "10 per 12":** 10% for every 12 months late. **Part D "1 per 1":** 1% for every 1 month.
- **IEP = 3 + 1 + 3 = 7.** Three months before, your birthday month, three months after.
- **GEP and MA OEP share a season:** "New Year's quarter" (Jan 1 to Mar 31).
- **Fall shopping season:** Oct 15 to Dec 7, new coverage on New Year's Day.
- **Benefit period "60 in, 60 out":** 60 days with no coinsurance, and a new period after 60 days out of the hospital.
- **Part A coinsurance ladder:** days 61-90 = ¼ of the deductible; reserve days = ½; SNF days 21-100 = ⅛.
- **SNF "3-20-100":** 3 days in the hospital first, 20 days free, 100 days max.
- **Under 65: "24-3-0":** SSDI waits 24 months, ESRD waits 3 months, ALS waits 0.
- **ESRD 30 months:** "Thirty months, the job plan pays first."
- **Medigap core "Hospital, Hospice, Blood, and B":** Part A hospital coinsurance + 365 days, hospice, 3 pints of blood, Part B coinsurance.
- **"C and F Covered the B deductible; D and G Don't Go there."**
- **"F and G Get the Excess."** Only F and G cover Part B excess charges.
- **K and L:** "K is Kinda half (50%), L is a Little more (75%)."
- **Loss ratios "65 alone, 75 together":** individual 65%, group 75%.
- **Levels of care "Skilled = Steady (24 hours), Intermediate = In and out, Custodial = Care for the body."**
- **ADLs "BD TTCE":** "**B**ig **D**ogs **T**oss **T**ennis balls **C**onstantly **E**veryday" (Bathing, Dressing, Toileting, Transferring, Continence, Eating).
- **Cognitive "MORJ":** Memory, Orientation, Reasoning, Judgment.
- **Indemnity vs reimbursement:** Indemnity pays the *Indicated* amount; Reimbursement pays what you *Really* spent.
- **Tax-qualified LTC = "Deduct in, tax-free out."**

### Notes on conflicting course content

The course says different things in a few places. Both versions are listed so you recognize either on the exam.

- **Part D and Medicare Advantage.** Section 3.4 says MA members are **ineligible** for Part D and that enrolling in Part D **ends** an MA plan. But the overview, section 2.4.4, and the review notes say Part D can be **included in** MA plans and MA members can choose a plan with Part D for extra premium. The way they fit together: drug coverage comes either as a stand-alone PDP (with Original Medicare) or built into an MA plan. You can't hold a stand-alone PDP and an MA plan at the same time.
- **ESRD and Medicare Advantage.** Section 3.3.1 says MA enrollees must **not** have ESRD, but section 3.3.2.4 says Special Needs Plans serve people with chronic conditions **such as ESRD**.
- **SNF deductible.** The text says the first 20 SNF days are fully covered with **no deductible**. The Part A summary table says "100% after the deductible / patient pays deductible."
- **Medigap Part A deductible and SNF coinsurance.** The text (and review notes) say every plan except A covers the Part A deductible and every plan except A and B covers SNF coinsurance. The chart shows **K (50%), L (75%), and M (50% for the Part A deductible)** cover only part.
- **Medigap look-back period.** Section 4.1 says the 6-month look-back "runs from the policy's effective date." Section 4.3 says it is the 6 months **immediately preceding** the effective date.
- **Pool of money math.** Section 5.6.2 says 365 × $100 = **$35,600**. The correct product is **$36,500**. The pool-of-money concept still holds.
- **Plan N.** The 2025 chart in the screenshot ends at Plan M, but the review notes list Plan N (foreign travel, Part B coinsurance with copays).
- **Automatic inflation rider.** The keyword page says it raises the **face amount** about 5% a year. The section says it raises the **stated daily benefit**, compounded annually.
- **PFFS and networks.** The review notes list PFFS among MA "provider networks," but section 3.3.2.3 says PFFS lets you see **any** Medicare-approved provider that accepts its payment.
- **Part A late penalty.** Section 2.4.1 says only that the penalty depends on the length of delay. The review notes give the rule: **10% for twice the number of years delayed**.
