## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning (course wording, condensed) |
|---|---|
| **Accidental means** | Both the **cause and the result** of the mishap must be accidental. Course example: Pedestrian A is accidentally bumped into the street by Pedestrian B and hit by a car. The cause and the result were both unintended, so it's covered. |
| **Accidental results (accidental bodily injury)** | Only the **result** has to be unexpected and unintended, even if the action was voluntary. **Far less restrictive** than accidental means. |
| **Additional Monthly Benefit (AMB) rider** | Short-term extra benefit for the **first 6 to 12 months** of disability. It can add to employer benefits, pay early expenses, or fill the **5-month SSDI waiting period**. |
| **Any occupation** | Disabled if you can't do your own job **or any job you're reasonably suited for by education, training, or experience**. |
| **Any occupation for which the insured is reasonably suited** | In states that use this phrase, it means the same as "any occupation." In states that don't, "any occupation" is read more literally, so it's **stricter**. |
| **Benefit period** | Maximum time benefits are paid **per disability claim**. Longer benefit period = **higher premium**. |
| **Business Overhead Expense (BOE)** | Reimburses the business for expenses and payroll if the owner is disabled. Does **not** replace the owner's income. |
| **Capital sum** | AD&D benefit for **dismemberment**. Typically **50% of the principal sum**. |
| **Principal sum** | AD&D benefit for **accidental death**. Equals the **face amount**. |
| **Cash surrender value rider** | Returns all premiums at **age 65**, less any benefits received. (Keyword page says "if no claims have been made.") |
| **Change of occupation provision** | Switch to a **more hazardous** job: **benefit** is reduced. Switch to a **less hazardous** job: **premium** is reduced and excess unearned premium is refunded. |
| **Concurrent disability** | Several events cause the same disability, or one loss fits several definitions. **Only one benefit** is paid. |
| **Confined disability** | Insured must **stay indoors** (at home or in a hospital). Non-confined means they don't have to (e.g., doctor says to take walks). |
| **Coordination of benefits (COB)** | Sets the order insurers pay. The primary pays as if it were the only carrier; the "contingent" coverage pays the remaining balance. **Group** disability benefits are reduced by Social Security and workers' comp. |
| **COLA rider** | Increases the benefit with the **CPI** to fight inflation, adjusted on each disability anniversary. Starts once a claim has been paid for **more than one year**. **Only found on LTD policies.** |
| **Credit disability** | Pays loan installments if the borrower is disabled. Pays the **creditor**, not the borrower. A form of **decreasing term disability**. |
| **Decreasing term disability policy** | Covers a fixed period from issue, and the number of possible payments shrinks over time. Example: 5-year loan, disabled at end of year 1 = up to 4 years of payments; at end of year 4 = 1 year at most. |
| **Delayed disability** | A total disability that shows up **later** after an accident. Policies allow a set window (20, 30, 60, 90 days, etc.). |
| **Disability buyout plan / policy** | Buy-sell agreement funded by disability insurance, used to buy out a disabled owner's share. |
| **Disability income rider** (on life insurance) | Pays **1% of the face amount** (up to $1,000) per month if the insured is totally disabled. |
| **Elective indemnity option** | Optional **lump sum** instead of monthly payments for injuries named in the contract. Chosen when applying. Also used in buyout policies. |
| **Elimination (waiting) period** | Time from the start of disability until benefits are payable. It's a **time deductible**. Longer elimination period = **lower premium**. |
| **FICA taxes** | Payroll taxes paid by employees and employers that fund Social Security. |
| **Flat amount approach** | Benefit is a **fixed dollar amount**. Used mainly in **individual** policies. |
| **Percentage of earnings approach** | Benefit is a **percentage of income** that adjusts as income changes. Used mainly in **group** policies. |
| **Fully insured** | SSA status for retirement and disability benefits. **40 quarters (10 years)** of FICA taxes = **permanent** fully insured. Younger workers need fewer quarters (sliding scale). |
| **Guaranteed insurability rider** (FIO, APO, GPO) | Right to buy more coverage at set future dates **without proof of good health**, as long as income has gone up. "**Use it or lose it**." Also called future increase option, additional purchase option, guaranteed purchase option. |
| **Guaranteed renewable** | Insurer must renew to the termination age if premiums are paid. Can't change policy terms but **can raise premiums by class**. |
| **Non-cancelable (non-cancelable and guaranteed renewable)** | Insurer must renew **and** can't change the terms **or** the premium. |
| **Hospital confinement rider** | Extra benefit for each day in the hospital, on top of the monthly benefit. |
| **Impairment rider** (exclusion rider, waiver for impairments) | **Permanently excludes** certain losses so the insurer can still issue the policy, sometimes at a standard premium. |
| **Income replacement contract** | Defines disability purely as a **loss of income**. |
| **Key employee (key person) policy** | Pays the **employer** if it loses a key employee's services. |
| **Lifetime extension rider** | Extends the benefit period **beyond age 65**. |
| **Long-term disability (LTD)** | **Monthly** payments, benefit period of **2 years or more**. |
| **Short-term disability (STD)** | **Weekly** payments, benefit period of **no more than 2 years**. Group STD usually **26 weeks max**, integrated with sick time and LTD. |
| **Loss of earnings test** | Requires an actual loss of income. If you can still earn as much as before, there's no loss and no claim. |
| **Material duties** | The actual tasks you do on the job. |
| **Substantial duties** | Your essential capabilities (also called "key duties"). |
| **Morbidity** | The risk (likelihood) of becoming disabled. Considers personal and occupational factors. |
| **Natural group** | A group formed for a legitimate purpose **other than buying group insurance**. |
| **Non-disabling injury (medical reimbursement) benefit** | Pays medical costs up to a set amount for an injury that isn't disabling, if no other claim is filed. Requires proof of treatment. |
| **Occupational coverage** | Covers job-related **and** non-job-related disabilities: **24 hours a day, 7 days a week**. |
| **Non-occupational coverage** | Does **not** cover disabilities from the insured's employment (workers' comp handles those). |
| **Own occupation** | Disabled if you can't do the material and substantial duties of **your own** job. |
| **Partial disability** | Can't do **one or more (but not all)** key duties, or can't work your job **full-time**. Covered if income drops. |
| **Residual disability** | Proportional benefit when working less than full-time with an income loss of **at least 20%**. |
| **Permanent disability** (workers' comp) | Condition considered permanent (dismemberment, loss of sight, degenerative or chronic). Lump sum or weekly income. |
| **Temporary disability** (workers' comp) | Condition expected to be temporary unless re-evaluated at the end of the state's temporary benefit period. |
| **Presumptive disability** | Automatic total disability for **loss of 2 or more limbs**, or **permanent loss of sight, hearing, or speech**. |
| **Primary Insurance Amount (PIA)** | Basic Social Security monthly benefit, based on income and the taxes paid on it. **SSDI = 100% of PIA.** |
| **Probationary period** | One-time period at the **start** of the policy when **only accidents** are covered, not illness. Keyword page: **7 to 30 days**. Section 5.1.1: ends after **15, 30, or 60 days**. |
| **Pro rata** | Insurers share a claim in proportion to their share of the coverage. 60% of the coverage = pays 60% of the claim. |
| **Recurrent disability provision** | A relapse within the set period (**6 months**) is a continuation of the first claim: no new elimination period, no new benefit period. |
| **Rehabilitation benefit** | Pays the approved cost of vocational training for a new occupation. |
| **Relation of earnings to insurance** | With multiple policies, total benefits **can't exceed income lost**. Each policy is reduced **pro rata**. Used by **individual** policies (group uses COB). |
| **Return of premium rider** | Refunds premiums if claims experience is favorable. Keyword page: dollar for dollar, **without interest**, if no claim is filed. Section 5.5.8: a percentage of premiums, at age 65 or every 5 or 10 years. |
| **Social Security rider** (Social Insurance Supplement / Substitute, SIS) | Pays a monthly benefit when Social Security (or workers' comp) is **delayed, denied, or less than the rider amount**. |
| **Substantial gainful activity (SGA)** | The Social Security definition of disability. Replaced the older term **"any gainful work."** |
| **Waiver of premium** | Premiums are waived while the insured is disabled and receiving benefits (after the waiting period). **Not a loan.** |
| **Work in the national economy** | SSA's final test for SSDI: jobs exist in significant numbers, even outside your area of expertise or location, whether or not you'd be hired. |
| **Workers' compensation** | A form of **liability insurance** for workers hurt by work-related occurrences. |

## 3. Basic concepts

### Purpose
Disability income insurance replaces income lost when a covered peril (accident or sickness) keeps you from working. It pays a specified income benefit for a defined period.

**Business uses** (from section 2.1):
- Keeps the company going when a principal owner is disabled (covers expenses)
- Lets remaining owners buy out a disabled partner
- Provides an employee benefit through group insurance
- Covers non-occupational disabilities alongside workers' comp

### Covered perils
- Accident (accidental bodily injury) or illness
- Illness coverage can be limited by a rider or a pre-existing condition limitation
- If an accident **and** illness both contribute ("combined accident and sickness disability"), the insured still **collects only once**

### Occupational vs non-occupational

| | Occupational | Non-occupational |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | On and off the job, **24/7** | Off the job only; no benefit if workers' comp pays |
| Typical example | **Individual** DI policies | **Group** DI policies |
| Coordination with workers' comp | **Not** coordinated. Pays full benefit unless the total would exceed lost income | Coordinated. Workers' comp reduces the group benefit; group pays only the **excess** |
| Underwriting | Partly based on the insured's occupation | Not underwritten on employment |

### Short-term vs long-term disability

| | STD | LTD |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Quickly replace income during a temporary, less serious injury or illness | Longer needs from more serious injury or illness |
| Payments | **Weekly** | **Monthly** |
| Waiting period | **Minimal** (group STD: 7, 10, or 14 days) | **Longer** (group LTD: 180 days) |
| Benefit period | Often up to **180 days**; **2 years max**. Group STD: **13 weeks** (where allowed) or **26 weeks** | **2 years to age 65** (sometimes retirement age or lifetime) |
| Other | Usually offered with LTD; often covers childbirth and medically necessary surgery | Costs more; a longer elimination period lowers the cost |
| Group benefit amount | 50% to 70% of pre-disability income | 50% to 70% of income |

LTD benefit period length depends on: group vs individual (group tends to be longer), the risk of the occupation, and personal traits (age, gender, medical history) for individual plans.

## 4. Qualifying for benefits

To collect, the insured must:
- Be **under a physician's care**
- Meet the policy's **definition of disability**
- Satisfy the **elimination period**
- Have a **loss of income** (presumed or actual)

Disability insurance is meant to **restore** your economic position, **not enhance** it.

### Material vs substantial duties
Policies usually define disability as the inability to perform the **material and substantial duties** of an occupation.
- **Material duties** = the actual tasks
- **Substantial duties** = the essential capabilities ("key duties")

*Course example:* A surgeon with Parkinson's can still hold a scalpel (material duty) but can't keep their hand steady enough to cut safely (substantial duty). Since they can't do both, they can't do either.

### Own occupation vs any occupation vs SGA

| | Own occupation | Any occupation | Substantial gainful activity (SGA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Used by | Commercial policies | Commercial policies | **Social Security (SSDI)** |
| Disabled if you can't… | Do the material and substantial duties of **your own** job | Do your own job **or any job you're reasonably suited for by education, training, or experience** | Engage in **any** substantial gainful activity due to a medically determinable physical or mental condition |
| Strictness | **Least** restrictive, most favorable to the insured | More restrictive | **Most** restrictive |
| Cost | **Most expensive**; may be reserved for certain professions | Lower | N/A (government) |
| Extra condition | Often own-occ for the **first 2 years**, then switches to any-occ | — | Condition must be expected to result in **death** or last (or have lasted) **at least 12 continuous months** |

*Dr. Smith example:* A neurosurgeon hurts a hand and can't do delicate brain surgery.
- **Own occupation:** disabled, even if they could teach or consult.
- **Any occupation:** disabled only if they can't do any job they're reasonably qualified for. If they can teach medical students, benefits might not be paid.

**SGA work includes** work that is: done for pay or profit, generally done for pay or profit, intended for profit (whether or not a profit is made), and **part-time** work. The keyword for SGA is **"gainful,"** which is **not used** in commercial definitions. SGA is also described as "any gainful work" or "gainful employment."

### Loss of earnings
- Now in most policies; originally most common in **group** plans
- Useful when a disability reduces earnings but isn't totally disabling
- Looks at **earned (active) income**: wages, salary, commissions, fees for services
- **Not** passive income: rental income, interest on savings, investment dividends
- **Pure loss of income:** earned income must usually drop by **at least 20%**. A policy that defines disability purely as income loss is an **income replacement contract**.

### Recurrent disability
- A relapse of the **same** disability within **6 months** after returning to work = continuation of the first claim
  - **No new elimination period** (benefits start right away)
  - **No new benefit period** (it counts against the original one)
- Relapse **after 6 months** = a **new claim** with a new elimination period and new benefit period
- *Example:* 5-year benefit period, disabled 6 months, returns to work, relapses: **4½ years** remain.

### Presumptive disability
- A **medical** definition. Qualifying losses: **double dismemberment**, total and permanent loss of **sight**, **hearing**, or **speech**
- Benefit usually equals the policy's **total disability benefit**
- Insurer **waives** the other criteria (like being unable to do your job) **and any remaining waiting period**; payments start immediately
- **Benefits don't stop** even if the insured can work later. Disability is defined by the medical condition, not the financial loss.

### Other definitions
- **Concurrent:** multiple causes, one period of disability = **one benefit**
- **Delayed:** total disability that develops after an accident; allowed window of 20, 30, 60, 90 days, etc.
- **Confining vs non-confining:** confined = must stay indoors

### At-work benefits: partial vs residual

Insurers created at-work benefits to encourage people to return to work.

| | Partial disability | Residual disability |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Can't do **one or more key duties** of own occupation, **or** can't work full-time | Working **less than full-time** with an income loss of **at least 20%** |
| Type | Functional definition | Proportional to income lost |
| Benefit | Exam standard: **50% of the total disability benefit** | **% of income lost × total disability benefit** |
| Notes | Paid for illness or accident; can be independent of total disability; lets insureds work part-time without losing all benefits | The amount of income lost defines the degree of disability |

### Credit disability
- Pays the monthly loan installment if the insured is disabled
- Also called a **decreasing term disability policy**
- Benefit decreases as the debt is paid down; benefit period lasts until the debt is paid off, then the policy ends

### Disability benefits in life insurance
- **Waiver of premium rider:** waives premium if disabled. **Optional in life** insurance, **standard in health** insurance.
- **Disability income rider:** small benefit, such as **1% of face amount (up to $1,000)**, if totally disabled.

## 5. Worked math examples (from the course)

### Bob: recurrent disability
Bob's policy pays **$1,000/month** with a **30-day** waiting period.
- Out of work **5 months**, back to work **3 months**, then relapses for **4 months**.
- First disability: 5 months − 1 month waiting = **4 months** paid
- Relapse within 6 months = no new waiting period = **4 months** paid
- **Total: 8 months** of benefits

If Bob had been back at work **7 months** (more than 6), the relapse is a new claim with a new waiting period: 4 + (4 − 1) = **7 months**.

### Partial disability: 50% of $2,400
- Earns **$4,000/month**, total disability benefit = **60%** of income = **$2,400**
- Partial benefit = 50% × $2,400 = **$1,200/month**

### Frank: residual disability
Frank earns **$5,000/month**; total disability benefit = **70%** = **$3,500**. A **60%** income loss = 60% residual rating = 60% × $3,500 = **$2,100** benefit, plus he still earns 40% = **$2,000**.

| Pre-disability wage | % earned | Wages earned | % disabled | Disability benefit | Cash flow (wages + benefit) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $5,000 | 0% | $0 | 100% | $3,500 | **$3,500** |
| $5,000 | 20% | $1,000 | 80% | $2,800 | **$3,800** |
| $5,000 | 40% | $2,000 | 60% | $2,100 | **$4,100** |
| $5,000 | 60% | $3,000 | 40% | $1,400 | **$4,400** |
| $5,000 | 80% | $4,000 | 20% | $700 | **$4,700** |

Notice he always comes out ahead by working more.

### George: elimination period (loss settlement)
- **$800/month**, **30-day** elimination period, disabled **90 days**
- Paid for 60 days (2 months) = **$1,600**

### Benefit limit
- Gross earned income **$4,000/month**. Insurer allows up to **60%** = **$2,400/month**

### Percent of earnings vs flat amount
- **Percent of earnings (group):** 60% of $3,000 = $1,800. Income rises to $3,200 → benefit rises to **$1,920**.
- **Flat amount (individual):** Barry earns $3,000; policy states **$1,800** (not "60%"). Income rises to $3,300 → benefit **stays $1,800** unless Barry buys more or adds riders.

### Dr. Jane Dough: business overhead expense
BOE maximum **$4,500/month**. Pays actual expenses up to the limit. (If expenses are $3,950 it pays $3,950; if $4,700 it pays $4,500.)

| Month | Maximum | Overhead costs | Benefit paid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $4,500 | $5,000 | $4,500 |
| 2 | $4,500 | $4,600 | $4,500 |
| 3 | $4,500 | $4,250 | $4,250 |
| 4 | $4,500 | $3,950 | $3,950 |
| **Total** | **$18,000** | **$17,800** | **$17,200** |

### Cross-purchase buy-out: number of policies
**Total policies = N × (N − 1).** 3 partners → 3 × 2 = **6 policies**. (Course diagram: $300,000 partnership, each policy $50,000.)

### Joe: group taxation with shared premiums
- Joe earns **$2,000/month**. Employer pays for a 50% benefit = **$1,000**.
- Joe buys an enhanced benefit of 20% of income = **$400**, with **after-tax** dollars. Total benefit = **$1,400**.
- **$1,000 is taxable** (employer-paid, deducted as a business expense). **$400 is tax-free** (Joe's after-tax money).

### Workers' comp + SSDI tax
If combined SSDI and workers' comp trigger a **$200** monthly SSDI reduction, then **$200** of monthly workers' comp becomes taxable.

### Residual quick example (review keywords)
20% income loss → residual benefit = **20% of the total disability benefit**.

## 6. Government (social) disability insurance

### Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)
- Paid through the Social Security **OASDI** program, funded by payroll taxes from employees, employers, and the self-employed
- Must be **fully insured**: **40 quarters (10 years)** of FICA = permanent fully insured. "Credits" can mean quarters. Younger workers need fewer (sliding scale by age).
- Definition: can't engage in **SGA** due to a medically determinable physical or mental disability expected to result in **death** or that has lasted or is expected to last **12+ continuous months**
- Work is "substantial" if it involves significant physical or mental activity for pay or profit (even if no profit results), **full-time or part-time**
- **Waiting period: 5 months.** Benefits accrue during the **6th month** and are paid at the end of the month.
- Benefits continue until the insured is eligible for **retirement** benefits. **Dependents** may also receive benefits.
- **Benefit = 100% of PIA.** SSDI is **coordinated with (reduced by) workers' comp.**

### Workers' compensation
- **Liability insurance** giving **medical, disability, and survivor** benefits for **work-related accidents or occupational diseases**
- Disability benefit based on wages up to a **state maximum**; states require employers to provide it for most employees
- Pays **total and partial** benefits; classifies disabilities as **temporary** or **permanent** (permanent often from dismemberment, loss of sight, degenerative conditions)
- **Primary coverage for work-related disability.** It coordinates with (reduces) other social insurance and **group** disability to avoid overinsurance.

### Private insurance advantages (review notes)
More flexible definitions, shorter elimination periods, higher benefits, customizable with riders, covers both occupational and non-occupational disabilities.

## 7. Individual disability income insurance

### Basics
- **Occupational** contract: underwritten on occupation and personal characteristics; **24/7** coverage; **monthly** payments
- Insurers limit coverage to a % of income because it replaces **net (take-home)** income and individual policies **don't coordinate** with Social Security or workers' comp
- Total benefits **can't exceed pre-disability earnings**. Limits discourage **malingering** and false claims.
- Basic structure: **benefit period + benefit amount + elimination period**, plus occupation and personal traits = premium. Optional benefits add cost. May have a one-time **probationary period** (accidents only).

### Probationary period vs elimination period

| | Probationary period | Elimination (waiting) period |
|---|---|---|
| When | **Once**, at policy inception | **Each** disability, right after onset |
| What it does | **Illness** not covered until it ends; accidents are covered | No benefits paid for any disability until it ends |
| Length | Section 5.1.1: 15, 30, or 60 days. Keyword page: 7 to 30 days | Individual: **30, 60, 90, 180, 365 days**. STD (mostly group): **7, 10, 14 days** |
| Purpose | Excludes pre-existing (not yet symptomatic) sickness; protects against **adverse selection** | **Time deductible**; longer = **lower premium** |
| Group plans | Usually **none**; instead a minimum length of service (**30 to 90 days**) before eligibility | Uniform; e.g., **7 or 10 days** for STD, **180 days** for LTD |

Probationary periods don't apply to accidents because accidents are fixed in time and space and are unintended by definition.

### Benefit period
- Max time paid per claim; longer = costlier
- For a **substandard risk**, the insurer may **shorten the benefit period** instead of charging more or excluding coverage. Shorter benefit periods are standard for high-risk jobs (construction, manual labor).
- **Individual STD:** a few months up to **2 years**. **Individual LTD:** **2 years up to lifetime** per disability.
- Common choices: **2, 5, or 10 years, to age 65, or to retirement age**; rarely lifetime.

### Benefit amounts
- LTD pays monthly; limit up to **60% of gross monthly earned income** or **80% of net monthly income**
- Two methods: **percent-of-earnings (group)** and **flat amount (individual)**

### Premiums
Based on the monthly benefit plus: length of waiting period, benefit amount, length of benefit period, age/sex/income/health, occupation, and other DI owned.
- Most individual policies: premium **doesn't change with age**
- Employer group or association plans: often **five-year age bands**
- **Annually renewable disability income (ARDI):** starts **lower** than level premium, then rises; above average in later years

### Exclusions
- War (declared or undeclared)
- Intentionally self-inflicted injuries
- Aviation as pilot or crew (unless a **commercial** aircraft or pilot, which is covered)
- Military service
- Illegal occupation

### Standard provisions and benefits
- **Change of occupation:** more hazardous → **benefit** reduced; less hazardous → **premium** reduced
- **Relation of earnings to insurance:** total benefits ≤ pre-disability income; each policy reduced **pro rata** to no more than 100% of the loss. **Individual** policies use this; **group** uses **coordination of benefits**.
- **Impairment rider:** excludes a specific risk (from occupation or prior injury) that's too high or can't be measured, so the policy can still be issued
- **Rehabilitation benefit:** pays approved vocational training costs while the insured stays permanently disabled and active in the program. Returning to work **within one year** is an important benchmark.
- **Medical reimbursement (non-disabling injury) benefit:** pays medical costs up to a limit for an injury when no other claim is filed; requires proof of treatment

### Renewal provisions

| | Non-cancelable (and guaranteed renewable) | Guaranteed renewable |
|---|---|---|
| Must renew if premium paid? | Yes | Yes |
| Can insurer change premium? | **No.** Premium guaranteed at issue | **Yes, by class only** (not one person) when losses exceed what actuaries expected |
| Can insurer change terms? | No | No |
| Who gets it | Preferred, lower-risk occupational classes | More common, especially higher-morbidity occupations |

**Coverage after 65:** usually requires the insured to be **actively working** a stated number of hours per week.

### Underwriting
- Evaluates **morbidity** (likelihood of becoming disabled); protects against **adverse selection**
- Classes: preferred, standard, substandard. Some substandard risks are declined; others get a **rated-up** premium.
- **Age:** older = higher risk. **Sex:** females statistically seek treatment more = higher premium. **Occupation:**
  - Professionals (physicians, attorneys): less hazardous, income far above benefits, more likely to return
  - White- and gray-collar: riskier when incentive to return is lower
  - Blue-collar: highest morbidity; benefit period limited to **2 or 5 years**

### Riders

| Rider | What it does | Key detail |
|---|---|---|
| **AD&D** | Pays for death, permanent loss of limbs, or loss of sight, hearing, or speech from an accident | Principal sum = death (face amount); capital sum = dismemberment (~50%). Available to a limited age (e.g., **70**). Death within **90 days**. Must name a primary beneficiary. Premiums not deductible; benefits not taxable. Also called **multiple indemnity**. |
| **Social Insurance Supplement (SIS)** | Pays when SSDI or workers' comp **denies or doesn't cover** a claim | Lowers insurer risk and the insured's premium; best for **middle-income** people. Must apply for all government benefits. |
| **Additional Monthly Benefit (AMB)** | Extra benefit for the first **6 to 12 months** | Fills the **5-month SSDI** wait |
| **Guaranteed insurability (FIO / APO / GPO)** | Buy more at set dates **without evidence of insurability** | Income must have increased. **Use it or lose it.** Group plans don't have it (benefits adjust automatically). |
| **COLA** | Indexes the benefit to the **CPI** | Increase is the lesser of CPI or a stated % (generally **5%**). Increases benefits **after a claim** (FIO increases coverage before). **LTD only.** |
| **Lifetime extension** | Extends benefits **beyond 65** | Disability must start before 65; insurer may require onset before a set age (e.g., **50**) for full benefits, otherwise reduced until 65 |
| **Hospital confinement** | Extra daily benefit while hospitalized | Indemnity type. **Waives the elimination period** during confinement. Paid from day one for up to **6 or 12 months**. |
| **Return of premium** | Refunds premiums with favorable claims experience | At **65** or every **5 or 10 years** |
| **Cash surrender value** | Returns all premiums at **65** | Less any benefits received |

## 8. Business uses

Two categories: **group** insurance for employees, and **individually underwritten** plans to protect against losing key people.

### Group disability income

**Valid group characteristics:**
- Must be a **natural group**
- Premiums based on **aggregate risk** (industry, occupations, claims experience)
- Employer may offer more than one plan and restrict by **class** (full-time vs part-time, union vs non-union, office vs warehouse/assembly line)
- **Minimum participation** required to avoid adverse selection

**Group features:**
- Total and partial benefits. Military personnel can reinstate coverage after active duty.
- **Group STD:** max **13 weeks** (where allowed by law) or **26 weeks**, paid weekly, **50% to 70%** of income, **own occupation** definition
- **Group LTD:** **2 years or more**, sometimes to retirement, **50% to 70%** of income. Employer may pay a 50% benefit and let employees buy **20%** more themselves.
- LTD starts after STD ends. LTD is often own-occ for the first **1 to 2 years**, then any-occ.
- No probationary period; instead **30 to 90 days** of service before eligibility
- Same benefit periods as individual
- Uniform elimination period. Example package: 2 weeks sick leave, a 6-month STD, then LTD with a **7- or 10-day** STD elimination period and **180-day** LTD elimination period.
- Benefit = **% of wages** (changes with pay). **No guaranteed insurability option.**
- Coordinates with government plans, pensions, SSDI, workers' comp. Supplements workers' comp so total doesn't exceed a set % of earnings. Some group plans cover **only non-occupational** disabilities.

### Individual vs group

| | Individual | Group |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage type | **Occupational** (24/7) | Often **non-occupational** |
| Benefit method | **Flat amount** | **Percentage of earnings** |
| Coordination | Not coordinated; uses **relation of earnings to insurance** | **Coordination of benefits** with SSDI, workers' comp, etc. |
| Probationary period | Yes, at inception | No; uses a 30 to 90 day service requirement |
| Guaranteed insurability | Available as a rider | Not offered (automatic adjustments) |
| Underwriting | Occupation and personal traits | Group's aggregate risk; minimum participation |
| Cost | More customization, higher price | Cost-effective, standardized |

### Business overhead expense (BOE)
- Reimburses **business expenses and payroll** if the owner is disabled. Does **NOT** cover the owner's lost income.
- Sold to professionals in private practice, self-employed owners, partners, and possibly close corporations
- Pays **actual covered expenses up to a monthly maximum** (not a fixed indemnity)
- Covered: rent or mortgage, leased equipment, utilities, employee wages, telephone and internet, cost of a temporary replacement. Everything that continues regardless of the disability.

### Disability buy-out

| | Cross-purchase | Entity |
|---|---|---|
| Who buys the policies | Each **partner/shareholder** buys one on every other owner | The **company** buys one on each owner |
| Beneficiary | The other partners | The company |
| Best for | A **very small** number of partners (gets cumbersome as it grows) | Larger groups |
| Number of policies | **N × (N − 1)** | One per owner |

- Funds a legally binding **disability buy-sell agreement**
- Can pay a **lump sum** (buying an asset, not replacing income); periodic payments allowed
- **Long elimination periods, often up to 2 years**, to be sure the disabled owner won't return
- A disabled partner is a **double liability**: the others pick up the slack **and** keep paying them

### Key person disability
- Pays the **business** a monthly benefit to cover extra help or outside services when an essential person (partner, working stockholder, or manager such as a sales manager) is disabled
- Value = lost business income + cost of hiring and training a replacement
- Lump sum or monthly. Elimination period **30 to 90 days**; benefit period **1 or 2 years**. Business is the **policyholder and premium payor**.

### Business products compared

| | BOE | Buy-out | Key person |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protects | Business operating costs | Remaining owners' ability to buy the disabled owner's share | Business against loss of an essential person |
| Pays | Actual expenses up to monthly max | Lump sum or installments | Monthly or lump sum |
| Elimination period | (not stated) | Often up to **2 years** | **30 to 90 days** |
| Benefit period | (not stated) | Purchase of the share | **1 to 2 years** |

All three are **individual contracts**, and the business is usually the owner, premium payor, and beneficiary.

## 9. Taxation

**Principle:** the IRS taxes the money **only once**: either going in (premiums) or coming out (benefits), not both.

| Situation | Premiums | Benefits |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual DI** | **Not deductible** (after-tax) | **Tax-free** |
| **Group, employer pays** | **Deductible** to employer (business expense) | **Taxable** to employee |
| **Group, employee pays pre-tax** (cafeteria plan) | Not taxed going in | **Taxable** |
| **Group, employee pays after-tax** | Taxed going in | **Tax-free** |
| **Group, shared** | Employer portion deductible | Employer-paid part **taxable**; employee after-tax part **tax-free** (if employee paid pre-tax, the **entire** benefit is taxable) |
| **AD&D** | Not deductible | Not taxable |
| **SSDI** | — | Partly taxable over **$25,000** (individual) or **$32,000** (married filing jointly): **50% to 85%** of benefits become taxable. Income counted = 50% of benefits + other taxable income + adjustments + tax-free interest. |
| **Workers' comp** | — | **Generally not taxable**, unless SSDI is reduced because of it; then taxable by the amount of the reduction |

## 10. Every EXAM TIP from the chapter

1. **Any occupation (state wording):** In some states (e.g., North Carolina), the exam calls this definition "any occupation for which the insured is reasonably suited." In those states, the label "any occupation" refers to the **Social Security** definition.
2. **SSDI and "any occupation":** In North Carolina and some other states, the exam calls the SSDI definition "any occupation." In most states, "any occupation" is the **commercial** definition (qualified by education, training, or experience). If a question is about **SSDI** and SGA isn't an option, **choose "any occupation."** Be careful with commercial-definition questions if you see this.
3. **Residual:** The **loss** is a percentage of the person's **total income**. The residual **benefit** is a percentage of the **total disability benefit**.
4. **Group: occupational or non-occupational?** The answer isn't simple, but the course says to **choose non-occupational**.
5. **BOE vs DI:** **Disability income** provides income until you can return to work. **BOE** makes sure a small business owner has **a business to go back to**. BOE does **not** replace the owner's lost income.

**Review-note exam tips:**
- Know the differences between disability definitions
- Understand **elimination period vs probationary period**
- **Individual** policies are **occupational**; **group** policies are often **non-occupational**
- Disability benefits can **never exceed pre-disability income**
- Money is **taxed only once**
- Know the differences between the **business disability products**

**Partial disability standard:** For the course and exam, assume the partial benefit is **50% of the total disability benefit**.

## 12. Memory tricks (made up by Claude, not from the course)

- **"Own = Only mine. Any = Anything I'm trained for. SGA = Social Security, Gotta be Awful."** Strictness goes up in that order.
- **SGA's "G" = Gainful.** If you see "gainful" in an answer, it's Social Security. Commercial policies never use it.
- **Recurrent = "6 and it sticks."** Relapse inside 6 months sticks to the first claim (no new waiting period). After 6, start over.
- **Partial = Half.** Partial benefit is 50% of the full benefit.
- **Residual = "Lose X%, get X%."** Lose 60% of income, get 60% of the total benefit.
- **Change of occupation: "Riskier job, smaller check. Safer job, smaller bill."**
- **Probationary = "Probably sick already?"** It screens out illness at the start, once. Elimination = "Every time" deductible.
- **Individual is "I'm on my own"**: occupational, flat amount, no coordination. **Group is "Go with others"**: non-occupational, percentage, coordinates.
- **Non-can = "No change."** Guaranteed renewable = "Guaranteed to stay, not guaranteed to stay the same price."
- **FIO before, COLA after.** FIO increases coverage before a claim; COLA increases benefits after a claim.
- **BOE pays the building, not the boss.**
- **Cross-purchase math: "Everybody insures everybody else."** 4 partners → 4 × 3 = 12 policies.
- **Taxes: "Pay with taxed money, get untaxed money."** After-tax premium = tax-free benefit. Employer or pre-tax premium = taxable benefit.
- **SSDI 5-month wait: "Five and then thrive in month six."**

### Notes on conflicting course numbers
The course gives two different figures in a few places. Both are listed above so you recognize either on the exam:
- **Probationary period:** keyword page says 7 to 30 days; section 5.1.1 says 15, 30, or 60 days.
- **Return of premium rider:** keyword page says a dollar-for-dollar refund without interest if no claim is filed; section 5.5.8 says a percentage of premiums with favorable claims, at 65 or every 5 or 10 years.
- **Cash surrender value rider:** section says all premiums at 65 less benefits received; review keywords say all premiums at 65 if no claims have been made.
