## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning (course wording, condensed) |
|---|---|
| **NAIC model law** | The *Required Uniform Individual Accident and Sickness Policy Provisions Law*. Nearly all states adopted it or something similar. Sets consistent wording for **individual** health contracts. |
| **Mandatory provisions (12)** | Must be in all individually underwritten contracts. Protect the **insured**. Not specific to any one type of policy. |
| **Optional provisions (11)** | Protect the **insurer**. Define the insurer's rights and so limit them. Some apply only to certain policies (e.g., relation of earnings in disability). States may modify them. |
| **Standard language** | All 23 are in standard wording. An insurer may use its own wording only with state approval, and it can't be **less favorable** to the insured. |
| **Absolute assignment** | Assignee gets full control of the policy and full rights to its benefits. |
| **Accidental death benefit rider** | Pays an extra sum to the beneficiary if the insured dies in a covered accident, often a multiple of the face amount. |
| **Accelerated benefits rider** | Pays part of the death benefit before death if the insured is terminally ill. |
| **Entire contract clause** | Contract = policy form + signed application + attachments (riders, endorsements). |
| **Time limit on certain defenses** | Incontestability. After a set period, the insurer can't contest application statements. Fraud always overrides it. |
| **Grace period** | Time after the due date when premium can be paid with no lapse: 7 / 10 / 31 days. |
| **Reinstatement** | Puts a lapsed policy back in force (evidence of insurability + past-due premium, per keywords). |
| **Notice of claim** | Insured tells insurer of a loss: 20 days. |
| **Claim forms** | Insurer must send forms within 15 days of notice. |
| **Proof of loss** | Insured proves the loss: 90 days (up to 1 year if legally incapable). |
| **Free-look period** | Days after delivery to return the policy for a full refund: typically 10 (30 for direct mail and Medicare supplements). Also called "right to examine." |
| **Insuring clause** | The insurer's promise: types of benefits, when paid, start date and end of term. |
| **Consideration clause** | The insured's side of the exchange: **premium** + **statements on the application**. |
| **Noncancelable** | Can't be canceled, terms can't change, premium can't rise while premiums are paid. Most expensive. Renewable to a stated age. |
| **Guaranteed renewable** | Can't be canceled or changed while premiums are paid, but premium **can** rise for an **entire class**. |
| **Impairment rider** | Also called exclusion rider, coverage waiver, waiver of disability. Restricts coverage for a specific illness/injury for years or for life. |
| **Pre-existing condition provision** | No coverage for conditions that existed before the policy's effective date. |
| **Probationary (incubation) period** | Illness not covered for a short time after the policy starts or is reinstated. Accidents covered right away. |
| **Coordination of benefits (COB)** | Stops duplicate payment when someone has more than one **group** plan. Total paid can't exceed allowable expenses. |
| **Subrogation** | Insurer pays its insured, then sues the at-fault third party to recover what it paid. |
| **Principle of indemnity** | Insurance restores ("makes whole") the insured to their pre-loss financial position, with no profit. |
| **Revocable / irrevocable beneficiary** | Revocable: owner can change anytime without telling them. Irrevocable: needs their **written consent**; has a vested interest. |

## 3. The 12 mandatory provisions (protect the insured)

| # | Provision | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Entire contract** | Policy form + signed application + attachments. Nothing else counts (no outside materials, no verbal agreements). No changes after issue without the insured's written consent. Changes must be approved by an **executive officer** of the insurer and added by endorsement or rider. |
| 2 | **Time limit on certain defenses (incontestability)** | NAIC Model: insurer has up to **3 years** to contest the application. Most states cut this to **2 years**. **Fraud always overrides** the time limit: insurer may rescind from the start, refund all premiums, deny all claims ("the policy never existed"). Greatly restricted on individual major medical policies under the **ACA (2010)**. |
| 3 | **Grace period** | **7 days** weekly premiums, **10 days** monthly, **31 days** all other modes. Not paid by the end → policy lapses. |
| 4 | **Reinstatement** | Reinstatement payments limited to premiums due in the previous **60 days**. **Automatic** if the insurer or its agent accepts the late premium and doesn't require an application. If an application is required, the insurer must act within **45 days** or the policy is reinstated automatically. **Accidents** covered immediately; **sickness** covered only if it begins at least **10 days** after reinstatement. |
| 5 | **Notice of claim** | Within **20 days** of the loss (or as soon as reasonably possible). For disability claims payable more than **2 years**, the insurer may require notice every **6 months**. |
| 6 | **Claim forms** | Insurer must send claim forms within **15 days** of notice. If it doesn't, the insured may send proof of loss any way they can, and the insurer must accept it. |
| 7 | **Proof of loss** | Within **90 days** of the loss. If legally incapable, up to **1 year**. Non-periodic claims are paid immediately on receiving proof. |
| 8 | **Time payment of claims** | Pay **immediately** on notice and proof of loss. Disability income: **no less frequently than monthly**. |
| 9 | **Payment of claims** | Benefits payable to the insured; insurer may pay providers directly. Death benefit → named beneficiary; none named → the insured's **estate**. If the beneficiary is a minor, the course says the insurer pays another relative by blood or marriage. |
| 10 | **Physical exam and autopsy** | Insurer, **at its own expense**, may require an exam or autopsy (unless state law forbids). Exam **no more than once every 6 months**. |
| 11 | **Legal actions** | No suit until at least **60 days** after written proof of loss (gives the insurer time to research). Suit must begin within **3 years** of proof of loss. Some states allow longer. |
| 12 | **Change of beneficiary** | Owner may change a **revocable** beneficiary any time. An **irrevocable** beneficiary needs written consent. |

## 4. The 11 optional provisions (protect the insurer)

| # | Provision | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Change of occupation** | Safer job → premium reduced, excess unearned premium returned, benefit unchanged. Riskier job → **benefits reduced** proportionately, premium unchanged. |
| 2 | **Misstatement of age** | Age stated too low → benefits **lowered** to what the premium buys at the true age. Age stated too high → benefits **raised**, unless that exceeds the loss/allowable indemnity, in which case premium is reduced and the excess refunded. Policy is **not canceled**. |
| 3 | **Other insurance with this insurer** | Insured has more than one policy with the **same** insurer → only the maximum benefit of one policy is paid; the other is terminated and premium refunded. Stops profiting. |
| 4 | **Insurance with other insurer** (expense-incurred basis) | Duplicate reimbursement coverage with **another** insurer → insurers share the claim pro rata; total limited to the loss. Premiums returned prorated. Addresses over-insurance; same problem as **COB** in group plans. |
| 5 | **Insurance with other insurers** (other than expense-incurred basis) | Rarely used today. Same idea but for indemnity / **"loss of time"** benefits like disability. Applies when the insurer wasn't told about other coverage before the claim; pays pro rata and refunds excess premium. |
| 6 | **Relation of earnings to insurance (average earnings)** | **Disability income** only. All policies together can't pay more than the income lost; benefits paid pro rata. Usually in noncancelable or guaranteed renewable policies. Guards against moral hazard. In the current NAIC Model it's combined with the "loss of time" version of insurance with other insurers. |
| 7 | **Unpaid premiums** | Insurer may deduct unpaid premium from a claim paid during the grace period. |
| 8 | **Cancellation** | Insurer could cancel any time with **5 days' written notice**; claims arising before cancellation are paid. **Most states prohibit it.** Appears in cancelable contracts. |
| 9 | **Conformity with state statutes** | Amends the policy to meet minimum state requirements. **Optional despite the name.** |
| 10 | **Illegal occupation** | Deny claims for injuries while committing an illegal act (robbing a bank) or in a felonious job (meth lab). If found after payment, insurer denies and tries to recover the money. |
| 11 | **Intoxicants and narcotics** | No liability for losses while under the influence of alcohol or **non-prescribed** drugs. Doesn't apply to prescribed drugs taken as directed. |

## 5. Additional necessary provisions (language not regulated, placement not mandated)

| Provision | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Free look (right to examine/return)** | Typically **10 days** from delivery; full refund. **30 days** for direct mail and some specialty policies such as Medicare supplements. Notice usually on the **face page**. Count from the day **after** delivery. |
| **Insuring clause** | Insurer's promise: benefits, circumstances, start date and end of term. |
| **Consideration clause** | Insured's consideration = **premium paid** + **statements (representations) on the application**. |

**Worked example (course):** Policy delivered **January 27**. Day 1 = January 28. The 10-day free look ends **February 6**.

## 6. Renewability provisions

Listed **most to least favorable** to the insured. More favorable = higher premium. An insured may cancel anytime in writing. Every type can end if the premium isn't paid by the end of the grace period, and coverage can end at an age stated in the policy.

| Type | Can insurer cancel? | Can insurer raise premium? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Noncancelable** | No (while premiums paid) | **No** | Double guarantee. Terms can't change. Most expensive. Renewable to a stated age. Sometimes called "noncancelable and guaranteed renewable." |
| **2. Guaranteed renewable** | No (while premiums paid) | **Yes, by class only** | Can't change benefits, conditions or terms. Used by some individual disability, LTC and Medicare supplement plans. |
| **3. Conditionally renewable** | Only for conditions in the policy (reaching an age, losing gainful employment), **never for health** | At renewal, class basis only | Some guarantee of continuance, less than 1 and 2. |
| **4. Optionally renewable** (company's option) | Yes, at the renewal date with notice | Yes | No guarantee beyond the current term. Much more favorable to the insurer. |
| **5. Nonrenewable** | Ends at term | n/a | Short-term, 1 year or less, temporary. Bridges coverage gaps. |
| **6. Period of time (term)** | Ends when the term expires | n/a | e.g., short-term major medical; renewable only for a stated term like 6 months. |
| **7. Cancelable** | **Yes, anytime**, with written notice; unearned premium returned | Yes | Least favorable, so lowest premium. Either side can terminate. Insurer may cancel if too many claims are filed. |

**Worked examples (course):**
- **Kayla**, 35, surgeon, noncancelable disability policy. Two years in she shows signs of a degenerative condition. Her premium can't rise and her terms can't change while she pays. That stability justifies the higher premium.
- **Guaranteed renewable, truck drivers:** an insurer may raise premiums for all truck drivers and similar blue-collar jobs because overall morbidity rose, but **not** for one driver because that driver filed a claim.
- **Nonrenewable 6-month policy timeline:** Month 0 coverage starts; Month 3 mid-policy review, no extension; Month 5 expiration notice; Month 6 coverage ends with no renewal; Month 6+ the insured must find new coverage (employer plan, ACA marketplace).

**Course quiz:** *Which is true of a guaranteed renewable policy?* → **The insurer can raise premiums only if the increase applies to an entire class.** (Not: cancel anytime; raise one person's rate after a claim; change terms if health worsens.)

## 7. Exclusions, limits and restrictions

**The exclusions section is NOT on the policy face (first page).** Common exclusions:

- War or act of war; self-inflicted injuries; injuries as a **pilot or crew member** of an aircraft
- Military service while covered by the government (some insurers add a **military suspension** provision for disability income)
- **Extended** foreign travel or foreign residence (short trips usually fine)
- **Noncommercial** air travel (test pilot, amateur pilot)
- Suicide or use of drugs/narcotics
- Hernia as an accidental injury (the course notes the loss may be covered under a medical expense policy)
- Riots; injuries while committing or attempting a felony
- Occupational injuries covered by **workers' compensation**, or care paid by the **VA**
- Cosmetic surgery, experimental surgery, vision correction (eye exams), general dentistry. **Exception:** medically necessary cosmetic, vision or dental treatment caused by an **accident** may be covered (e.g., a baseball player hit in the face by a fastball).
- **Not medically necessary:** even a covered service is paid only if medically necessary. *Diedra's policy covers appendix removal, but not a "proactive" removal with no medical need.*

### Pre-existing conditions

- No coverage for conditions that existed before the effective date. Often used for conditions not disclosed.
- If the applicant **discloses** a condition, the insurer can: **deny** the application; **charge more**; **permanently exclude** the condition at standard premium; or treat it as **minor** and either cover it from **day one** or apply the standard pre-existing exclusion.
- Disability and medical expense policies **not affected by the ACA** typically exclude pre-existing conditions. Essential in group and simplified-underwriting contracts.
- Protects the insurer against **adverse selection**, even if the applicant didn't know they were ill.
- Subject to the **time limit on certain defenses**.
- Waiting period generally **no more than 12 months**.

**Worked example (course): Marcus**, 42, freelance designer, discloses mild asthma diagnosed 5 years ago, no recent flare-ups. The insurer may deny; approve at a higher premium; approve at standard premium with asthma permanently excluded; or call it minor and cover it from day one or apply a **12-month** exclusion. It applies even if Marcus didn't know about the condition.

### Impairment (waiver for impairment) rider

- Also called **exclusion rider, coverage waiver, waiver of disability**.
- For a serious pre-existing condition: restricts coverage for that illness/injury for **a period of years or for life**.
- Shows the insurer is accepting greater risk. May be issued **with or without** extra premium.
- Must be **explained to the owner at delivery**; owners generally **sign a statement** that they understand it.

**Worked example (course):** A disability income insured has had several **left knee** operations. The rider says no coverage for total disability from any injury to the left knee. The agent should explain it clearly.

### Probationary (incubation) period

- Group plan: takes the form of **delayed eligibility** (must be employed a while first).
- Individual policy: starts at **inception or reinstatement**; a **one-time** delay for **illness** only. **Accidents are covered immediately** because we know exactly when an accident happens; illness onset is less certain (COVID-19 example).
- Lets the insurer avoid illnesses contracted before the effective date.
- States limit length: **10 days is common, 30 at most.**

## 8. Additional health insurance policy provisions

| Provision | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Coordination of benefits (COB)** | Common in **group** plans. Prevents duplicate payments and over-insurance. Primary plan pays its full contract benefit first; secondary pays additional benefits. Total never exceeds costs incurred or total max of all plans. |
| **Owner's rights / assignment** | Owner holds ownership rights, including assigning benefit payments directly to providers. Unlike life insurance, owners **can't transfer ownership** of the policy. Assignment saves paying out of pocket and waiting for reimbursement; the policy still reimburses only eligible expenses. |
| **No loss/no gain** | State laws stopping profit from insurance. Purpose is to **indemnify** (restore). |
| **Restoration of benefits** | Amounts paid don't reduce benefits available for future claims. Immediate in disability (per-claim benefit); some restore at the end of the policy period; LTC may add it as a rider after a claim-free recovery period. |
| **Benefit payment** | How benefits are paid: disability = periodic (weekly or monthly); medical = reimburses the insured or provider per expense. |
| **Reductions in coverage** | Coverage amounts aren't reduced after a loss, but most policies have **lifetime maximums**. |
| **Military suspense** | Coverage suspended during **active military service**; resumes afterward with no loss of benefits or gap. |
| **Subrogation** | Insurer pays the insured, then sues the liable third party. Insured gets paid faster; insurer recovers; insured can't collect twice. |
| **Modes of premium payment** | Monthly, quarterly, semiannual, annual. **Annual is cheapest; monthly most expensive.** Health policies have **no single-premium or paid-up-for-life** option. |
| **Waiver of premium** | Premiums waived after the insured is **totally disabled** for the period stated in the policy. |

### COB rules (course)

| Situation | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Married couple, each in own employer plan + spouse's | Your **own** employer plan | Spouse's plan |
| Dependent children | Plan of the parent whose **birthday comes first in the calendar year** | Other parent's plan |
| Worker **65+**, employer has **20+** employees (COBRA group) | **Employer group plan** | Medicare (all claims except work-related injuries/illness) |
| Retiree medical through an employer | **Medicare** | Retiree plan |
| Worker with an **individual** health policy | **Medicare** | Individual policy |
| Group plan with **fewer than 20** employees | **Medicare** | Group plan |

**Worked examples (course):**
- **$700 rule:** someone with **$700** of allowable expenses can't collect more than **$700**, however many group plans cover it.
- **John and Cindy:** John's plan is primary for John, Cindy's is secondary for him; Cindy's plan is primary for Cindy, John's is secondary for her. Kids: the parent whose birthday is earlier in the year is primary.

### Beneficiaries

- Death benefit with a named beneficiary skips **probate**. No beneficiary → goes to the insured's **estate** (probate delays and costs).
- **Revocable:** change anytime, no notice or permission.
- **Irrevocable:** needs **written consent**; vested interest, so the owner can't assign, take loans or surrender without consent; entitled to a copy of the policy.

| Order | Who | Gets paid when |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | First in line; can be several, equal or different shares | Insured dies (course says proceeds are income tax-free) |
| **Secondary (contingent)** | Second in line | Primary died before the insured |
| **Tertiary** | Third in line | Insured outlives both primary and contingent |

### Uniform Simultaneous Death Act vs Common Disaster Provision

| | Uniform Simultaneous Death Act | Common Disaster Provision |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A law | A survivorship clause added to the policy |
| Situation | Insured and primary die in a common disaster (plane crash) and **no one can tell who died first** | Primary dies **shortly after** the insured |
| Rule | **Insured is presumed to have survived** (died last) → primary treated as dying first → paid to **contingent** | Primary must outlive the insured by a set period, **typically 14 to 30 days**; if not, paid to **contingent**. Paid to primary's estate only if primary survives past the period. |

### Principle of indemnity (Learning Objective 8)

Restore the insured to their pre-loss financial position; prevent profit; **COB and subrogation** support it; benefits limited to actual expenses incurred.

## 9. Every EXAM TIP from the chapter

1. **Mandatory vs optional:** ask "Who benefits?" Mandatory = the **insured**. Optional = the **insurer**.
2. **Entire contract:** remember **WYSIWYG**, "What you see is what you get!"
3. **Time limit on certain defenses:** unless the test references state law or mixes law and general questions, assume **3 years** (the Model Act). Otherwise use state law; if no specific reference, assume 3 years.
4. **Notice of claim:** unless the exam specifies a disability claim or special circumstances, assume **20 days**.
5. **Time payment of claims:** disability payments may be described as **"no less frequently than monthly."** Read carefully.
6. **Legal actions:** unless state law says otherwise, assume **3 years**, also when the question is general and the exam separates general and state questions.
7. **Optional provisions and state law:** if your exam mixes state and general content, compare these with the same-named provisions in the state law section and use the **state** version if they differ. If the exam separates them, use this chapter for the general section.
8. **Change of occupation:** safer job → premium reduced. Riskier job → premium stays the same (benefits drop).
9. **Misstatement of age:** age too low → benefits lowered once the age is raised. Age too high → benefits increase once it's lowered.
10. **Conformity with state statutes** sounds mandatory but is **optional**. It protects the insurer by ensuring compliance with state minimums.

**Review-notes exam tips:** focus on mandatory vs optional; know renewability types; understand how pre-existing provisions limit coverage; mandatory benefit the insured, optional benefit the insurer; know timeframes for grace periods, proof of loss and legal actions; understand how COB prevents duplicate payments.

**"Remember" list:** entire contract = policy form, application, attachments; grace periods vary by payment frequency; renewability determines long-term stability; pre-existing provisions protect against adverse selection; COB prevents over-insurance; beneficiary designations decide who gets paid and in what order.

**Review-notes "Important distinctions" (renewability):** more favorable renewability = higher premium; all policies can terminate if premiums aren't paid by the end of the grace period; noncancelable = double guarantee (no termination, no premium increase).

## 11. Spots where the course contradicts itself

1. **Grace period for "other" modes.** The text and review notes say **31 days**. The grace period graphic says **"30/31 days (30 or 31 days, state-dependent)."** Go with **31** for the general exam.
2. **Time limit on certain defenses.** The keywords and review notes say **"two to three years" / "typically 2–3 years."** The section says the NAIC Model is **up to 3 years** and most states use **2**. The exam tip says **assume 3** unless state law is referenced.
3. **Is COB an optional provision?** The chapter summary says the 11 optional provisions include "the misstatement of age provision **and coordination of benefits**." But COB isn't in the list of 11. It's taught in section 6 (additional provisions, group plans). The optional provision that does the same job is **insurance with other insurer(s)**. If asked to pick from the 11, COB is not one of them.
4. **Who can cancel a cancelable policy?** The section says **either the insured or the insurer**. The review notes say **"by the insurer at any time with notice."** Both agree the insurer can cancel any time; the insured can always cancel in writing (renewability intro).
5. **Counting to 11.** The 11 only works if "insurance with other insurer" (expense-incurred) and "insurance with other insurers" (other than expense-incurred) count as **two** provisions. The course also says relation of earnings is now **combined** with the loss-of-time version in the current NAIC Model, which would make fewer than 11. For the exam, stick with **11**.

## 12. Memory tricks (made up by Claude, not from the course)

- **Mandatory = Me (the insured). Optional = Owner of the company (the insurer).**
- **Grace "7-10-31":** a **week** for weekly, about a **third of a month** for monthly, a **full month** for everything else.
- **Claims timeline "20 → 15 → 90 → 60 → 3":** you **notify** (20), they **send forms** (15), you **prove** (90), you **wait to sue** (60 days), you **must sue by** (3 years).
- **"Twenty to tell, fifteen to send."**
- **Reinstatement "60-45-10":** 60 days of back premium, 45 days for the insurer to answer, 10 days before sickness is covered.
- **Renewability ladder "No Good Cats Ordinarily Nap Past Curfew":** Noncancelable, Guaranteed, Conditionally, Optionally, Nonrenewable, Period of time, Cancelable.
- **Noncancelable = "Nothing changes."** **Guaranteed renewable = "Group (class) raises only."**
- **Change of occupation: "Riskier job, smaller check."** Premium never goes up.
- **Misstatement of age: "Lie young, get less."**
- **Probationary period: "Accidents don't wait, germs do."**
- **Simultaneous death: "The insured always wins the tie"** (presumed to die last), so money skips the primary and goes to the contingent.
- **COB birthday rule: "First birthday, first payer."** Month and day only, not age.
- **Modes: "Pay once a year, pay the least."**
- **Free look: count from tomorrow.**
