# Chapter 4 Study Guide: Health and Accident Insurance

## Chapter overview

Health and accident insurance indemnifies people for losses from **two perils: accidents and sickness**. Xcel labels this Life & Health Chapter 13 / Health Chapter 4. It has six sections:

1. Health and Accident Insurance Overview
2. Covered Perils
3. Covered Losses (the five types)
4. Classes of Health Insurance (individual vs. group, private vs. government, limited vs. comprehensive)
5. Limited Insurance Policy Types
6. Dental Insurance

"Health and accident insurance" is also called health insurance, accident and health, accident and sickness, or sickness insurance. Medical expense is only **one** type of health coverage.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Morbidity | The probability or risk of becoming ill or disabled |
| Accidental bodily injury (accidental result) | Only the **injury** must be unexpected and unintended, even if the act was intended. Less restrictive, most common, legally accepted |
| Accidental means | More restrictive: the **cause and the result** must both be unforeseen, unexpected and unintended |
| Principal sum | The **death benefit** paid under an AD&D policy |
| Capital sum | The lump sum paid for accidental **loss of sight or dismemberment** under AD&D |
| Decreasing (term) disability insurance | Disability credit insurance. Fixed end date; pays the monthly loan payment during disability; insurer exposure shrinks as the loan is paid down |
| Elimination period | Waiting period at the start of a disability or LTC claim before benefits begin. A deductible in time, not money |
| Formulary | The list of medications a prescription drug plan covers |
| Predetermination of benefits | Review of whether proposed dental services are medically necessary and covered, the approved amount, and the patient's share. It does **not** authorize the procedure |
| Endodontics | Root canals; diseases of the tooth pulp |
| Periodontics | Gum disease diagnosis, treatment and prevention |
| Orthodontics | Straightening teeth with braces and retainers |
| Prosthodontics | Dentures and other artificial dental devices |
| Oral surgery | Extractions and other surgical treatment |
| Indemnity (two meanings) | Medical and dental: a plan that **reimburses** covered expenses. Disability, LTC and hospital indemnity: a **flat dollar amount** paid periodically (hospital indemnity and LTC pay it regardless of cost) |

## Perils, losses, and classes of coverage

### Two perils: sickness and accidents

- **Sickness (illness or disease):** an internal occurrence. Sudden or gradual onset; acute infections and chronic conditions. Illness from ongoing workplace exposure = **occupational disease**.
- **Accident:** an unintended occurrence from an **external** source, **fixed in time and space**. A loss caused intentionally by a deliberate act is excluded.

| Definition | What must be unintended | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accidental bodily injury (accidental result) | Only the **result** (the injury) | Most policies; commonly accepted, legally valid |
| Accidental means | Both the **cause and the result** | More restrictive; pays less often |

Course example: Robert voluntarily jumps from a questionable height and is hurt. A "results" policy pays (the jump was voluntary, the injury was not). An "accidental means" policy pays nothing, because he chose to jump.

### Five covered losses

| Type | Coverage | Losses covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medical expense | Reimbursement insurance | Hospital, surgical, physician expenses; outpatient care |
| Disability | Income replacement | Income lost to a disabling disease or injury |
| Dental | Health expense coverage | Diagnostic and preventive; regular care; care needed due to accidents |
| Long-term care | Care for those who can't do daily tasks | Physical loss of function; senile dementia, Alzheimer's, Parkinson's |
| Accident | Benefits for injuries in accidents | AD&D |

- **Medical expense:** reimburses fully or partly. A health program should **begin** with enough medical expense coverage. Most Americans have a major medical policy or a service plan (HMO). A lower deductible ($500 vs. $2,500) or richer coinsurance (80/20 vs. 75/25) costs **more**.
- **Disability (income replacement):** guaranteed weekly or monthly payments when wages are lost. Social Security is a **secondary** source (narrow, strict definition); personal plans should be **primary**. A longer elimination period lowers premium (a "time deductible"). Individually funded benefits are **income-tax-free**, so benefits are capped at **60% to 70% of gross** earnings. Example: earns $3,000, takes home $2,000, benefit $1,800.
- **Long-term care:** loss of ability to do daily tasks; always an **organic** cause. Covers senile dementia, Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, but **not anxiety or depression** (psychological).
- **Dental:** usually written on a **group** basis with deductible and coinsurance; preventive care often has **no deductible**; annual limit often **under $2,000**.
- **Accident:** pays for injury on or off the job. AD&D is sold standalone, in a group plan, or as a rider; pays **lump sums**.
- **Other limited coverages:** hospital care or the financial impact of certain diseases.

### Classes of health insurance

| Distinction | Side A | Side B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who is covered | **Individual:** contract between insured and insurer; application and evidence of insurability; coverage starts on the **effective date** | **Group:** contract between insurer and **group** (employers, associations, unions, credit unions); standardized options; lower admin cost (single monthly bill); must follow COBRA |
| Who provides it | **Private:** commercial insurers, service providers, provider groups, self-insured employers and associations | **Government (social insurance):** Medicare, Medicaid, Social Security |
| Breadth | **Comprehensive:** "open-peril"; covers everything not expressly excluded (major medical, private disability income) | **Limited:** covers only what is listed; anything not named is NOT covered; requires a **"Notice to Insured"** |

- Group premiums: **experience rating** (group's claim history) for **large** groups; **community rating** (area risk) for **small** groups.
- **COBRA** gives employees and families extended but duration-limited coverage after employment ends.
- **Medicare:** federal; seniors and people with severe disabilities. **Medicaid:** state and federal funding; low-income; federal standards, state administration. **Social Security:** disability income for those unable to be gainfully employed.
- **ACA** created state-based exchanges (marketplaces) for the individual and small group market; only qualified health plans can be sold there.
- **Limited vs. special risk:** limited covers specific **common** risks (an airport flight accident policy). Special risk covers **unusual hazards** ordinary policies exclude (a ballet dancer's $1 million legs, a test pilot in an experimental plane).

## Limited policy types

Every limited policy covers only a **listed** set of perils, losses, or circumstances: accident-only, specified diseases, limited services, or a fixed dollar amount per day (excluding LTC).

### AD&D (accident-only)

- Pays a **lump sum** if an accident causes death or dismemberment. The accident must be the **sole cause** of the loss.
- Death usually must occur **within 90 days** of the accident. Some states pay after 90 days if the insured was totally and continuously disabled from accident to death.
- No benefit if the loss was due to being under the influence of **alcohol or narcotics**.
- **Principal sum** = the death benefit = the face amount = the **maximum** the policy pays.
- **Capital sum** = paid for permanent loss of hearing, speech, sight, or a limb; a **percentage of the principal sum** (often 50%).

| Loss | Typical benefit |
| --- | --- |
| One hand or one foot | 50% of principal sum |
| One arm or one leg | Two-thirds of principal sum |
| Both feet, or sight in both eyes | 100% (full principal sum) |

Course example: $50,000 for loss of life, two limbs, or both eyes = principal sum. $25,000 for one eye or one limb = capital sum.

### Limited illness

- **Specified (dread) disease:** limited benefits for one disease (cancer, heart disease). Pays **scheduled, fixed-dollar** amounts for listed items like hospital confinement or chemotherapy.
- **Critical illness:** a **lump sum** paid to the insured on **diagnosis and survival** of a critical illness. Use it for anything, including non-medical costs. Typical conditions: heart attack, stroke, organ transplant, end-stage renal failure; may include Alzheimer's or permanent loss of sight. Some require surviving a set period (e.g., **30 days**).

### Other limited policies

| Policy | Key facts |
| --- | --- |
| Hospital indemnity (income) | Pays a set daily, weekly or monthly amount **directly to the insured** (not the hospital) while confined. Based **only on days hospitalized**, not on expenses. May have benefit limits, pre-existing condition rules, elimination periods |
| Credit disability | Protects the **lender**. Pays monthly debt payments while the debtor is disabled. Creditor = **owner and beneficiary**; debtor = insured, usually pays premium. Needs debtor's permission. Certificate issued **within 30 days** of the debt. Pays until no longer disabled or debt paid, whichever is first. Group credit needs a minimum number of debtors per year (state law). Also called **decreasing or reducing term disability** |
| Blanket | Covers a group exposed to the **same risks at different times**, whose members keep changing: common carrier passengers, school students or sports teams, volunteer firefighters. Group is owner; **no individual underwriting, no enrollment, no certificates**. Benefits go to insureds, beneficiaries or estate; minors' benefits may go to parents or guardians |
| Short-term medical | Up to **90 days**; **cannot be renewed**. Scaled-down major medical; interim gap coverage or a cheaper alternative to ACA plans. Dollar limits; may skip outpatient Rx; often excludes maternity, substance abuse and mental health. May be medically underwritten and exclude pre-existing conditions; limits depend on state law |
| Prescription drugs | Covers drugs **not** dispensed in a hospital or extended care facility. **Formulary** sorts drugs by price class, family, purpose (generic, brand-name, specialty). Excludes non-prescription drugs, appliances, hypodermic needles, third-party administration; often sexual dysfunction and infertility unless state law requires. Most require a **copay** |
| Hearing | Some private plans cover audiology tests and hearing aid evaluations, with partial or full hearing aid coverage |
| Vision | Reasonable and customary charges for eye exams by ophthalmologists and optometrists; lenses and frames often partly covered. Often **new lenses yearly, frames every two years**. Excludes safety lenses, cosmetic extras, plastic lenses, frame upgrades. LASIK often partly covered; other eye surgery and eye disease go to **medical expense** plans |

## Dental insurance

About **half of all state exams** ask **one to four** dental questions. Dental is health expense coverage focused on diagnostic and preventive care, usually bought through **groups**, with a deductible and coinsurance. Group dental generally has **no conversion privilege** but is subject to **COBRA** continuation.

### Three plan types

| Plan | Providers | Deductibles and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standalone | Any provider | Separate deductible and benefit limits, even inside an employer package |
| Integrated | Shares the **medical** plan's network | Shares medical's deductible structure: lower premium, but higher deductibles and out-of-pocket |
| Combination | May share a provider network | Own deductible and benefits like standalone; basic services at usual and customary, major services on a schedule |

### Two ways to define benefits

Dental plans are written on an **indemnity (reimbursement)** basis with free choice of provider; insureds can **assign benefits** to providers.

| Scheduled (basic) | Non-scheduled (comprehensive) |
| --- | --- |
| Pays actual cost of each listed service up to a published maximum | **Most common** type |
| Insured pays anything over the maximum | Pays on a **UCR** (usual, customary, reasonable) basis |
| **No deductible or coinsurance**; first-dollar coverage | Deductible and coinsurance vary by level of care |

### Levels of treatment (non-scheduled): 100 / 80 / 50

| Level | Examples | Plan pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preventive | Periodic cleanings and exams, annual X-rays | **100%**, often no deductible |
| Basic | Fillings, extractions, root canals, gum disease treatment | **80%** of usual and customary, up to an annual max; deductible applies |
| Major | Oral surgery, crowns | Insured's coinsurance typically **50%**; deductible and annual max apply |

### Specialties

| Specialty | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Oral surgery | Extractions; surgical treatment of injuries, diseases, jaw defects |
| Endodontics | Root canals; diseases of the pulp inside each tooth |
| Periodontics | Gum disease and diseases of tissues supporting the teeth |
| Prosthodontics | Replacing teeth: bridgework, dentures, artificial devices |
| Orthodontics | Correcting teeth and jaw irregularities with braces and retainers |
| Pediatric dentistry | Preventive and restorative care for children and adolescents |
| Oral pathology | Tissue biopsies to treat oral diseases such as oral cancer |

### Limitations

- Routine cleanings, exams, X-rays, fluoride, preventive care and local anesthetics are covered at 100%, but plans **limit how many per year**. Orthodontics often has calendar-year maximums or **lifetime caps**.
- **Predetermination of benefits** (also called **pre-certification** or **prior authorization**): review that treatment is reasonable and necessary. Skipping it does not void coverage, but nobody knows the approved amount in advance.
- Common exclusions: cosmetic work (**unless due to an accident**), services by government agencies, treatment covered by workers' comp, duplicate or lost/stolen dentures, oral hygiene training, exotic procedures (splinting, restoring occlusion).

## Exam Tips and review notes

### Exam Tips (as written in the course)

1. When answering exam questions, pay attention to context to correctly apply technical definitions (especially **"indemnity"**, which means different things for medical vs. disability insurance).
2. Assume losses that result from **complications are excluded** under accident policies.
3. If a question asks for a time frame within which an accidental loss must occur, **assume 90 days**.
4. The standard percentage for the **capital sum** on the exam is **50%**.
5. Some policies might award a capital sum for **partial loss** of a foot or hand.
6. Illness arises from **internal** factors; accidents are **external** events.
7. Focus on the aspect of coverage the question asks about. Daily benefit or daily room cost points to **hospital indemnity**; the disease that caused the stay points to **critical illness**.
8. Know the difference between accidental means and accidental results.
9. Social Security has a **strict definition of disability** many people don't meet.

### Remember (from the review notes)

- **Morbidity** = risk of illness or disability; **mortality** = risk of death.
- AD&D requires the accident to be the **sole cause** of the loss.
- Limited policies require a **"Notice to Insured"** stating it is a limited benefit policy.
- Dental typically pays preventive **100%**, basic **80%**, major **50%**.
- Short-term medical **cannot be renewed** and lasts up to **90 days**.
- Credit disability makes the borrower's loan payments; the **lender is owner and beneficiary**.
- LTC covers physical or cognitive losses from **organic** causes, not psychological conditions.
- Blanket insurance covers group members exposed to the **same risks at different times**.
- **Principal sum** = death benefit; **capital sum** = dismemberment benefit.
- Dental predetermination of benefits determines whether services are medically necessary and covered.

## Memory hooks

These are Claude's own study aids, not course wording.

- **"Means is mean."** Accidental *means* is the stricter, meaner definition: cause AND result must be accidental. *Results* only cares about the result.
- **Principal = Passed away.** Both start with P. **Capital = Cut off** (both start with C): dismemberment, sight, hearing, speech.
- **"Half a hand."** One hand or foot = 50%. An arm or leg is bigger, so it gets more: two-thirds. Lose both = 100%.
- **90 for the short stuff.** AD&D death within 90 days; short-term medical up to 90 days. If unsure of a time frame on an accident question, say 90.
- **"Dentist report card: 100, 80, 50."** Preventive gets an A+, basic gets a B, major gets a failing 50.
- **Five losses = "My Dog Doesn't Like Accidents":** Medical, Disability, Dental, Long-term care, Accident.
- **Specialties by sound:** **Endo** = inside (pulp, root canal). **Perio** = perimeter (gums). **Ortho** = straight (braces). **Prostho** = prosthetic (dentures). **Pedo** = kids.
- **Credit disability protects the Creditor.** Creditor is owner and beneficiary; the debtor just pays.
- **Blanket = a blanket over whoever is on the bus today.** Members change, risk stays the same, no names, no certificates.
- **Hospital indemnity counts days, not dollars.** Critical illness pays for the diagnosis, not the stay.
- **LTC = organic, not emotional.** Alzheimer's yes, depression no.
