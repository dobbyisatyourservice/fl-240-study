## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning (course wording, condensed) |
|---|---|
| **Agent** | In Florida: a general lines, life, health or title agent. Includes "producer." It does **not** include customer representatives, limited customer representatives or service representatives. |
| **Insurance agency** | A business location where insurance transactions that only licensed agents can do take place. Each location needs an **agent in charge**. |
| **Insurance producer** | Licensed to sell, solicit or negotiate insurance for compensation. Also called agent or broker. |
| **Transacting insurance** | Solicitation or inducement, preliminary negotiations, effectuation, and handling matters afterward. Servicing counts too. |
| **Solicitation** | Trying to persuade someone to buy: describing benefits, terms or premiums; handing out an invitation to contract; making recommendations; completing applications; comparing or interpreting products. |
| **Insurer** | Every person engaged as indemnitor, surety or contractor in the business of insurance or annuities. |
| **Reinsurance** | Insurance for insurance companies. The **ceding** insurer passes risk to the **reinsurer** for more capacity and stability. |
| **Domestic / Foreign / Alien** | Formed under Florida law / formed in another U.S. state or territory / formed in another country. |
| **Fraternal benefit society** | Incorporated society for members and beneficiaries only. No capital stock, not for profit, lodge system with ritualistic work, representative government. |
| **Certificate of authority (COA)** | The insurer's "business license," issued by **OIR**, to transact insurance in Florida. |
| **Authorized (admitted)** | Has a COA from OIR. |
| **Unauthorized (nonadmitted)** | No COA. Denied, or never applied. |
| **Stock company** | Owned by stockholders. Generally nonparticipating. Goal is profit for owners. |
| **Mutual company** | Owned by policyowners. Participating, pays dividends. Goal is the lowest net cost. |
| **Mixed-plan company** | A stock company that issues both participating and nonparticipating policies. |
| **Fiduciary** | A position of trust. Florida agents are fiduciaries: they collect, record and remit premiums. The application page adds that it means acting in the **customer's best interest**. |
| **Trust funds** | All premiums, return premiums and other funds an agent receives are held in a fiduciary capacity. |
| **Commingling** | Mixing insurer money with personal or agency money. A breach of fiduciary duty. |
| **Controlled business** | Insurance written on the agent's own life, health, property, immediate family or business associates. Generally limited to **50%** of sales. |
| **Rebating** | Giving anything of value not stated in the policy to induce a purchase. Gifts of **$100 or less** are not rebating. |
| **Twisting** | Misrepresenting to get someone to drop a policy and buy one from **another insurer**. |
| **Churning** | The same misrepresenting behavior, but with policy values used to buy from the **same insurer**. |
| **Sliding** | Telling a buyer that extra coverage is legally required or free when it isn't, or charging for it without consent. |
| **Coercion** | Physical or mental force to make someone buy. |
| **Defamation** | Knowingly making or spreading false or maliciously critical statements meant to injure a person. |
| **Unaffiliated insurance agent** | Self-appoints. Has no insurer appointment. Advises for a fee set in advance by a written contract. |
| **Appointment** | Authority from an insurer or employer to transact insurance on its behalf. |
| **Receiver** | A third party that takes control to rehabilitate or liquidate an insurer. In Florida that's **DFS**. |
| **Impaired insurer** | Not insolvent, but the Department thinks it may be unable to meet its obligations. |
| **Insolvent insurer** | A court has issued an order of liquidation with a finding of insolvency. |
| **Grandfathered plan** | Health plan someone was enrolled in on **March 23, 2010**, covering them continuously since. Doesn't have to meet every ACA rule. |
| **Balance billing** | A provider charging the insured the difference between what the insurer pays and what the provider charges. |
| **Mini-COBRA** | The Florida Health Insurance Coverage Continuation Act. Covers employees of small employers (**fewer than 20**) that federal COBRA doesn't reach. |
| **Qualified beneficiary** | The covered employee (unless fired for gross misconduct), spouse or dependent child, judged the day before the qualifying event. |
| **Coordination of benefits (COB)** | Required in group hospital, medical and surgical expense policies. Insurers together can't pay more than 100% of reasonable expenses. |
| **Outline of coverage** | A summary of a policy's main benefits, exclusions and renewal terms, given at the time of application. |
| **Buyer's Guide / Shopper's Guide** | A consumer guide. Medicare supplement uses a Buyer's Guide; LTC uses the NAIC Shopper's Guide. |
| **Chronically ill** | Certified as unable to do at least **2 ADLs** for at least **90 days**, or needing substantial supervision because of severe cognitive impairment. |
| **ADLs** | Bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, continence, eating (6). |
| **Cognitive impairment** | A deficiency in short- or long-term memory, orientation to person, place and time, deductive or abstract reasoning, or judgment about safety. |
| **Guaranteed issue** | A policy that must be offered regardless of health status, preexisting conditions or claims history. |
| **Modified community rating** | Spreads risk across a geographic area but allows separate factors for age, gender, family composition and tobacco use. |
| **Late enrollee** | Enrolls outside the first eligibility period or a special enrollment period. |
| **DMPO** | Discount medical plan organization. Licensed by OIR but **not insurance**. Marketers don't need an agent license. |

### Twisting vs churning vs replacement

| | **Twisting** | **Churning** | **Replacement** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal? | **No** | **No** | **Yes**, if it is in the owner's best interest and the owner understands it |
| Which insurer gets the new policy | A **different** insurer (external) | The **same** insurer (internal) | Either |
| What makes it wrong | Misleading statements, incomplete comparisons or omissions to induce a lapse or surrender | Using existing values with no demonstrable benefit, or without telling the owner values will drop or more premium is due | Nothing, if the disclosure rules are followed |
| Penalty | 1st-degree misdemeanor + admin fine up to **$5,000** non-willful / **$75,000** willful (willful needs fraudulent conduct) | Same as twisting | N/A |
| Paperwork | | | "Notice Regarding Replacement." Medicare supplement: replacing insurer notifies the old insurer within **5 working days**. LTC: within **5 working days** of the application or issue, whichever is sooner. |

### Group health: continuation vs conversion

| | **Mini-COBRA continuation** | **Group health conversion** | **Group LTC conversion** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | Employees and dependents of employers with **fewer than 20** employees | Anyone whose group hospital, surgical or major medical coverage ends | Anyone whose group LTC coverage ends |
| Time insured first | Covered the day before the qualifying event | At least **3 months** continuously | At least **6 months** continuously |
| Deadline | Notify the insurer within **63 days**; insurer sends forms by certified mail within **14 days**; elect and pay within **30 days** | Apply and pay within **63 days** | |
| Evidence of insurability | No | No | No |
| Premium cap | **115%** of group premium (**150%** during the disability extension) | **200%** of the standard risk rate | |
| How long | **18 months** (29 with a disability extension) | Individual policy | Individual policy |
| No coverage if | | Ended for nonpayment, or replaced by similar group coverage within **31 days** | |

### Medicare supplement vs long-term care

| | **Medicare supplement** | **Long-term care** |
|---|---|---|
| Free look | **30 days** | **30 days** after delivery |
| Renewability | Guaranteed renewable | Guaranteed renewable or better |
| Preexisting | Can't limit for more than **6 months**. Can't exclude if continuously covered before applying. | Defined as advice or treatment in the **6 months** before coverage. Can't exclude a loss that begins after the first **6 months**. |
| Consumer guide | Buyer's Guide at application | NAIC Shopper's Guide **before** presenting an application |
| Outline of coverage | At time of application, with acknowledgment to insurer | At time of application. Must show a benefit comparison over at least **20 years** and premiums at ages **75 and 85**. |
| Replacement notice | "Notice Regarding Replacement of Medicare Supplement Coverage." Old insurer told within **5 working days**. | "Notice Regarding Replacement of Accident and Sickness or Long-Term Care Coverage," signed by the applicant. Old insurer told within **5 working days**. |
| Advertising | Notice to buyer on page one of outline and policy | Filed with OIR. May be used right away, subject to disapproval. |

### Health plan types

| Plan | Key features |
|---|---|
| **HMO** | Comprehensive care for members, usually enrolled through an employer, which prepays a fixed amount. **No coverage outside the network.** Primary care physician is the **gatekeeper**. Emphasis on preventive care. |
| **PPO** | In- or out-of-network care. Lower out-of-pocket cost in network. Providers are independent businesses. In-network providers can't charge more than the copay; out-of-network providers can bill the difference. |
| **EPO** | Has a written agreement with an insurer to provide care for specific (exclusive) insureds. |
| **PLHSO** | Prepaid limited health service organization. Limited services through an exclusive panel: ambulance, dental, vision, mental health, substance abuse, chiropractic, podiatric, pharmaceutical. |
| **Indemnity plan** | Hospital indemnity (fixed rate). Pays a set amount per day in the hospital (e.g., $100/day), directly to the insured. Minimal underwriting. Benefit periods from 6 months to lifetime. |
| **DMPO** | Discounts on medical services. Licensed by OIR but **not insurance**. Marketers need no agent license. |
| **Dread disease** | Critical illness coverage for specific illnesses (cancer, heart disease). Often a lump sum. |

## 4. Section 1: Common to all lines

### Department of Financial Services (DFS)

- **Divisions:** Accounting & Auditing; Consumer Services; Funeral, Cemetery & Consumer Services; Insurance Agent & Agency Services; Investigative & Forensic Services; Public Assistance Fraud; Rehabilitation & Liquidation; Risk Management; State Fire Marshal; Treasury; Unclaimed Property; Workers' Compensation; Administration. Plus the **Office of Insurance Consumer Advocate**.
- **Who investigates whom:** DFS investigates agents, adjusters, agencies, customer reps and service reps. OIR investigates administrators, service companies, people managing an insurer, promoters of a domestic insurer, and holding companies.
- **Division of Consumer Services:** educates and helps consumers. On a complaint it contacts the company or agent first. Violations go to OIR.
- **Division of Investigative & Forensic Services:** law enforcement. Insurance fraud, workers' comp fraud, arson, misuse of state funds. Investigators are state law enforcement officers: they can arrest, execute warrants and serve subpoenas.
- **Anti-fraud reward:** up to **$25,000** for information leading to **arrest and conviction**. One reward per occurrence, which may be split.
- **Receivership:** DFS is the receiver of any insurer in receivership. It tries **rehabilitation** first. If that fails, it petitions the **court** for liquidation.
- **Unclaimed property:** the CFO oversees the Bureau, which holds more than **$2 billion**.
- **Cease-and-desist:** the Department or OIR serves a complaint, and the person gets a hearing. An **emergency** order can be issued without a hearing (e.g., insolvency). Violating an order after notice and hearing: up to **$50,000**, plus suspension or revocation.

### CFO discipline (license suspension or revocation)

Grounds include:

- false application information
- obtaining a license by fraud
- misrepresenting policy terms
- a felony conviction
- unfair trade practices
- dishonesty or incompetence
- license action in another state
- forging a name
- cheating on the exam
- knowingly taking business from unlicensed people
- failing to pay court-ordered child support
- getting a license mainly for controlled business

Instead of suspending or revoking, the CFO may fine or put the licensee on probation.

| Violation | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Not answering a CFO subpoena or order | **$1,000** fine |
| Violating a cease-and-desist order | Up to **$50,000** |
| Willfully violating the code | Misdemeanor |
| Willfully submitting fraudulent signatures | **3rd-degree felony** |
| No specific penalty listed | **$5,000** first offense, **$10,000** each after |
| 48 months with no appointment | License **terminates** |

### Licensing

- You can't act as or advertise as an agent unless you are **licensed by DFS and appointed**. You can only sell the kinds of insurance you're licensed for.
- **Requirements:**
  - natural person, at least **18**
  - U.S. citizen or legal alien with work authorization, and a bona fide Florida resident
  - not an employee of the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs or a state service office
  - not a funeral director or direct disposer, or their employee
  - pass the exam
  - qualified by knowledge, experience or instruction
- **Pre-licensing education:** health = **40-hour** health course, or a **60-hour** multi-line course that includes health. Life is the same with life. General lines = 200 hours. Personal lines = 60 hours.
- **Veterans:** no pre-licensing coursework is required for Armed Forces members or veterans, or their spouses (Review Notes).
- **Application:** filed with DFS under oath, with fees and **fingerprints**. Fingerprints go to the Florida Department of Law Enforcement **and** the FBI.
- **Exam:** separate fee for each class. You can't take it until the application is approved and fees are paid. No more than **5 tries in 12 months** at the same exam.
- **Adjusters:** a public adjuster works for the insured, for pay. An all-lines adjuster works for a public adjuster or the insurer.

### Maintaining the license

- **CE every 2 years.** It includes a **4-hour law and ethics update** specific to the license held.

| Licensee | Electives (body) | Total incl. 4-hr update (Review Notes) |
|---|---|---|
| Licensed less than 6 years | 20 | **24** |
| Licensed 6+ years | 16 | **20** |
| Customer rep | 6 | **10** |
| 25+ years **and** CLU, CPCU or a B.S. in risk management or insurance | 6 | **10** |

- Extra hours carry forward. An extension for good cause can be at most **1 year**. Active military can request a waiver in writing.
- **Nonresidents:** exempt from Florida CE if they meet their home state's CE.
- **Change of name or address:** tell DFS in writing within **30 days**. The fine is up to **$250** for a first offense, then at least **$500** or suspension. If both residence and business move out of Florida, licenses terminate immediately.
- **Report** an administrative action within **30 days** of the final disposition, and a criminal action within **30 days** of the final ruling.

### Appointments

- The insurer pays all appointment fees and taxes and can't make the appointee pay.
- Initial appointments are due no later than **45 days** after the appointment date. Late fee is **$250** per appointee.
- **Fee:** **$60** for a life, health, property or casualty agent or customer rep (**$42** fee + **$12** state tax + **$6** county tax). Surplus lines is **$150**. Late renewal adds **$20**.
- **Denied appointment:** refund of the state and county **taxes** only.
- **Renewal:** in the appointee's **birth month**, then every **24 months**.
- No limit on the number of appointments, but you need one per insurer. A single appointment covers life **and** health with the same insurer.
- **Insurer terminating an agent:** at least **60 days'** advance notice, except for cause. The insurer files notice with the Department within **30 days**. The agent can terminate at any time.
- **48 months** with no appointment means requalifying as a first-time applicant (course and exam).
- An insurer can't make an appointment depend on attending a CE-approved course.

### Agent responsibilities

- Premiums are **trust funds**. Keep premium payment records at least **3 years after payment**.
- Keep a separate account for funds of insurers the agent isn't appointed with (other than surplus lines).
- Commissions can only be shared with someone licensed in the same lines. An unlicensed person may receive residual commissions if they were licensed at the time of sale.
- Extra service charges are allowed only if written into a contract signed by both client and agent.
- Answer a Department or OIR written request about a consumer complaint within **20 days**. Fines for not answering: an entity up to **$2,500** per violation; an individual **$250**, then **$500**, then up to **$1,000**.

| Amount diverted or misappropriated | Crime |
|---|---|
| **$300 or less** | 1st-degree misdemeanor |
| Over $300, under **$20,000** | 3rd-degree felony |
| $20,000 to under **$100,000** | 2nd-degree felony |
| **$100,000+** | 1st-degree felony |

### Ethics and the Policyholders Bill of Rights

- DFS adopts the code of ethics, which was developed with **NAIFA-Florida** and adopted **July 2012**. **All applicants** must subscribe to it.
- **Policyholders Bill of Rights:**
  - competitive pricing
  - comprehensive coverage
  - accurate, balanced advertising
  - a financially stable insurer
  - a competent, honest agent
  - a readable policy
  - economic delivery and loss prevention
  - balanced regulation

### Marketing guidelines

- An agent's ads about an insurer need the insurer's **written approval in advance**. Ads must clearly be about insurance. Agents must say they are insurance agents and name the insurers.
- Offering **free insurance** as an inducement to buy property or services is prohibited.
- **Designations:**
  - No designation that falsely implies special training or certification to advise seniors.
  - No "financial advisor" title that falsely implies a license for non-insurance products.
  - A real CFP, CLU, ChFC, LUTC or FINRA license may be mentioned.

### FIGA (Florida Insurance Guaranty Association, a property and casualty fund)

- Pays covered claims of insolvent insurers. Membership is required for authorization.
- Assessments are capped at **2%** of net direct premium, plus another **2%** (total **4%**) in emergencies.
- Up to **$300,000** per claim. Homeowners can get up to **$200,000** more for structure and contents. **$100** deductible.

### Marketing practices and unfair trade practices

| Penalty (Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act) | Non-willful | Willful |
|---|---|---|
| General, per violation | up to **$5,000** | up to **$40,000** |
| Insurer aggregate, same action | **$20,000** | **$200,000** |
| Twisting / churning / fraudulent signatures, per violation | up to **$5,000** | up to **$75,000** |
| Aggregate for those, same action | **$50,000** | **$250,000** |

- **Unfair claims practices** (done knowingly or as a general business practice) include:
  - settling on an altered application
  - not affirming or denying coverage, or not giving a written statement that the claim is being investigated, within **30 days** after proof of loss when the insured asks in writing
  - not paying when liability is clear
  - not paying undisputed first-party residential property benefits within **90 days**
- **Unfair discrimination:**
  - Can't treat people of the same class and hazard differently.
  - Can't refuse, cancel or raise rates because someone sought help for abuse. A health insurer may consider a medical condition but **not** whether abuse caused it.
  - Can't discriminate over lawful firearm ownership.
- **Complaint records:** keep a complete record of all complaints since the last exam. A complaint is a written communication mainly expressing a grievance.
- **Misrepresentation on applications:** includes not telling the buyer about a preexisting condition clause in a replacement policy.

## 5. Section 2: Florida Life and Health Insurance Guaranty Association [631.711]

- A nonprofit that protects policyowners, insureds, beneficiaries and annuitants if a member insurer is **impaired or insolvent**. It's funded by **assessments on insurers**, and all L&H insurers must be members.
- **Agents may not use the Association's existence to sell.**
- **Covers** Florida residents. It also covers residents of other states if the insurer is domiciled in Florida, the insurer isn't licensed in their state, and their own state's association doesn't cover them.
- **Not covered:**
  - unguaranteed variable portions
  - fraternal benefit societies
  - HMOs, dental, pharmaceutical, optometric, ambulance and pre-need funeral plans
  - prepaid health clinics
  - non-individual annuities
  - Medicare Part C or D
  - amounts over the limits

| Per-life maximum | Limit |
|---|---|
| Life insurance net cash surrender/withdrawal values | **$100,000** |
| Deferred annuity net cash surrender/withdrawal values | **$250,000** |
| All other benefits for one life (incl. **LTC** and cash values) | **$300,000** |
| Basic hospital, medical-surgical, major medical health (**not LTC**) | **$500,000** |

- **Assessments:** at most **1%** a year of the average Florida premiums over the past 3 years. LTC assessments are split **50/50** between health insurers and life and annuity insurers.
- The Department is appointed **liquidator or rehabilitator** of a **domestic** insurer, and **conservator** of a foreign or alien one.

## 6. Section 3: Health

### Standard and mandated provisions

- **Grandfathered plans [627.402]:**
  - Enrolled on **March 23, 2010**. Sold through agents and brokers, not the Marketplace.
  - **Lose status by:** eliminating substantially all benefits for a condition; raising cost-sharing beyond federal limits; lowering the sponsor's contribution.
  - **Still must:** cover adult children to **26**; have no waiting period over **90 days**; have no lifetime or annual limits on essential benefits (a grandfathered *individual* policy may have annual limits); include a statement that the plan believes it is grandfathered under **Section 1251 of PPACA**.
  - Grandfathered group plans don't have to provide free preventive care or essential health benefits.
- **Non-grandfathered:** effective after March 23, 2010, or lost its status. Subject to all PPACA rules.
- **Preexisting:** insurers offering comprehensive individual coverage, and HMOs, must offer **at least one** comprehensive policy with no preexisting exclusion.
- **Optometrists, chiropractors, podiatrists:** must be paid for procedures within their license.
- **Diabetes:** cover medically necessary equipment, supplies and services, including outpatient self-management training, when a physician certifies them.
- **Osteoporosis:** cover diagnosis and treatment for **high-risk** people. Exempt: specified accident, specified disease, hospital indemnity, Medicare supplement, LTC.
- **Newborns:**
  - Covered from the **moment of birth**.
  - A newborn of a covered family member (a grandchild) is covered for **18 months**.
  - The policy may require notice of the birth within at least **30 days**. Late notice can mean premium is charged from birth, but coverage can't be denied.
- **Adopted and foster children:** covered from **placement**. No preexisting exclusion, except for a foster child.
- **Child health supervision:** birth to age **16** (Florida-specific). Covers exams, developmental assessment, immunizations and lab tests.
- **Disabled children:** coverage continues while the child is incapable of self-sustaining employment because of an intellectual or physical disability **and** chiefly dependent on the employee or member.
- **Mastectomy:**
  - Covers prosthetics, reconstruction and symmetry surgery. The hospital stay is set by the physician.
  - Can't deny coverage for breast cancer once the person has been cancer-free **2 years**.
  - **Mammograms:** a baseline at **35–39**; every **2 years** at **40–49**; **yearly** at **50+**; more often if at risk.
- **Fibrocystic condition:** can't be the sole reason to deny or exclude unless a **breast biopsy** shows increased risk.
- **Cleft lip and palate:** child **under 18**. Medical, dental, speech, audiology and nutrition care.
- **Wellness rebate** (individual policies): up to **10%** of premiums paid.
- **Emergency services:** no prior authorization, in or out of network. Same cost-sharing either way.
- **Autism and Down syndrome:** speech, occupational and physical therapy, and applied behavior analysis, subject to dollar limits.
- **Opioids:** prior authorization for abuse-deterrent opioids only if non-abuse-deterrent ones need the same. Can't require trying the non-abuse-deterrent drug first.
- **Step therapy:** can't be required if another plan already approved the drug and paid for it during the **90 days** before the request.
- **Balance billing:**
  - Prohibited for nonemergency services at an in-network facility.
  - Prohibited for emergency services at **any** facility.
  - The insured pays only copays, coinsurance and deductibles.
- **Prohibited provisions:** the Office of Civil Rights enforces **ACA Section 1557**, which bans discrimination by race, color, national origin, age, disability or sex.

### Group health: eligible groups

| Group | Key rules |
|---|---|
| **Employer** | The employer is the policyowner. Can include directors, former and retired employees, a proprietor or partner, public officials, and employees of entities under common control. Classes can't be used to exclude hazardous jobs. **Full-time = 25+ hours a week** average. |
| **Trustee** | Policy issued to the **trustees** of a fund. At least **5** people per employer unit. |
| **Association / labor union** | At least **25** members. Has existed **1 year** for reasons other than insurance. At least **15** must enroll. |
| **Debtor (creditor)** | Policy issued to the **creditor**. Credit disability needs at least **100** new entrants a year, and the insurer may require evidence of insurability if under **75%** enroll. Mortgage insurance covers loans **over 10 years** secured by a first mortgage. |
| **Blanket** | Special groups: common carrier passengers; school students and teachers; volunteer fire departments; Boy Scouts, FFA and camps; newspaper delivery people; health care providers covering patients (may be offered but not mandatory); HMO subscribers; sports teams; travel agencies. **No individual application.** **No certificate**, except for schools and colleges. |

- **Certificate of coverage [627.657]:** the insurer gives each group member a certificate showing the group number, essential features and who gets benefits. Only **one per family unit**.

### Mini-COBRA continuation [627.6692]

- **Qualifying events:** the employee's death; termination or reduced hours; divorce or legal separation; a child losing dependent status.
- **Steps:**
  1. Written notice to the insurer within **63 days**.
  2. The insurer sends the election form by **certified mail** within **14 days**.
  3. Elect and pay within **30 days**. Coverage is retroactive.
- **Billing:** monthly, due on the 1st, with a **30-day** grace period.
- **Ends at the earliest of:**
  - **18 months**
  - nonpayment
  - other group coverage without a preexisting exclusion
  - entitlement to Medicare A or B
  - the employer ending the plan for everyone
- **Disability extension:** **11** more months (**29** total) at up to **150%**. The Social Security determination must reach the insurer within **60 days** of the determination and before the 18 months end.

### Coordination of benefits [627.4235]

- **Mr. Senior** (67, on Medicare, covered by his own employer plan and his wife's): his **employee plan** pays first, **spouse's plan** second, **Medicare** last.
- **Child of married parents:** **birthday rule**. The parent whose birthday comes earlier in the year pays first. If the birthdays are the same, the plan that has covered a parent longer pays first.
- **Divorced or separated parents:** **custodial parent**, then the custodial parent's spouse, then the noncustodial parent.
- **No COB** against indemnity, excess, specified illness or accident, or Medicare supplement policies.

### Disclosure, renewal and advertising

- **Outline of coverage [627.642]:** completed and given **at the time of application**, with an acknowledgment of receipt sent to the insurer. It covers:
  - the category of coverage
  - principal benefits
  - exclusions and limitations
  - renewal terms, including any right to change premiums
  - a statement that it's only a summary
  - home health care, if provided
- **Renewal:** health coverage must be renewed **at the policyholder's option**, except for these reasons.

| Individual: may nonrenew only for | Group: may nonrenew for |
|---|---|
| Nonpayment | Nonpayment |
| Fraud or intentional misrepresentation | Fraud or intentional misrepresentation |
| Insurer leaves the individual market | Breaking the employer contribution or participation rules |
| Insured leaves the service area | Insurer stops offering that coverage type |
| Insured leaves the bona fide association | No enrollee in the service area |
| Policy form discontinued with **90 days'** notice, plus an offer of other coverage | Employer leaves the bona fide association |
| | Product discontinued with **90 days'** notice, plus an offer of other coverage |
| | Leaving the whole small- or large-group market: **180 days'** notice |

- An insurer that drops **all** health coverage in Florida can't write health insurance there for **5 years**.
- **Advertising [626.9531]:**
  - No exaggerating with "all," "full," "complete," "comprehensive," "unlimited," "up to" or "as high as."
  - Limitations can't be worded as benefits (e.g., calling a waiting period a "benefit builder").
  - Hospital-confinement policies can't be advertised as "tax-free," "extra cash," "extra income" or "extra pay."
  - Limited policies must say so in prominent type: "THIS IS A LIMITED POLICY," "CANCER ONLY," "AUTOMOBILE ACCIDENT ONLY," "NURSING HOME COVERAGE ONLY."
  - Waiting, elimination and probationary periods must be disclosed. Preexisting rules must be disclosed in negative terms, and "preexisting condition" must be defined.
  - Association invitations need a separate application and signature, with membership fees shown separately.
- **Marketing methods:**
  - Every ad needs **insurer approval**.
  - **Ads include:** publications; notices, circulars, letters and posters; radio and TV.
  - **Not ads:** training material, internal communications, and negotiations with a prospective group.
  - The insurer keeps an advertising file for **4 years** or until the next exam, **whichever is longer**.
  - No disparaging competitors, fake testimonials, misleading trade names, government lookalikes, or untrue statements about the insurer's finances.
- **Application responsibilities:**
  - The agent asks every question and records the answers honestly.
  - If the applicant fills in the application, the agent warns that misstatements can mean lost coverage or denied claims.
  - The agent gets all signatures and gives all disclosures.
  - The agent **never signs** for the applicant.
  - The agent forwards the premium and application promptly.

### Medicare supplement

- **Preexisting:** can't be limited for more than **6 months**. Can't be excluded if the person was continuously covered before applying.
- **Free look:** **30 days**.
- **Open enrollment:**
  - The annual period is **Oct 15 – Dec 7**, with changes effective **Jan 1**.
  - **65+:** no denial or health-based pricing if the application comes before or within **6 months** of the first month in **Part B**.
  - **Under 65** on Medicare for disability or ESRD (end-stage renal disease): must be offered enrollment (see the note in Section 10). The same applies within **60 days** after group coverage ends.
- **Advertising:** page one of the outline and policy must say: *"Notice to buyer: This policy may not cover all of the costs associated with medical care incurred by the buyer during the coverage period. The buyer is advised to review carefully all policy limitations."* Selling more than one Medicare supplement policy is prohibited.
- **Medicare Advantage (Part C) enrollees can't buy Medicare supplement.**
- **Prohibited marketing [627.6743]:**
  - **Twisting**
  - **High-pressure tactics** (force, fright, intimidation, threat)
  - **Cold lead advertising** (not saying it's an insurance solicitation and that someone will contact you)
- **Multiple policies:** can't sell if it would give the person more than one Medicare supplement, or if they're entitled to **Medicaid** (see the exceptions in Section 10). Allowed if the new policy replaces the old one and the replacing insurer sends a statement to the old insurer.
- **Buyer's Guide** and **outline of coverage:** given at the time of application, with an acknowledgment to the insurer. Direct response insurers deliver the guide on request, no later than policy delivery.
- **Replacement:**
  - The application asks directly whether the person intends to replace.
  - The applicant gets a "Notice Regarding Replacement of Medicare Supplement Coverage" **before** the policy is issued.
  - The old insurer gets a copy within **5 working days**.
  - If the old policy was in force **6+ months**, the new one can't restart preexisting, waiting, elimination or probationary periods.
- **No duplicating Medicare benefits.**
- **Standardized plans A–N** (effective on or after Jan 1, 2020):
  - Guaranteed renewable.
  - Sickness and accident treated the same.
  - Benefits change automatically with Medicare's deductibles and copays.
  - A spouse can't lose coverage just because the insured's coverage ends, unless premium isn't paid.
  - Pre-2006 drug benefits may be renewed for people without Part D.
- **Excluded** (not covered by Medicare): dental, vision, hearing, long-term care, routine foot care, cosmetic or elective procedures.
- **Medicaid:** a state/federal program for people with limited income. A state that doesn't participate must offer a comparable program.
- **Medicare Advantage:**
  - Types: HMO, PPO, PFFS, SNP, HMOPOS, MSA.
  - Covers all Original Medicare services **except hospice**.
  - Emergency coverage outside the service area, but **not outside the U.S.**
  - Most include Part D.

### Long-term care

- **Purpose:** health insurance and Medicare cover **acute** care. LTC pays for help with daily activities, in a nursing home, assisted living, adult day care or **at home**.
- **Outline of coverage [627.9407]:** given at the time of application. It must include:
  - a graphic comparison of benefit-increasing vs level benefits over at least **20 years**
  - age-based premium increases at **75 and 85**
- **Shopper's Guide** (NAIC): delivered **before** any application is presented. Doesn't apply to life policies with accelerated LTC riders, which get a life insurance Policy Summary instead.
- **Advertising:** filed with **OIR**. It can be used right away, subject to disapproval. The policy and outline must prominently say whether it's a **qualified** LTC policy.
- **Free look:** **30 days** after delivery.
- **Renewability:** guaranteed renewable or better. "Level premium" may be used only if the insurer can't change the premium. Rates are based on **issue age**, not attained age.
- **Preexisting:** advice or treatment in the **6 months** before coverage. Can't exclude a loss that begins after the first **6 months**. Can't use waivers or riders to exclude a specific preexisting condition.
- **Allowed exclusions:**
  - preexisting conditions
  - mental or nervous disorders (but **not Alzheimer's**)
  - alcoholism and drug addiction
  - war
  - felony, riot or insurrection
  - suicide or self-inflicted injury
  - aviation (other than as a fare-paying passenger)
  - care in a government facility
  - services Medicare covers
  - workers' comp or no-fault
  - care by an immediate family member
  - services normally provided free
- **May NOT:**
  - cancel or nonrenew for age or health
  - impose new waiting periods on a conversion or internal replacement
  - limit coverage to skilled nursing
  - have an **elimination period over 180 days**
  - **require prior hospitalization**
- **Home care:** can't require a prior acute condition or prior skilled care. Can't limit to Medicare-certified providers. Can't exclude home health aides or adult day care.
- **Inflation protection:** must be **offered**. At least **5% compounded** annually, either as an automatic increase or a guaranteed insurability option.
- **Nonforfeiture:**
  - Must be **offered**: reduced paid-up, extended term or shortened benefit period.
  - Eligible after the **3rd policy year**.
  - Pays the **greater of 100% of premiums paid or 30 times** the daily nursing home benefit.
- **Contingent benefit on lapse:** kicks in when a premium increase reaches the threshold in Florida law and the policy lapses within **120 days** of the increased premium's due date. It applies when the insured rejected nonforfeiture, or in the first **3 years**.
- **Grace period:**
  - At least **30 days**. Interest on overdue premium is capped at **8%** a year.
  - A **secondary addressee** is notified **30 days** after the premium is due, which gives about **30 extra days**.
  - **Reinstatement** is available for **5 months** if the lapse was caused by cognitive impairment, loss of functional capacity, or confinement over **60 days**.
- **Minimum standards:**
  - At least **24 months** of nursing home coverage.
  - At least one lower level of care at **50%** or more of the nursing home benefit (e.g., $100 a day nursing home means $50 a day lower level).
  - **Lower levels of care:** nursing services; assisted living; home health agencies; adult day care centers; adult foster homes; community care for the elderly; personal care; social services; others approved by OIR.
- **Benefit triggers:** set out in a separate paragraph titled "Eligibility for the Payment of Benefits." The triggers are loss of **ADLs** and **cognitive impairment**. A trigger can't be stricter than needing hands-on help, needing help performing the ADL, or needing supervision or verbal cueing. Licensed professionals assess, and the policy must describe the appeal process.
- **Extension of benefits:** if the policy ends during an institutional stay that began while it was in force, coverage continues.
- **Riders:** a rider that reduces benefits needs the insured's signed acceptance. A benefit increase with a premium increase needs written, signed agreement.
- **Group LTC:**
  - The sponsor doesn't have to contribute. If it does, all members must be eligible.
  - Must offer **continuation or conversion**. Conversion requires **6 months** of coverage first.
- **Care definitions:**

| Type of care | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Skilled** | Licensed medical staff under doctor's orders. A nursing home provides 24-hour nursing. |
| **Intermediate** | Between skilled and home care. |
| **Custodial** | Help with ADLs. |
| **Home health care** | Skilled professionals at home. |
| **Home care** | Nonskilled caregivers at home. |
| **CCRC** | Continuing care retirement community: independent living, assisted living and skilled care in one place. |
| **Alternate care** | Small residential settings, or adult day care that gives family caregivers a break. |
| **Adult day care** | A program for **6 or more** people. |
| **Hospice** | Comfort care at end of life. |

- **Case management goals:** assessment, education, advocacy, planning, communication.
- **Replacement [69O-157.016]:**
  - The agent lists **all** health policies sold to the applicant, not just LTC.
  - The applicant signs the notice before the new policy is issued or delivered.
  - The old insurer is notified within **5 working days**.
- **LTC Partnership:**
  - Medicaid and private insurers working together.
  - Gives **dollar-for-dollar** asset protection from the Medicaid spend-down.
  - The policy must be qualified, issued to a Florida resident (or a resident of a state with a reciprocal agreement), and include inflation coverage based on attained age.
  - The disclosure notice goes on the insurer's letterhead.
- **Medicaid** is the **largest provider** of long-term care. A spend-down is required first.

### Small employers [627.6699]: Florida Employee Health Care Access Act

- **Purpose:** make coverage available to small employers regardless of claims or health status.
- **Small employer:** principal place of business in Florida, **1 to 50** eligible employees, most of them in Florida.
- **Eligible employee:** full-time, normal workweek of **25+ hours**. Includes a sole proprietor, partner or independent contractor if covered. Excludes part-time, temporary and substitute workers.
- **Late enrollees** (employer with fewer than 2 employees): may be excluded up to **24 months** if not continuously covered more than **63 days** before.
- **Enrollment:**
  - Initial enrollment of at least **30 days**.
  - Annual **30-day** open enrollment.
  - **Special enrollment** within **30 days** after other coverage ends because of: COBRA ending; divorce, separation, death, termination or reduced hours; the employer ending its contributions.
- **Insurers can't:** limit coverage to certain people (except late enrollees); exclude specific diseases; raise participation requirements after acceptance.
- **May refuse to offer coverage if:** the employer isn't in the service area (which can't be smaller than a county); an employee doesn't live or work there; or the network can't serve the group.
- **May deny** if it shows the **Department** it lacks reserves, applied uniformly. It then can't offer small-group coverage for **180 days**.
- **Fair marketing:**
  - All plans are **guaranteed issue**.
  - Can't steer employers away because of health, claims, industry, occupation or location, or base agent pay on those.
  - Can't encourage firing an employee to get coverage.
  - A denial must be in writing with reasons.
- **Forms** need Department approval.
- **Employers with 50 or more employees must offer coverage.** Under 50 don't have to.
- **Rating:** modified community rating. The rate can't change for **12 months** after issue or renewal unless the group or the benefits change.
- **Riders** for extra benefits may be medically underwritten.
- **Declining coverage:** an employee may decline, but then waits for the next open enrollment or a qualifying event.

### State programs and HIV/AIDS

- **Florida Healthy Kids Corporation [624.91]:**
  - Created **1990**. A public-private venture, and participation is **voluntary**.
  - Pools local, state, federal and family money to pay premiums to commercial insurers, and families pay part.
  - Covers preventive care through major surgery.
  - **Main recipients:** school-age children in families under 200% of the federal poverty level (see Section 10) who don't qualify for Medicaid.
- **HIV/AIDS [627.429]:**
  - Testing requires **written informed consent** first.
  - A positive result is reported by a physician the applicant chooses or by the **Department of Health**.
  - Insurers may ask about positive tests or a diagnosis.
  - **Sexual orientation** can't be used in underwriting. Marital status, living arrangements, occupation, gender, beneficiary or ZIP code can't be used to infer it.
  - Results stay **confidential**. They can't go to an industry data bank in identifiable form.
  - No HIV exclusion except through a preexisting condition clause.
  - Life benefits can't be denied because death was from HIV.
  - A health policy can't be canceled or nonrenewed for an HIV/AIDS diagnosis.

## 8. Memory tricks (made up by Claude, not from the course)

1. **"OIR = Insurers, DFS = Do-ers (agents)."** OIR regulates companies and issues COAs. DFS licenses the people.
2. **"Twisting turns you Toward another company; Churning stays in the same Church."**
3. **"Mini-COBRA math: 63-14-30."** You have 63 days to tell the insurer, the insurer has 14 days to mail the form, and you have 30 days to pay. Then **18 months**, plus **11** for disability, makes **29**.
4. **"115, 150, 200: the further from the group, the higher the price."** Continuation 115%, disability extension 150%, conversion 200%.
5. **"Convert after 3 for health, 6 for LTC."** LTC is the bigger commitment, so it waits twice as long.
6. **"LTC's 5s and 30s."** 5% inflation, 5 months to reinstate, 5 working days for replacement notice. 30-day free look, 30-day grace, 30 times the daily benefit for nonforfeiture.
7. **"No more than 180, no prior hospital."** LTC elimination period is at most 180 days, and the policy can't require a prior hospital stay.
8. **ADLs: "Don't Bother To Eat Cold Tacos"** for **D**ressing, **B**athing, **T**oileting, **E**ating, **C**ontinence, **T**ransferring. Six ADLs; chronically ill means you can't do **2** of them for **90 days**.
9. **"$100, $250, $300, $500: life, annuity, other, health."** The guaranty limits climb in that order.
10. **"Medigap: no Advantage, no Medicaid, no double-dipping."** You can't sell a Medicare supplement to someone in Medicare Advantage, someone on Medicaid (with exceptions), or someone who already has one.
11. **"Small employer: 1 to 50. Mini-COBRA: under 20."** Two different laws, two different sizes.
12. **"Birthday rule = whose birthday comes first in the calendar, not who is older."**

## 10. Spots where the course contradicts itself

1. **Mini-COBRA length: 8 vs 18 months.** The list on the continuation page says coverage ends "Eight (8) months after the qualifying event." The same page then refers to "the end of the eighteen (18)-month continuation period," and the disability extension is 11 months for **29** total (18 + 11). **Answer 18 months.** "Eight" is a typo.
2. **Who issues the certificate of authority.** The Review Notes say DFS issues "certificates of authority to companies." Everywhere else (the definitions, the OIR pages, and the Review Notes' own definitions list) says **OIR** issues the COA. **Answer OIR.**
3. **Agent record retention: 5 vs 3 years.** The Maintaining a License page says keep records at least **5 years after policy expiration**. The Review Notes say **3 years after the expiration of the policy**. Separately, *premium payment* records are kept **3 years after payment**. If asked about general agent records, the body says **5 years**. Watch the wording.
4. **Defamation target.** The keywords say false statements about "a competing insurer's **financial condition**." The body and Review Notes say false statements calculated to injure **any person**. The broader definition is the statute's. A question about a competitor's finances fits both.
5. **Controlled business.** The keywords say insurance on "the licensee, employer, immediate family." The body says the agent's "own life, health, property, immediate family, or business associates." Both describe insuring yourself and the people close to you.
6. **Fraudulent signature fines.** The CFO page says "$5,000–$75,000 per violation." The marketing page says "up to $5,000 non-willful / up to $75,000 willful." The second is the clearer reading.
7. **Cease-and-desist.** The keywords say the "Commissioner" may send one. The DFS page says the **Department or OIR** serves the complaint and order.
8. **FIGA funding.** The FIGA page says it's funded by assessments on "member insureds." The keywords say all authorized **insurers** fund it. Insurers is correct.
9. **Wrong fund named.** The L&H Guaranty section's advertising rule names the "Florida Insurance **Guaranty Association**" (FIGA, the property and casualty fund). The L&H rule is about the **Florida Life and Health Insurance Guaranty Association**. Either way, agents can't use a guaranty fund to sell.
10. **Stock company dividends.** The keywords say stock companies are nonparticipating and don't pay dividends. The definitions page says stock companies that issue both par and nonpar policies are **mixed-plan** companies. "Generally nonparticipating" is the safe answer.
11. **Medicaid and Medicare supplement.** The Multiple Policies page says flatly that a Medicare supplement can't be sold to someone entitled to Medicaid. The Medicaid page adds exceptions: **unless Medicaid pays for the Medigap policy or only pays the Part B premium**.
12. **Modified community rating.** The definitions page says it allows adjustments for **claims experience, health status and duration**. The Rating Act page says the premium is based **solely** on age, gender, family composition, tobacco use and geographic area. For the small-employer rating question, use the "solely" list.
13. **Small employer size.** Mini-COBRA uses **fewer than 20**. The Access Act uses **1 to 50**. The Availability page says employers with **50 or more** must offer coverage, so an employer with exactly 50 fits both "small" and "must offer." Match the number to the law being asked about.
14. **Medicare supplement open enrollment.** The page gives **Oct 15 – Dec 7** (Medicare's annual plan-change period). It then describes the separate Medicare supplement protection of **6 months after enrolling in Part B**. For the under-65 disability or ESRD group, the page says the request must be made "six (6) months **before** the first month of coverage," which reads oddly. If asked, the 6-month window is the key number.
15. **Healthy Kids income.** The course says families with income "**200% below** the federal poverty level," which isn't mathematically possible. It most likely means **at or below 200% of** the poverty level.
16. **Medicare supplement exclusions.** The body says Medicare supplement policies **will not** cover anything Medicare doesn't. The Review Notes say "**most**" will not.
17. **Placeholder page.** Small Employers page 12 of 12 shows only "Header / Content." It's a blank course page, not missing material.
